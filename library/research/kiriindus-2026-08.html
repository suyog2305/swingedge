Read This First: H-Acid Is Not the Story Here

Kiri appears on the dye-intermediates beneficiary list, and the operating business genuinely is in that value chain. But treating this as an H-acid trade misses what actually determines the outcome by an order of magnitude.

ItemScale
Q1 FY27 consolidated operating EBITDA₹15.90 Cr
Q1 FY27 other income (treasury and interest)₹286 Cr
Market capitalisation₹3,215 Cr
Net DyStar proceeds available for reinvestment> ₹5,200 Cr

The treasury income in one quarter is eighteen times the entire operating EBITDA. Whatever happens to H-acid pricing will move the ₹15.90 crore line. What happens to ₹5,200 crore of cash will decide the share price.

This is a capital-allocation situation wearing a chemicals company’s clothing, and it should be analysed as one.

The Deployment Plan — And Why the Market Is Discounting It

Use of the ₹5,200 CrShareDestination
New growth projects in India> 90%Fertilisers and metals, with a particular focus on copper
Special dividend to shareholders< 10%Under consideration
To promotersNilExplicitly none; negative pledge on promoter shares to be removed

Read that first row again, because it is the entire bear case. A dyes and dye-intermediates company intends to put more than 90% of a ₹5,200 crore windfall into fertilisers and copper metals — two industries with no operational, technical or customer overlap with anything Kiri has ever done.

This is unrelated diversification at the largest scale in your library — larger relative to the company than Kirloskar’s ₹1,053 crore NBFC or Man Industries’ real-estate venture, both of which this library has flagged. ₹5,200 crore is 1.6 times the entire market capitalisation being redeployed into businesses the management has not previously run.

Copper in particular is capital-intensive, cyclical, and dominated globally by very large integrated players. Fertilisers in India is price-controlled and subsidy-dependent. Neither is a natural adjacency for a specialty chemicals operator.

These plans are stated as subject to formal board and shareholder approvals. What has actually been committed, and on what timetable, is not established in the sources reviewed — which is itself the most important open question on this name.

Roughly ₹286 crore of the ₹290 crore profit is other income. Strip it out and the operating business earned a thin positive result after a period of losses. The 4.0× trailing P/E in your scan is therefore a multiple on treasury income, not on operations, and it will not repeat once the cash is deployed into assets rather than deposits.

06 —

The Core Business

Kiri manufactures dyes, dye intermediates and basic chemicals — genuinely in the H-acid value chain, and genuinely levered to the squeeze described in the circulating note.

The problem is scale relative to everything else. At roughly ₹312 crore of quarterly revenue and ₹15.90 crore of EBITDA, even a doubling of operating profitability adds about ₹64 crore annualised to a company sitting on ₹5,200 crore of cash. The chemicals business would have to improve beyond recognition to matter as much as a single capital-allocation decision.

The historical context is also relevant: this business was loss-making as recently as last quarter. It has been through the same Chinese-dumping compression that hit Bodal and Balaji Amines, and it is recovering from a lower base than either.

Every other name in the cluster participated in the H-acid rally. Kiri did not. It is also the only one failing the trend template, the only one outside Stage 2, and the only one 36.7% below its 52-week high.

There are two competing readings, and this report does not claim to know which is right:

  • The market is looking past H-acid to the capital allocation. On this reading, no amount of dye-intermediate margin matters while ₹5,200 crore is pointed at copper and fertilisers, and the 0.45× book multiple is the discount the market applies to that plan.
  • The market has not connected Kiri to the theme. On this reading, a company whose standalone revenue just grew 63% on better realisations, trading at 0.45× book, is the un-priced way to own the squeeze.

The first reading is better supported. A 0.45× book multiple on a debt-free company holding cash worth more than its market capitalisation is not an oversight — it is a deliberate discount, and the deployment plan is the obvious reason for it.

You can buy the entire company for roughly 62 paise on every rupee of cash it holds, and receive the operating chemicals business for nothing. The 0.45× book multiple corroborates this independently — implied book value of about ₹7,140 crore is consistent with a balance sheet that absorbed a ₹6,200 crore receipt.

Two caveats on that arithmetic. The ₹5,200 Cr is the stated net figure after tax and expenses, and the timing of tax outflows is not established here. And cash that management intends to spend on copper smelting is not the same asset as cash that will be distributed — a discount to cash is rational when the holder controls its use and has announced an unrelated use for it.

A negative enterprise value is a rare thing and it deserves to be named plainly. On the stated figures, the market is valuing Kiri’s entire operating business — plants, dyes franchise, intermediates capacity, an H-acid position in a global shortage — at less than zero.

Markets do that for reasons. Here the reason is legible and stated: more than 90% of the cash is earmarked for copper and fertilisers. If you believe that deployment destroys value, a 0.45× book multiple is not cheap — it is the market pricing in the destruction ahead of time. If you believe the plan will be moderated, or that shareholder pressure redirects a larger share to distribution, then this is the most mispriced asset in your universe.

That is the entire investment question, and it will be settled by board resolutions, not by H-acid prices.

Scenarios

Bull
The deployment plan is moderated — a materially larger special dividend, a buyback, or a phased and disciplined entry rather than 90% into copper. Meanwhile H-acid realisations carry the core chemicals business, already growing standalone revenue 63%, from marginal profitability to real earnings. The 0.45× book discount closes as the capital-allocation uncertainty resolves, and a negative enterprise value corrects.
Base
The company proceeds broadly as announced into fertilisers and copper. The market continues applying a conglomerate and execution discount, so the book multiple stays low for years while the projects are built. Treasury income falls away as cash converts to capex, removing the flattering P/E. The chemicals business improves on realisations but is too small to move the valuation.
Bear
₹5,200 Cr — 1.6× the market capitalisation — is spent building copper and fertiliser capacity by a management team with no track record in either, into cyclical industries dominated by far larger players. Capital is consumed, returns disappoint, and the discount to book widens rather than closes. The stock has already shown it will not rally on the sector theme.

13 —

Summary

RATING: HOLD — THE CASH IS REAL, THE PLAN IS THE RISK

Kiri Industries is the most unusual situation in this library. It received US$689 million — roughly ₹6,200 crore — in January 2026 on the resolution of a ten-year Singapore court battle over DyStar. It is debt-free, holds more than ₹5,200 crore net, and has a market capitalisation of ₹3,215 crore. On the stated figures its enterprise value is negative and it trades at 0.45× book — the lowest multiple in the entire library.

It appears on the H-acid beneficiary list, and the operating business is genuinely in that chain — standalone revenue grew 63% on improved realisations, turning a loss-making operation profitable. But that business generated ₹15.90 crore of EBITDA against ₹286 crore of treasury income. H-acid will not decide this.

What will decide it is that more than 90% of the cash is earmarked for fertilisers and copper — unrelated diversification at 1.6× the market capitalisation, by a management team with no history in either. That is the legible reason a company can hold more cash than its market value and still trade at 0.45× book, and it is why Kiri rose 0.7% in a week when the rest of this cluster rose 7% to 26%.

The discount is not an oversight; it is a verdict on the plan. The event that would change it is a board resolution, not a chemical price.
