On the rounded ₹ Cr values shown the arithmetic gives +3.6% and +17.0%. The benign reading was the right one. The chemicals business grew value 17.1% while the price-controlled fertiliser half grew 4.0%, and the two blend to 10.0%. Shree Pushkar was not failing to participate in the dye-intermediates market. It was participating with roughly half of itself.

Every figure in this table is derived — disclosed sales value divided by disclosed sales volume, using the ₹ Cr figures as printed. It is a blended basket realisation, not a product price, and the company does not publish product-level pricing.

A 90% jump in chemicals realisation looks like the H-acid thesis landing a quarter early. It is not, and the reason is in the transcript. Asked how the business was handling high sulphur and sulphuric acid prices, the Chairman and Managing Director explained on 13 August that the chemicals tonnage fell because “we have kept our acid plants on low load” — a deliberate choice, taken because sulphur requires advance payment while acid sells on credit, and the spread did not justify the working capital.

This is the single most important qualification in the report. Sulphuric acid is the cheapest tonnage in the chemicals basket. Taking it off load removes low-priced volume from the denominator, and the blended realisation rises whether or not any product got dearer. A large part of the derived +90.4% is mix, not price. It cannot be read as H-acid price capture, and any Q2 report showing chemicals value up on continued low acid volumes will have the same problem.

What it does settle is the August capacity hypothesis, and it settles it against the bullish reading. Chemicals nameplate is 1,27,460 MTPA. Q1 FY27 chemicals sales volume annualises to roughly 36,450 MT, about 29% of nameplate (derived), against roughly 59,350 MT and about 47% a year earlier. Sales volume is not production and a tonnage nameplate spanning dye intermediates, dyes, feed supplements and acid is a crude denominator — but the direction is unambiguous. Capacity was not the constraint. The company chose not to run it. The August report’s hope that completing the expansion would convert a price-only benefit into a price-and-volume benefit does not survive that.

Management’s own framing of the trade-off, on the same call, was blunt: “First, you need value, then volume.” It is a defensible way to run a chemicals book through a raw-material spike. It is also an explicit statement that volume was sacrificed on purpose.

Longer-standing company descriptions and third-party company profiles additionally name gamma acid and sulpho para base among the dye intermediates. The presentation’s own list does not carry them, and this report does not assume they are still in production.

The differentiator is K-acid, not H-acid. On the 13 August call the Chairman and Managing Director stated that the company is the largest manufacturer of K-acid in India and that no one else is in K-acid; third-party company profiles go further and describe it as the world’s largest K-acid manufacturer. Neither claim is independently verified in this report — they are recorded because the scope of the two claims differs and the difference matters.

Why it matters: the cluster trade is an H-acid trade, and H-acid is a crowded molecule with several large Indian producers. K-acid is not. If the Chinese supply squeeze is genuinely broad across dye intermediates rather than confined to H-acid, a producer with a near-monopoly domestic position in a different molecule captures more of it, for longer, with less competition.

The one price the company quoted itself — in rupees

On the same call, management said the price of the product had moved from ₹550 to ₹700-plus — roughly +27% (derived) — in the context of K-acid, and reported “plenty of inquiries for K-acid, H-acid, and Vinyl Sulphone to China”, with China placing orders back into India.

The transcript does not state the unit for the ₹550 → ₹700-plus figures. The context implies a per-kilogram price, but this report does not assume it, does not convert it, and does not build anything on it beyond the percentage change between the two numbers the company itself gave. It is recorded here because it is the only price in this entire report quoted in rupees by the company — everything in Section 06 is in yuan or dollars, and conflating the two is the error the August work exists to prevent.

Why the squeeze looks structural

The mechanism reported through 2026 is consistent: H-acid runs on sulfonation and nitration, among the most polluting processes in chemistry, with difficult waste-acid disposal under tightening environmental and safety standards. Non-compliant small and mid-sized Chinese capacity has been closing since March 2026, new compliant capacity takes time to permit and build, and feedstock costs — naphthalene derivatives, sulphuric acid, caustic, energy — have risen alongside. Permitting, not plant, is described as the binding constraint, which is why capacity does not return quickly.

The strongest single claim in the September reporting is that China, historically a large net exporter of H-acid, is expected to become a net importer in September 2026. Management’s remark on 13 August that China was placing orders back into India for K-acid, H-acid and vinyl sulphone is independent corroboration of the same flow, from the other end of it. Both remain forecasts and order enquiries. Neither is a customs statistic, and neither is a shipped tonne in a reported quarter.

The five project lines reconcile exactly to the ₹512 Cr / ₹209 Cr / ₹303 Cr totals. Nameplate moves from 5,90,200 to 10,40,200 MTPA on fertilisers and from 1,27,460 to 1,99,460 MTPA on chemicals, which is the same +4,50,000 and +72,000 stated another way. Funding is stated as internal accruals plus proceeds of a preferential issue. Separately, ₹9.33 Cr was spent in Q1 on ~30,000 sq m of adjacent land at Lote Parshuram; asked what would be built there, management said candidly that there is no plan yet and it was bought because it adjoined Unit 1 and came up at auction.

Roughly 85% of the planned capex (derived: ₹435 Cr of ₹512 Cr) sits in the two fertiliser-led units. Of the 72,000 MTPA of additional chemical capacity, 66,000 MTPA is acid complex and the balance is a 6,000 MTPA dyes plant that management described on the call as a small vertical costing ₹37 Cr. There is no new dye-intermediates capacity anywhere in the announced programme. If the H-acid squeeze runs for two years, the capital arriving to meet it is pointed almost entirely at complex fertilisers.

The fair counter-argument, stated properly. Unit 6’s acid complex feeds the chemicals division, and sulphuric acid is a direct input to dye intermediates. Cheaper captive acid plus 10 MW of additional solar improves dye-intermediate economics without adding a tonne of dye-intermediate capacity — and it answers precisely the problem that cut Q1 volumes, which was acid economics, not dye demand. That is a real and coherent piece of capital allocation for an integrated producer.

But it is a cost answer to a price opportunity. The H-acid thesis is that a producer sells more tonnes at much higher prices. Shree Pushkar’s programme lets it make the same tonnes more cheaply, and lets it make a great many more tonnes of complex fertiliser — a price-controlled, subsidy-linked product that structurally cannot capture a commodity upcycle.

Commissioning is the live execution risk

Units 5 and 6 were targeted for August and September 2026. As of 13 August, management described both as complete or near-complete but said capitalisation had not yet been announced, expected dry trials around the end of August and wet trials in September, and was taking a measured approach to commencement given raw-material price volatility — the same volatility that put the acid plants on low load. The working assumption offered was four to five months of Unit 6 and Unit 5 operation within FY27. The guided ₹1,350–1,400 Cr depends on that happening. As of 8 September, no commissioning intimation has been found.

A separate matter, recorded for completeness and not verified against a primary filing: a preferential issue of 7,36,196 warrants at ₹407.50 to the Joint Managing Director and promoter, raising roughly ₹30 Cr, is reported, with the reachable document dated 15 November 2025. Full conversion would add about 2.3% to the share count (derived) on the ~3.24 crore shares implied by market capitalisation divided by price. The balance sheet does not need it — FY26 net debt/equity was (0.01)× and non-lien deposits stood at ₹125 Cr at 30 June 2026.

The multiple expanded by almost exactly the price move, because the denominator did not move at all. Nothing was earned, revalued, contracted or commissioned in these eight sessions. What the market repriced is an expectation about a quarter that closes at the end of this month and reports in November.

But there is now a bridge, and it did not exist in the August report. On management’s own guided range, the current market capitalisation sits at roughly 13–14 times the year being guided to. That is not a demanding number for a business claiming domestic leadership in K-acid into a supply-short market. The entire question is whether the guided year arrives, and every input to it — the commissioning of Units 5 and 6, the acid plants coming back on load, Chinese orders converting from enquiry to shipment — is unproven and lands, if it lands, in Q2 and beyond.

Set against that: 51% of Q1 revenue came from a price-controlled fertiliser business that grew 4.0%, and 85% of the capital programme is aimed at making that half bigger. You are paying 22.96× trailing earnings and 2.70× book for a company that is roughly half levered to the theme that moved the price.

On that outcome today’s market capitalisation is roughly 13 times the delivered year (derived), the K-acid franchise prices off a China that has genuinely turned net importer, and the trailing multiple is revealed as a stale-denominator artefact.
Base
Chemicals growth improves materially from 17.1% as Q2 captures the post-July pricing, but the fertiliser half — 51% of Q1 value, price-controlled, growing 4.0% — caps the blend well short of the guided high-thirties. Commissioning slips a quarter, as it already has once. Margin recovers toward 8.5% at the PAT line without breaking meaningfully above the 11.4% EBITDA level that has now held for a year. The company grows at high-teens rather than at guidance, the stock tracks the delivered number rather than the guided one, and the re-rating already paid for absorbs most of it.
Bear
Q2 shows chemicals value up again on continued low acid volumes, so the realisation is flattered by mix for a second quarter and EBITDA stays near 11% — the pattern that has now repeated for four quarters. Units 5 and 6 slip further on the same raw-material volatility management has cited twice, and the guided ₹1,350–1,400 Cr, which was explicitly predicated on them running, is quietly reset. The sector bid unwinds with the cluster — Kiri, Bodal and Bhageria moved up on the same sessions and would move down on them too — and Shree Pushkar, which was never named in the reporting that caused the move, has no company-specific news to defend itself with. At a new all-time high there is no overhead resistance to reference and no recovery discount to fall back on, the moving averages sit a long way below and untested, and the capital programme is pointed at fertilisers.

Key Risks

01
The re-rating is the entire change
Trailing P/E moved 20.25× → 22.96× and Price/Book 2.41× → 2.70× between 27 August and 8 September. Zero quarters were reported in that window and no announcement of substance was made. The move is a change in what the market will pay, not in what the company earned.
02
The expansion is a fertiliser expansion
Roughly 85% of the ₹512 Cr planned capex (derived) sits in Units 8 and 6. Of the 72,000 MTPA of additional chemical capacity, 66,000 MTPA is acid complex and the rest is a ₹37 Cr dyes plant. No new dye-intermediates tonnage is announced anywhere in the programme — and the expansion was the August report’s main forward catalyst.
03
Half the revenue base structurally cannot participate
Fertilisers were 51% of Q1 FY27 sales value and grew 4.0%. Indian fertiliser pricing is controlled and subsidy-linked; it cannot capture a commodity upcycle however far H-acid runs. The capital programme makes this half larger, not smaller.
04
The realisation jump is substantially mix, not price
The derived ~90% rise in chemicals realisation coincides with management’s deliberate decision to run the acid plants on low load, which strips the cheapest tonnage out of the basket. Any Q2 print showing chemicals value up on still-depressed acid volumes will carry the same distortion and must be read the same way.
05
Q2 FY27 is the whole case, and it lands in November
The first quarter that can contain the H-acid step change is July–September, reported around November. Nothing before then confirms or refutes it: the 28 September AGM is a governance event and publishes no quarterly numbers. A price at an all-time high must hold through roughly two months with no confirming data.
06
The guidance is verbal, non-binding and conditional
The ₹1,350–1,400 Cr range with an 8.5–9% PAT margin was given on a call on 13 August and is explicitly predicated on Units 5 and 6 running. Management corrected an analyst who suggested 10%. It is the basis of the 13–14× guided-earnings figure in Section 10, and it is not a filed forecast.
07
Commissioning has already been deferred once
Units 5 and 6 were targeted for August and September 2026. On 13 August management described them as complete but capitalisation unannounced, trials pending, and commencement being timed carefully because of raw-material volatility. No commissioning intimation has been found as of 8 September.
08
It is a cluster trade, and this name was not even named in it
Kiri, Bodal and Bhageria hit upper circuits in the same session on 24 August on H-acid pricing; the reporting reviewed does not mention Shree Pushkar. It has re-rated by association with a basket. Correlation of that kind works in both directions and means company-specific analysis explains less of the price than usual right now.
09
No recovery discount, and now no overhead reference either
The stock has taken out the ₹481.60 all-time high the August report flagged and set a new one at ₹545.20. Every gain from here is new ground, there is no trapped-holder level to work against, and the session in which the new high was recorded closed lower.
10
The disclosure is management’s, not an audited segment note
The 51/49 split, the volume figures and the capex table all come from the investor presentation and the analyst call. No Ind AS 108 segment note could be retrieved. Animal health and nutrition sits inside Chemicals and is never separately quantified, and dye intermediates are never separated from dyes or from the acid complex — so the pure H-acid-chain exposure still cannot be sized.
11
Smallcap scale, and a promoter warrant overhang
~₹1,655 Cr, with the liquidity and volatility that implies. A reported preferential issue of 7,36,196 warrants at ₹407.50 to the Joint Managing Director would add about 2.3% to the share count on full conversion (derived), at a price well below the current market.

Summary

RATING: HOLD AT CMP — RE-RATED ON THE SECTOR, NOT ON A NUMBER

The rating does not change. The reason for it changes completely. In August this desk rated Shree Pushkar Hold because growth was single-digit and the cause was undiagnosed — it was not possible to tell whether the chemicals business was diluted by fertilisers or simply absent from the theme. That question is now answered, and answered in the company’s favour. The rating stays at Hold anyway, because the price has already taken the answer and more.

The arithmetic first. Between 27 August and 8 September the price rose 13.1% (derived), the trailing multiple went 20.25× → 22.96×, the book multiple 2.41× → 2.70×, and market capitalisation ~₹1,459 Cr → ~₹1,655 Cr. Those are the same move measured four ways, because no denominator moved. Zero quarters were reported. The only filings were an annual report, an AGM notice, a sustainability report, a newspaper publication and a ₹2.10 dividend. A 13% move on an unchanged Q1 is a re-rating, not a re-forecast.

What genuinely improved — and the honest caveat about when. The chemicals division grew sales value 17.1% against 4.0% for fertilisers, so the 10% blend was dilution rather than non-participation. Management raised FY27 visibility from ₹1,200–1,250 Cr to ₹1,350–1,400 Cr, citing Chinese orders returning for K-acid, H-acid and vinyl sulphone, and named a K-acid price move from ₹550 to ₹700-plus. The expansion is fully sized at ₹512 Cr. All of it was disclosed on 12 and 18 August, before the August report was published. It is new to this desk, not new to the market, and it is not a change in the reported growth rate.

Two of the new facts cut the other way, and they are the reason this is not an upgrade. The chemicals realisation appears to have risen ~90% (derived), but management explained that the volume fall came from deliberately running the acid plants on low load — so a large part of that is mix, not price, and it cannot be read as H-acid capture. And the expansion, sized at last, turns out to be 85% fertiliser (derived): of 72,000 MTPA of new chemical capacity, 66,000 MTPA is acid complex, and there is no new dye-intermediates tonnage anywhere in the programme. The August report hoped the capex would convert a price benefit into a price-and-volume benefit in dye intermediates. It will not.

And the timing caveat stands exactly where it did. Q1 FY27 ran April to June and closed four weeks before H-acid’s 28 July step change. Q2 FY27, reported around November, is the first quarter that can contain any of this. Nothing in any reported number yet reflects this thesis — not the 17.1%, not the realisation, not the guidance.

Why Hold rather than Reduce, and why Hold rather than Accumulate. Not Reduce: the framework passes every check it makes with no surveillance flag, the Stage 2 run is dated three months before the sector story so this is not purely a news trade, the chemicals half is genuinely growing, and on management’s guided year the market capitalisation sits at roughly 13–14 times earnings (derived) — undemanding if it lands. Not Accumulate: nothing reported has changed, the multiple has expanded by the full amount of the price move, half the revenue base is structurally excluded from the upcycle, the capital programme points away from the thesis, the realisation improvement is substantially a mix artefact, and the stock is at an all-time high it could not hold on the day it set it. The evidence improved in quality but not in date; the price improved in fact. When those two diverge, the rating follows the date.
