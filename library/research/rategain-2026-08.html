02 —

The Sojern Acquisition Defines This Company Now

Everything about RateGain’s reported numbers in Q1 FY27 flows from a single corporate event: the consolidation of Sojern, a travel digital-marketing business.

Sojern is why revenue rose 188%. Sojern is why MarTech grew 341% and now represents 81.1% of the revenue mix. Sojern is why adjusted EBITDA is up 289%. None of these figures describe organic performance, and any reading of this quarter that treats them as growth rather than as consolidation is misreading it.

That is not a criticism of the deal. Acquiring a demand-generation platform and cross-selling it into an existing hotel and travel customer base is a coherent strategy with real logic. But it changes what RateGain is:

  • Before Sojern: a mid-sized SaaS and data business serving travel, with three roughly balanced segments.
  • After Sojern: a company where four-fifths of revenue comes from digital marketing — a business with different margin characteristics, different customer economics, and greater sensitivity to advertising budgets than to software subscriptions.

The integration, not the growth rate, is the thing to underwrite.

Strip out MarTech and the picture changes completely. The two segments RateGain owned before the acquisition grew at 22.7% and 3.1%. That is the organic business: one decent segment and one that is barely moving.

Two conclusions follow directly, and they are the core of this report:

  • The 188% headline is acquisition arithmetic. The underlying pre-Sojern business is growing in the low-to-mid teens at best, blended across the two legacy segments.
  • Distribution at +3.1% is a concern. Connectivity between hotels and booking channels is a mature, competitive market. A segment growing at 3% inside a company valued as a growth technology business is a drag that will only become more visible as the acquisition anniversary passes.

Adjusted Versus Reported — A ₹22 Crore Gap

MeasureQ1 FY27YoY
Adjusted PAT₹116.8 Cr+148.8%
Reported PAT₹94.9 Cr+102%
Gap₹21.9 Cr19% of reported PAT

Roughly ₹22 crore separates what the company presents from what the accounts report — a gap equal to 19% of reported profit and 47 percentage points of growth rate.

Adjustments after a large acquisition are normal and often legitimate: one-time transaction costs, integration expenses, and amortisation of acquired intangibles all commonly get added back. The discipline is to know which:

  • Genuinely one-time — deal fees, advisory costs, restructuring. Adding these back is reasonable.
  • Recurring but non-cash — amortisation of acquired intangibles. Adding this back is standard practice and defensible for cash-flow purposes, but it represents real capital that was spent.
  • Recurring and cash — integration costs that persist year after year. Adding these back overstates earnings power.

This report notes the gap and its size rather than assuming which category applies; the composition is disclosed in the results and is worth reading directly. The screener figure of +102% profit growth is the reported number — the audited line, not the presented one.

Separately, at least one commentary headline described the quarter as showing a “profit drop”. That reading appears to compare sequentially rather than year on year; on a year-on-year basis reported profit rose 102%.

06 —

The USD 65 Million Guarantee

Alongside the results, the board approved a USD 65 million guarantee — roughly ₹575 crore at prevailing rates.

A corporate guarantee of that scale is a balance-sheet commitment, typically connected to acquisition financing or to a subsidiary’s borrowing. Against a ₹11,064 crore market capitalisation it is around 5%, which is not alarming in itself.

What it tells you is that the Sojern transaction has left financial obligations attached to it. A company that has just made a transformative acquisition, is guaranteeing USD 65 million, and is presenting adjusted rather than reported earnings is a company whose balance sheet deserves as much attention this year as its income statement. The relevant items to check are net debt, the interest line, and the terms under which the guarantee could be called.

What Organic Growth Actually Looks Like

The single most useful exercise with this company is to ask what it would have reported without Sojern. Using the disclosed segment shares and growth rates: DaaS at 12.6% of revenue growing 22.7%, and Distribution at 6.3% growing 3.1%, together represent 18.9% of the current revenue base.

Those two segments — the pre-acquisition business — are compounding at a blended rate in the mid-to-high teens, weighted toward the faster DaaS line.

This is an inference from disclosed segment data, not a company-reported organic growth figure. RateGain has not, in the sources reviewed, published a clean like-for-like organic number, which is itself worth noting.

Mid-teens organic growth is respectable and unremarkable. It is not what a 188% headline implies, and it is the rate that will become visible from Q1 FY28 onward once the acquisition laps and the comparison base includes Sojern. That anniversary — roughly four quarters out — is when the market will see RateGain’s true growth rate for the first time.

09 —

The Cyclicality Nobody Prices

With MarTech at 81.1% of revenue, RateGain is now overwhelmingly a travel advertising and demand-generation business.

That matters because advertising and software behave differently in a downturn. A SaaS subscription for rate intelligence is embedded in a hotel’s operations and is difficult to cancel mid-contract. A digital marketing budget is discretionary and is among the first lines cut when occupancy falls.

RateGain has therefore traded a defensive revenue mix for a faster-growing but more cyclical one. In a strong travel market — which is what the FIFA World Cup quarter reflects — this is clearly the better business. In a travel downturn it is clearly the worse one. The market has not yet observed the company through the second condition.

Summary

RATING: HOLD — AN ACQUISITION, NOT YET A TRACK RECORD

RateGain reported revenue up 188% and adjusted EBITDA up 289%. Almost all of it is the Sojern consolidation. The two segments the company owned a year ago grew 22.7% and 3.1% — that is the organic business, and it is a mid-teens compounder, not a hypergrowth one.

What makes this report different from the others in this batch is that RateGain is the only holding where the valuation and the chart are the conservative parts of the story. At 42.3× earnings, 5.51× book, just 4% above its 50-DMA and 11% off its high, it carries none of the technical stretch that KRN, HFCL or TD Power do. The risk here is not that the price has run too far; it is that the earnings base is at its most flattering point and the multiple is being applied to it.

The specific things to resolve before treating this as a settled position: the composition of the ₹22 crore in adjustments, the balance sheet after the USD 65 million guarantee, and whether Distribution can be fixed or should be written off as a mature drag. The date that matters is Q1 FY28, when the acquisition laps and the real growth rate becomes visible for the first time.
