02 —

Read This First

The timing rule applies as it does across this cluster: Q1 FY27 covers April to June 2026, and H-acid spiked from 28 July. Nothing reported here contains the thesis.

But Shree Pushkar poses a sharper question than its peers. Where Bhageria grew revenue 82% and Bodal 57% in that pre-squeeze quarter, Shree Pushkar grew 10.0% — and profit grew 9.4% on a flat margin.

That matters because the other names were already improving before the squeeze arrived. This one was not. The entire case here rests on Q2, with no operating momentum underneath it.

04 —

The Volume Dip

Commentary on the quarter describes growth holding up as volumes dip. That combination is significant and it deserves unpacking.

If revenue grew 10% while volumes fell, then realisations rose meaningfully — price did the work, not throughput. For a dye-intermediates producer in a market where Chinese supply discipline was already tightening through Q1, that is exactly the expected signature.

Read positively: Shree Pushkar was already capturing better prices before the July spike, which is a leading indicator for Q2.

Read cautiously: falling volumes in a market that is supposed to be supply-short is the wrong direction. If the deficit is real, a producer should be selling everything it can make. A volume decline suggests either capacity constraints — the expansion in Section 06 — or demand-side softness.

The volume decline is not quantified in the sources reviewed, nor is the split between the chemicals and fertiliser segments. Both are needed to resolve which reading applies, and both should be available in the quarterly filing.

Three Businesses in One

SegmentCharacter
Dye intermediatesThe H-acid-adjacent business. Cyclical, price-driven, currently the reason to look at this name.
FertilisersPrice-controlled and subsidy-dependent in India. Low margin, steady volume.
Animal / cattle feedStable, low-growth, consumer-linked.

The integration logic is genuine and unusual: by-products and effluent streams from chemical manufacturing are converted into fertiliser inputs, turning a disposal cost into a revenue line. In an industry where each tonne of H-acid generates roughly seven tonnes of wastewater, a company that monetises its waste stream has a real structural advantage — and a lower environmental risk profile than peers.

The cost of that integration is dilution. Only part of the business is levered to the dye-intermediates squeeze. Fertilisers, being price-controlled, cannot capture a commodity upcycle at all. That is a plausible explanation for why revenue grew 10% while pure-play peers grew 57% and 82%.

06 —

The Expansion

The company’s capital expansion is described as nearing the finish line, with a capex plan detailed alongside the Q1 results.

The timing could be favourable. Capacity arriving into a supply-short market with elevated realisations is the best possible sequence — it is the opposite of Happy Forgings or Jyoti CNC in your library, both of which are building capacity ahead of demand.

It also directly addresses the volume question. If volumes dipped because existing capacity is constrained, completing the expansion converts a price-only benefit into a price-and-volume benefit — which is where the real earnings leverage would come from.

The size, product mix, commissioning date and capital cost of the expansion are not established in the sources reviewed. This report does not estimate them. Given that this is the main forward catalyst, obtaining those details from the investor presentation is the highest-value homework on this name.

09 —

The Price Gap Nobody Else Has

At ₹451.10 against an all-time high of ₹481.60, Shree Pushkar is within 6% of the highest price it has ever traded.

That is a materially different position from its peers, and it cuts both ways:

  • No overhead supply. Almost every holder is in profit. There is no trapped population selling into strength, which is the drag on Bodal and Oriental Aromatics.
  • No recovery discount either. Bodal at 49% below its peak has an argument that the dumping cycle destroyed value that can return. Shree Pushkar has no such gap to close — any gain from here is genuinely new ground.

The interpretation is that this business did not suffer the same destruction in the Chinese dumping cycle — consistent with the integrated fertiliser model providing ballast. It is a steadier company, and correspondingly it has less cyclical upside.

You are paying more for a business that has been consistently rather than spectacularly profitable — which is a legitimate preference, but it is the opposite of what the H-acid thesis is asking you to buy.

Summary

RATING: HOLD — RIGHT VALUE CHAIN, SINGLE-DIGIT GROWTH

Shree Pushkar is genuinely in the dye-intermediates value chain — unlike Oriental Aromatics or Foseco, which only share the sector label — and its integrated model, converting chemical by-products into fertiliser inputs, is a real structural advantage in an industry defined by effluent.

But it had the weakest quarter of the four genuine dye names by a distance: revenue +10.0%, PAT +9.4%, margin flat, against Bhageria at +82%/+212% and Bodal at +57%/+178%. Growth came from realisations while volumes dipped — the wrong direction in a market that is supposed to be short of supply.

It is also the most expensive of the four on assets at 2.41× book and has the least room to recover, sitting only 6% below its all-time high where Bodal is 49% below. The steadiness is real and the integrated model is genuinely defensive — but that is an argument for owning a stable compounder, not for expressing a cyclical supply-squeeze thesis. The expansion completing into a short market is the one thing that could change that, and its size and timing are not yet disclosed.
