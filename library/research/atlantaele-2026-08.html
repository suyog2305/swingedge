07 —

EHV — The Value-Chain Question

Order book by voltage classValue
220 kV transformers>55% of the order book
400 kV transformers and reactors~₹275 Cr

Voltage class is the single best measure of a transformer maker’s technical standing. Higher voltage means tighter insulation engineering, fewer qualified competitors and better margins. Moving from distribution transformers to 220 kV to 400 kV EHV is the value ladder in this industry.

More than 55% of the book at 220 kV is a genuinely strong position, and ~₹275 crore at 400 kV — roughly 9% of the book — shows real progress into EHV. The May note identified “EHV prototype timing” as one of the two open debates; ₹275 crore of 400 kV work in the book is evidence that side of the debate is resolving favourably.

This is the sharpest good-company/bad-chart divergence in the imported batch, wider even than MTAR’s. Every operating line is strongly positive; every price measure is negative.

The most likely explanation is the one the May note itself named: multiple compression. The stock was up 87% year-to-date at the time of that report and traded at ~57× trailing earnings. A de-rating from that level does not require anything to go wrong with the business — only for the market to decide 57× was too much. At 60.3× today the compression is still working through.

09 —

Balance Sheet

The May note recorded a fully repaid term-loan stack — the company de-leveraged faster than expected during FY26 — alongside an FY27 capex plan described as bolder than modelled.

That combination is favourable: a debt-free-ish balance sheet funding its own expansion into a strong order cycle is the right position for a capital-goods company. It is also, at 14.2× book, priced as though it will continue indefinitely.

Current net debt and the FY27 capex figure are not established in the sources reviewed for this update. Worth confirming from the Q1 filing before sizing.

The de-rating is real but incomplete. The stock has fallen 22% from its high, yet still trades at 60.3× earnings and 14.2× book. For comparison within this batch: Balaji Amines at 3.88× book, Sanghvi Movers at 2.91×, Paramount at 2.45×.

A high-quality transformer maker in a strong T&D cycle deserves a premium. Whether it deserves 14.2× book while carrying RS 15 is the open question, and the market has been answering it in one direction for three months.

Scenarios

Bull
The order book keeps compounding at a 2× book-to-bill and the EHV progression continues past ₹275 Cr of 400 kV work into a genuine high-voltage franchise. India’s T&D and solar build-out runs multi-year. Earnings grow into the 60× multiple rather than the price falling to meet it, and the RS 15 reading proves to be a consolidation within a longer uptrend.
Base
The business keeps delivering 40–50% growth while the multiple keeps compressing. The stock goes sideways or drifts for several more quarters as 60× earnings normalises toward the growth rate. Nothing goes wrong operationally; the return simply does not arrive until the valuation resets.
Bear
T&D ordering slows, or competition in 220 kV transformers intensifies as capacity is added across the sector. Growth decelerates from 48% just as the market is already de-rating the stock. At 14.2× book with RS 15 and no Stage 2 support, there is nothing technical to arrest a decline.

13 —

Summary

RATING: HOLD — BUSINESS DELIVERING, TREND BROKEN

Atlanta Electricals is executing well. Q1 revenue +48%, EBITDA +58%, consolidated profit +50%, margin +105 basis points, order book up 25% in a quarter to ₹3,117 crore on a book-to-bill above 2×, and ~₹275 crore of 400 kV EHV work now in the backlog — resolving the EHV question the May note flagged as open.

And the stock carries RS 15, sits 22% below its 52-week high, and has fallen 15% over three months and 9.3% in the final week. It fails your trend template and is not in Stage 2.

The May note said the debate was “purely about multiple and EHV prototype timing”. Both have been answered since: EHV favourably, the multiple unfavourably. At 60.3× earnings and 14.2× book after a 22% fall, the de-rating from ~57× on a stock that had run 87% YTD looks incomplete rather than finished. The business is not the problem here; the price it was carrying is.
