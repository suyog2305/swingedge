01 —

Company Overview

This is a correcting edition. A desk audit of 43 sources, finished on 19 September 2026, overturns two statements in this desk’s own 26 August report and re-frames the oil thesis [45]. One: the August report said the CrudeChem purchase price was undisclosed. It was disclosed, entity by entity, in the company’s Regulation 30 filing of 5 December 2025: up to USD 11.2 mn for 53.33% [2]. Two: the August report described CrudeChem as a production-chemicals business and therefore “less cyclical than a frac-chemicals business”. On the company’s own descriptions its core is friction reducers and frac additives, which is completion chemistry [3][6][7][8]. The oilfield-cyclicality risk deserves more weight than August gave it, and the beta is to well completions, not to barrels. Both corrections are set out in full in §02 to §04. A third item surfaced while this edition was being written, and it is an omission rather than an error of fact: on 17 August 2026, nine days before the August report, the board resolved to seek authority to raise up to ₹800 crore of new equity, and shareholders gave it on 11 September [48][9][49]. The August report did not mention it and the audit did not pick it up. It is an enabling resolution, not an issuance. It is set out in §02 and carried into §09 to §13.

The related-party ceiling deserves its own sentence. Authority to lend up to ₹400 crore to the FZE and to CrudeChem within one year is more than the whole treasury recorded in August. What the corrected price does and does not tell you. It says the first tranche was bought cheaply on turnover, from founders who were short of capital, and that Fineotex did not overpay for what has since become two-thirds of its revenue. It does not say the second tranche will be cheap. At the first tranche’s implied valuation, 25% would cost about USD 5.3 mn (desk arithmetic: 25% of USD 21.0 mn), or roughly ₹50 crore on management’s own loose equivalence of ₹1,000 crore to “almost USD 100 million” [5]. The ₹363 Cr of cash and liquid investments recorded in August [1] would cover that several times over. But nothing in any filing says the second tranche is priced on the first tranche’s terms, and the business is far larger than when that value was struck. The August edition’s instruction to watch for the consideration terms stands, now with a number to compare them against, and with one new fact beside it: shareholders have since authorised an equity raise of up to ₹800 crore (§02), so the treasury is no longer the only way the tranche could be paid for.

Correction Two — What CrudeChem Actually Sells

Oilfield chemicals are not one market. Drilling-fluid additives follow rigs and wells drilled. Friction reducers, frac acid, surfactants and coiled-tubing additives follow frac crews and wells completed, and the water and proppant pumped into each well. Production chemicals (demulsifiers, corrosion and scale inhibitors, paraffin control) follow the count of producing wells and the fluids they produce [45]. Which of these a company mostly sells decides what it is exposed to. The company has never published a product split, so the question has to be answered from its own descriptions, taken in date order.

The measure closest to CrudeChem’s core fell by half. The US frac spread count, Primary Vision’s series as published by the American Oil & Gas Reporter, peaked at 295 on 31 March 2023 and bottomed at 145 on 6 February 2026, a fall of 51%, across a period in which Texas output rose [17]. Causality runs from drilling to completion to production, months later, and never the other way. A production tracker therefore lags the activity that buys CrudeChem’s chemicals. There is one offsetting point for the chemicals thesis, and it is an inference: longer laterals and bigger frac designs mean more friction reducer per well, so completion-chemical volumes probably fall by less than the well count does. No public volume series was found to test that [45]. It is a reason to watch frac crews, which measure pumping, rather than wells or rigs alone.

What the tracker cannot tell you

  • The split. CrudeChem’s product split, customer concentration, Permian share and standalone margin are all undisclosed. The mapping to completions is inferred from the company’s own descriptions [45].
  • Share gain against the market. The 2026 step-up happened against falling Permian completions. It came from capital, supplier terms and wallet share. If that reverses, because a large customer re-sources, no rig or frac series will warn you (compare Innospec in 2024 [24]).
  • Price against volume. A “war surcharge” and raw-material pass-through inflate revenue when oil-linked inputs spike [5]. Revenue can rise on flat volumes and fall when inputs deflate.
  • What reaches the shareholder. 46.67% of CrudeChem’s profit belongs to minorities until January 2028, and the step-up price is undisclosed [4].
  • The share price. It has moved with results, corporate actions, a rating and Indian market risk, not with crude [32]. Valuation is outside any oil tracker.
  • Cash conversion and receivables. The August edition’s top risk is invisible to activity data [1].

What the corrections do to the valuation argument

  • The multiple is being paid mostly for the legacy business. On the §05 derivation about 71% of owners’ profit is not oil. A trailing multiple in the mid-fifties is therefore, arithmetically, mostly a multiple on a textile, hygiene and treasury income stream whose revenue is flat, plus a smaller, faster-growing, minority-diluted oil stream. The August edition asked whether 48× was the right price for “acquired, minority-diluted, lower-margin oilfield earnings”. The sharper question is what multiple the other seven-tenths deserves.
  • The oil stream now deserves a cyclical’s multiple, not a compounder’s. Earnings that follow frac crews were cut in half, peak to trough, between March 2023 and February 2026 on the activity measure (§06). A stream of that kind is ordinarily valued as a cyclical, on a lower multiple of mid-cycle earnings, because the next downswing takes part of the earnings with it. No listed pure-play trades in India to anchor the number, and none is invented here.
  • The entry price is a genuine positive the August edition missed. About USD 21.0 mn for 100% (auditor arithmetic) of a group that, on the §05 derivation, earned about ₹20.6 Cr after tax in a single quarter. Whatever else is said about this deal, it was not an expensive one. That is a statement about management’s capital allocation, and it belongs on the positive side of the ledger.
  • The step-up is worth less than August implied, and more uncertain. The August edition called it “the cleanest EPS catalyst available”. On Q1 FY27 numbers it adds roughly ₹5 Cr a quarter to owners’ profit, about 13% (pro forma, §05), if CrudeChem’s profit in 2028 looks like its profit today. EIA’s path has WTI below the Permian breakeven by the month before it closes (§07). A contracted purchase of more of a cyclical business, at an undisclosed price, timed by calendar rather than by cycle, is a two-sided event.
  • The share count is no longer a fixed input. Shareholders have authorised up to ₹800 crore of new equity (§02). Purely as an illustration of scale, ₹800 crore issued at the 18 September close would be about 14 crore new shares on about 116 crore, roughly 12% more (derived [44][1]); an actual issue, if one ever comes, would be priced under the regulator’s formula on the day and could be any size up to the ceiling, or nothing. Every per-share figure in this report, and every percentage in §05, is a share of a pie that the company now has permission to cut into more slices. Whether that is good or bad for an existing owner depends entirely on what the money earns, and the stated uses, which run from working capital to “inorganic growth”, do not yet say.
None of this says the stock is going down. §08 says it is behaving very well, and well-behaved stocks at full multiples can stay that way for a long time. It says that the two things an oil-led thesis would be paying for, a proportional link to Texas and a non-cyclical earnings stream, are not what the evidence shows is there.

Summary

RATING: NEUTRAL — REAL OIL EXPOSURE, WEAKER LINK, FULLER MULTIPLE

This desk got two things wrong in August, and both mattered. It told its reader that the price paid for CrudeChem was undisclosed, when the 5 December 2025 filing gives it entity by entity: up to USD 11.2 mn for 53.33%, or about USD 21.0 mn for the whole group on the auditor’s arithmetic. And it told its reader that the acquired business sold production chemicals and was therefore less cyclical than a frac-chemicals business, when the company’s own website, deck, annual report and calls put friction reducers and frac additives first. The first correction is good news about how the deal was struck. The second is bad news about what was bought: its demand follows frac crews and completed wells, the part of the oilfield that is cut first and fastest.

The exposure is real and it is not proportional. Oil was about 65% of Q1 FY27 revenue and, on management’s implied margin, about 55–59% of EBITDA. But on the desk’s derivation from the filed minority-interest line it was only about 29% of the profit that belongs to a Fineotex shareholder, because the business nets about 8% and 46.67% of that goes to its founders. At constant margins a 10% rise in CrudeChem’s revenue adds about 3% to owners’ quarterly profit. The contracted step-up to about 78% in January 2028 improves that arithmetic, at a price nobody outside the company knows.

The chain from Texas crude to the Fineotex share price fails at three of its four links. More production does not mean more drilling; it has meant less: Texas output up 8.7% on 39% fewer rigs, Permian output up on fewer rigs, fewer wells and a DUC inventory run down by more than half. CrudeChem’s sales have not tracked the basin: down about 10% while Texas output rose, then sharply up while Permian completions fell 8%, for reasons management itself describes as capital, suppliers and wallet share. And the share price has not traded on oil: a correlation of −0.03 with WTI since the deal closed against +0.36 with the Nifty, a 52-week low made with WTI near USD 103, and a 69% rally while crude fell by a third. What does lead CrudeChem’s revenue, in order, is frac crews, Permian completions and DUCs, then rigs, then WTI against the USD 67 breakeven as a regime flag. The desk’s Theme Trackers page now follows those series. Texas production is the last chart on it, because it lags.

The framework has no objection, and the evidence has moved the other way. Every technical reading in §08 is strong. Against that: the price is about 12% higher than in August on identical earnings; the trailing multiple is in the mid-fifties against peers at 16–26×; about seven-tenths of the profit under that multiple comes from a legacy business whose revenue is flat; the August edition’s top risk, a year of profit with negative operating cash flow, has had no new evidence either way; and EIA’s own forecast path, which is only a forecast, takes WTI below the Permian breakeven by the month before the step-up falls due.

One more thing the August report should have carried. On 17 August the board resolved to seek, and on 11 September shareholders granted, authority to raise up to ₹800 crore of new equity, alongside authority to lend up to ₹400 crore to the US side within the year. Neither is a transaction. Together they suggest that the growth in Texas is being financed at least partly from Mumbai, that the company wants room for more capital than its treasury holds, and that the share count beneath every per-share number in this report is no longer fixed. The August edition’s cleanest argument, that the 2028 step-up could be paid from cash already in hand, now sits beside a shareholder-approved equity option. The board’s decision was public nine days before the August report, and it is reported here late.

Why the rating’s wording has changed. The August rating was “Hold / Accumulate on Declines”. The second half is withdrawn, for two reasons. It was an entry instruction, and the desk’s own rules say a report analyses a company and does not tell anyone when to buy it. And the evidence since has gone against it: a fuller multiple on unchanged earnings, and an engine more cyclical than this desk said. Neutral describes a two-sided body of evidence: a strong balance sheet, a cheap entry into Texas, a contracted step-up and a very strong trend on one side; a full multiple, a completions beta, a flat core, unproven cash conversion, a standing authority to issue equity and an oil link much weaker than it first appears on the other. It is not a view on how much of this stock anyone should hold, or when, or whether any plan for it is wise. The report’s job ends at the evidence.
