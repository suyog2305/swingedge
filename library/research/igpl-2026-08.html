02 —

Read This First: This One Is Different

Across this cluster the recurring caution has been that Q1 FY27 predates the H-acid squeeze, so the thesis is not yet in the numbers. IGPL is the exception, and for a specific reason.

IGPL is not primarily a dye-intermediates play. Its product is phthalic anhydride, whose main markets are plasticizers, resins and paints. It has a genuine but secondary adjacency to pigments (Section 07). What drove its quarter was its own spread recovery — the gap between PAN prices and orthoxylene feedstock — not H-acid.

That makes IGPL the one name here whose turnaround is already visible in reported numbers rather than anticipated. The ₹71 crore is banked. The question is durability, not arrival.

Management attributes the improvement to better realisations, recovery in core chemical spreads, and robust domestic demand.

04 —

The Spread — What Actually Drives This Business

Phthalic anhydride economics are simple and brutal: IGPL buys orthoxylene, converts it to PAN, and earns the spread. Volume is relatively stable; the spread is everything.

That structure explains both halves of the story:

  • Why it lost money. When PAN prices are compressed — by Chinese oversupply, or by orthoxylene costs rising faster than PAN can pass through — the spread collapses and a high-fixed-cost plant goes to a loss. That is where IGPL was a year ago at a −0.15% margin.
  • Why the recovery is violent. When the spread widens, virtually all of it drops to EBITDA. Revenue grew 32% and EBITDA grew fiftyfold — the incremental rupee of revenue carried almost pure margin.

The essential caution follows directly. A margin that moved 1,778 basis points in one direction can move comparably in the other, and IGPL does not control either side of its spread. Orthoxylene is a crude derivative; PAN pricing is set by regional supply. This is a price-taker at both ends.

Maleic anhydride revenue is disclosed at roughly ₹17–18 crore, with improved realisations noted at the GEP facility — a small but growing second product line.

05 —

The Plasticizer Plant — The Forward Catalyst

Capacity75,000 tonnes
StatusMechanically completed
Commercial productionExpected by September 2026
FY27 target output15,000 – 20,000 tonnes

This is forward integration, and it is the most strategically important development on this name. Plasticizers are made from phthalic anhydride — IGPL currently sells PAN to plasticizer producers, and will now convert some of it itself.

Two consequences follow:

  • It captures a second margin. Instead of earning only the orthoxylene-to-PAN spread, IGPL earns the PAN-to-plasticizer spread as well on the integrated volume.
  • It reduces spread dependence. When PAN pricing is weak, an integrated producer can push volume downstream rather than sell into a poor market. That is the structural answer to the vulnerability described in Section 04.

“Mechanically completed” with commercial production due in September is about as de-risked as a capacity milestone gets — the plant is built, not planned. The FY27 target of 15,000–20,000 tonnes is 20–27% utilisation, so this is a ramp story into FY28 rather than an immediate earnings event.

An organic waste-to-biogas project is unrelated diversification of the kind this library has flagged repeatedly — Man Industries into real estate, Kirloskar into an NBFC, Kiri into copper.

The case for treating it more leniently here: CBG in India carries policy support through blending mandates, the scale appears modest relative to the core business, and energy self-supply has some logic for a chemical manufacturer.

The capital committed, capacity and expected returns are not established in the sources reviewed. Given the pattern across your library, the thing to watch is not whether it exists but how much capital it absorbs relative to the plasticizer plant — which is the investment with a clear strategic rationale.

07 —

The Dye Adjacency — Real but Secondary

Phthalic anhydride is a precursor to phthalocyanine pigments — the blue and green pigment family used in inks, paints, plastics and textiles. That gives IGPL a genuine link to the colourants value chain.

Keep it in proportion. Pigments are one of several PAN end-markets, and plasticizers and resins are larger. The H-acid squeeze described in the circulating note concerns dye intermediates for reactive and disperse dyes — a different chemistry from phthalocyanine pigments.

So IGPL sits between the two ends of this cluster: more connected to colourants than Oriental Aromatics or Foseco, materially less connected than Bodal, Bhageria, Kiri or Shree Pushkar. Its recovery is a phthalic anhydride spread story that happens to be occurring at the same time.

09 —

The 48% Gap

IGPL trades at ₹532 against an all-time high of ₹1,018.60 — 48% below the peak, while sitting within 2% of its 52-week high.

That gap is the record of the downcycle the company has just emerged from. A business that fell to a −0.15% operating margin was priced accordingly, and the recovery has retraced only part of it.

The constructive reading is that the earnings power that justified ₹1,018 has returned — a ₹71 crore quarterly profit annualises to roughly ₹284 crore against a ₹1,638 crore market capitalisation. The cautionary reading is that the previous peak was itself set on a favourable spread environment, and a cyclical business should not be valued on its best conditions.

Annualising Q1 assumes the current spread persists for four quarters. For a commodity spread business that is a strong assumption — it is shown to illustrate the gap between trailing and current earnings power, not as a forecast.

The ~5.8× annualised multiple is the single most striking valuation number in this cluster, and it is also the one most dependent on an assumption. If PAN spreads hold, IGPL is trading at under six times current earnings with a plasticizer plant about to add a second margin layer. If spreads normalise halfway back, the multiple doubles and the stock is ordinary.

At 1.22× book the downside is better protected than the earnings multiple suggests — you are paying close to asset value for India’s largest PAN producer.

Scenarios

Bull
PAN spreads hold near current levels on continued domestic demand and disciplined regional supply. The plasticizer plant starts in September and ramps through FY28, adding a second margin layer and reducing spread dependence. Annualised earnings of roughly ₹284 Cr against a ₹1,638 Cr market cap prove sustainable, and the stock closes a good part of the 48% gap to its all-time high.
Base
Spreads narrow from an unusually wide Q1 level but stay well above the loss-making conditions of a year ago. Margin settles somewhere between 18.25% and mid-single digits. The plasticizer ramp proceeds slowly, hitting the low end of the 15,000–20,000 tonne FY27 target. Earnings are good but not annualisable at the Q1 rate.
Bear
Orthoxylene costs rise or regional PAN capacity returns, and the spread compresses as violently as it expanded. A 1,778 bps swing runs in reverse and a high-fixed-cost plant returns toward breakeven, as it was twelve months ago. The plasticizer plant adds depreciation before it adds volume, and the CBG project absorbs capital that the core business needs.

Summary

RATING: ACCUMULATE ON DIPS — THE SPREAD RECOVERY IS ALREADY BANKED

I G Petrochemicals is the one name in this cluster whose turnaround is in the reported numbers rather than in front of them. A ₹8.2 crore loss became a ₹71 crore profit, operating EBITDA rose roughly fiftyfold to ₹113 crore, and the margin swung 1,778 basis points — the largest single-year move in this entire library.

It trades at 1.22× book and roughly 5.8× annualised current earnings, and the 75,000-tonne plasticizer plant is mechanically complete with commercial production due in September — forward integration that captures a second margin and directly reduces the spread dependence that caused the loss in the first place.

Two disciplines. This is a pure spread business with no control over either end — orthoxylene in, PAN out — and a margin that moves 1,778 basis points up can move comparably down. And it is not the dye-intermediates play the circulating note describes: its colourants link runs through phthalocyanine pigments, not the reactive-dye chemistry behind the H-acid squeeze. Own it for the PAN spread and the plasticizer ramp, not for H-acid.
