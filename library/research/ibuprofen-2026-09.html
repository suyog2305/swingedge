01 —

What This Report Is

A request came to this desk for a consolidated report on SMS Pharmaceuticals, IOL Chemicals & Pharmaceuticals, Solara Active Pharma Sciences, Shree Pushkar Chemicals & Fertilisers and Vinati Organics, framed as beneficiaries of “the shortage of ibuprofen in Europe amid supply cuts from China”. Before writing that report, the premise was tested against primary sources. It did not survive the test as framed, and this note says so in its title, in its verdict and here. What follows is not the case for the thesis. It is an audit of the thesis, and then an answer to the question actually being asked: which of these five has any exposure to ibuprofen at all, and under what conditions any of them benefits.

The claim as it circulated

Two articles launched the theme: Equitymaster on 7 September 2026 [1] and Moneypost/NewsX on 8 September 2026 [2]. Both state that ibuprofen has “availability problems… in Germany, Spain, Ireland, Italy and Sweden, covering 21 product presentations”, and both source that single fact to one commercial tracker, Mobidoctor [3]. Neither article mentions China, API supply, or supply cuts of any kind. The “supply cuts from China” leg was added somewhere between those articles and the request that reached this desk; it has no source in either piece.

What this note is and is not

It is a comparative note on five names, of which four have some exposure to the ibuprofen chain and one — Shree Pushkar — has none; it is in the note because it was asked about, and §07 explains the finding. It carries no rating and no recommendation; the framework’s own signals for each name are generated into §08 from the 10 September close and refresh with each scan. It does not size positions, allocate capital or advise on holdings. The desk’s standalone reports on two of these names — the 8 September IOLCP update and the 8 September Shree Pushkar update, both in this library — are cited where useful and are not contradicted here.

02 —

The Verdict

The premise partly holds at its narrowest and fails as framed. The disagreement between the story and the evidence is the finding, so it comes first.

LegStatusThe evidence, dated
Ibuprofen is short in EuropePartly holds, at its narrowestFive of nine tracked national registers list active issues; 22 presentations (11 Sep 2026) [3]; 15 rows in Italy [4], 5 in Spain [5]. But the registers attribute them to product-level manufacturing problems (15 of 16 causes) or high demand (1), alternatives are available in every case, the UK and France list none [6][7], the German advisory board has not mentioned ibuprofen in five sittings to June 2026 [8], and ibuprofen is on neither the EU Union list of critical medicines (v2.1, Dec 2025) [12] nor BfArM’s critical-supply list [10]. Some Italian entries date from 2022–2025 [4].
China has cut supplyRefuted by Chinese primary filingsShandong Xinhua’s H1 2026 report: analgesic API revenue −16.42%, group profit −33.35%, price cuts “to consolidate market share”, outlook “demand continues weak, competition intense” [28]. Hendi cancelled a 5,000 t expansion because the market was “saturated” and prices “low”, wording repeated in its H1 2026 report [30][33]. Three price trackers describe Chinese plants running near nameplate with prices firming only 4–7% a quarter on feedstock costs [39][40][41]. No 2026 evidence of a Chinese ibuprofen plant shutdown, inspection stoppage or export restriction was found.
These five Indian names benefitNot supported as framedSolara’s ibuprofen runs at a −12% to −23% EBITDA margin and bankers are evaluating “strategic options” for it [61][64]; IOL is deliberately shrinking ibuprofen from 82% to 57% of pharma revenue [53][54]; SMS is roughly 20% ibuprofen and attributes 2026 price rises to war-driven feedstock [70]; Vinati is upstream, with IBB at 10–12% of revenue and volumes down 20% [50]; Shree Pushkar has no exposure whatsoever [77].
The strongest part of the claim is that dated register entries exist: 22 presentations are genuinely listed, with prognoses running from 30 September 2026 to 31 March 2027 [3]. The weakest part is everything built on top of them — the China leg, which the Chinese makers’ own half-year reports contradict, and the implied Indian windfall, which no Indian filing supports. “Shortage in Europe” is true only in the sense that is true for roughly 900 other presentations in Spain and 2,600 in Italy on the same day [4][5].

The one verified 2026 phenomenon is cost-push. Ibuprofen prices firmed by 4.3% (Q1) and 5.3–7.4% (Q2) quarter on quarter in 2026, and the trackers that explain the move attribute it to isobutylbenzene feedstock and Middle East petrochemical disruption, not to scarcity of the API [39][40]. SMS Pharma’s CEO said the same thing on 27 May 2026: “there is a price increase also that is happening in products like ibuprofen… because of this war” [70]. That is a real effect with real winners and losers among these names — §09 sets them out — but it is not the thesis that was circulated, and it is already inside the quarter every one of these companies has reported.

The tracker the articles relied on

Mobidoctor’s page, updated 11 September 2026 [3], shows five of nine tracked registers with active issues and 22 presentations — one more than the articles’ 21 — across film-coated tablets of 200–800 mg, effervescent granules and depot tablets. Its stated causes: 15 of 16 manufacturing problems, 1 unexpectedly high demand. Its prognoses run from 30 September 2026 to 31 March 2027. All five registers indicate alternatives available. The page makes no statement about an API shortage; it addresses finished-dose availability only, and it says so in its own words: “Availability is national — a shortage in one country does not mean it is short everywhere.”

The policy layer, checked

The Critical Medicines Act reached a provisional political agreement between Council and Parliament on 12 May 2026 (EMA news [13]; Commission IP/26/1017 [14]; Council release [15], which returned HTTP 403 on fetch; European Pharmaceutical Review, 15 May 2026 [16]). The Commission text says that where there is “a high dependency on a single or a limited number of third countries, the CMA goes further, foreseeing the obligation for contracting authorities to favour ‘manufacturing in the EU’”, and provides for Strategic Projects for critical medicines “or their active substances”; it is “now subject to formal approval” [14]. No medicine is named in the Commission or EMA text. The Critical Medicines Alliance Strategic Report of 28 February 2025 [17] contains no ibuprofen-specific recommendation, and no HERA statement on ibuprofen was found. One fetched Commission health page carried “July 10, 2026” as an agreement date; EMA, the Council URL and EPR all give 12 May 2026, which is the date used here. Two European Times pieces (4 May and 12 Jun 2026) [24] speculate about paracetamol and ibuprofen shortages arising from the Iran conflict and cite no data.

What a Real Ibuprofen Shortage Looked Like

Europe has had two documented ibuprofen shortages in the last decade. Both are useful because they show what the evidence trail looks like when the API itself is short — and how different that trail is from the one in §03.

The order of events is the tell. In 2018 the shortage began upstream — one plant equal to a sixth of world supply — and showed up in wholesale rationing within weeks, with the regulator making inquiries about supply. In 2026 the sequence is reversed: a scatter of finished-dose entries with product-level causes, an API market that the two Chinese makers themselves describe as saturated, and no regulator anywhere in Europe treating ibuprofen as critical. Nothing comparable to 2018–19 or 2022 is documented for 2026.

IOL is the one name where a genuine European ibuprofen shortage would land on a large volume base: 12,000 MTPA of ibuprofen and its own 12,000 MTPA of IBB behind it [56][58]. It is also the company most deliberately moving away from the molecule. Ibuprofen was 82% of pharma revenue in FY21, 63% in FY26 and about 57% in Q1 FY27, when non-ibuprofen products were 43% [53][54]; the desk’s 8 September IOLCP update — rated Hold at CMP — Thesis Intact, Price Ahead of the Evidence — is built on exactly that mix shift toward a 50-50 target, not on ibuprofen. FY26 revenue was ₹2,319 cr with PAT of ₹138 cr [55] (a second source carries ₹2,340.3 cr, likely total income — both shown); Q1 FY27 revenue ₹756 cr (+37%) and PAT ₹64.5 cr (+89.9%) [53]. Management’s own words on ibuprofen demand in May 2026 were “stabilized after previous overstocking issues” [55] — the language of a market returning to normal, not of one running short. Promoter holding has risen from 48.19% (Sep 2023) to 62.28% (Jun 2026) [59], and the stock carries a Long-Term ASM Stage I flag [60].

Solara Active Pharma Sciences — a possible seller, not a beneficiary

Solara owns what it calls one of the world’s largest ibuprofen facilities and runs it at a quarter to a third of combined capacity (derived), with the Vizag plant mothballed since 2024 at a fixed cost of ₹12–15 cr a year [62][64][67]. The business loses money at the EBITDA line: −22.9% in Q3 FY26 on ₹102.4 cr of revenue and 23% gross margin [64], −12% in Q1 FY27 [61]. In February 2026 the board appointed bankers to evaluate “strategic options” for ibuprofen, with a conclusion due by the end of H1 FY27 [61][63][64] — which is to say, a sale or restructuring of the very business this thesis says should be booming is on the table, and a polymers/CRAMS carve-out has been put on hold [61]. The wider record is a FY24 net loss of ₹567 cr [69], a ₹449.95 cr rights issue at ₹375 a share (record date 15 May 2024, allotment 19 Jun 2024) [67], and net debt of ₹479 cr at 30 June 2026 [61]. Its FY26 bottom line conflicts across sources: a ₹7.41 cr net loss on ₹1,369 cr of revenue per the Free Press Journal [65] against a ₹17 cr profit on Screener [69]; FY25 likewise (₹0.54 cr profit [65] vs ₹1 cr loss [69]). Two further figures in circulation are noted and not used: Business Standard’s “ibuprofen revenue… Rs 849 crore” for Q4 FY26 is evidently ₹84.9 cr, and Moneypost’s FY26 ibuprofen revenue of ₹334 cr with −₹53 cr EBITDA [2] could not be verified against a filing. A price rise a seller cannot capture at a positive margin is not a windfall.

SMS Pharmaceuticals — a capex-led volume story, priced for cost not scarcity

SMS is the most straightforwardly exposed of the three API makers in proportion to its size — “roughly around 20%” of revenue is ibuprofen [70], about ₹177 cr on FY26 revenue of ₹887 cr (derived), with FY26 EBITDA of ₹171 cr and PAT of ₹102 cr [71] and Q1 FY27 revenue of ₹207 cr with PAT of ₹21.1 cr [72]. Its story is volume: Vizag running at about 80% of 500 t/month, expanding to 800 t/month by March 2027 — a 60% capacity increase (derived) — within a ₹280 cr programme [70][71], with the CEP (July 2022) and EU GMP (October 2024) already in hand [74][75]. On price, the CEO’s attribution on 27 May 2026 was explicit: the increase in ibuprofen is happening “because of this war” — feedstock, not shortage — and it is backward integration, not scarcity pricing, that he credits for holding margin [70]. Its regulated-market share of ibuprofen sales is not disclosed in accessible materials; the last guidance was 70% export in February 2023 [73]. One customer accounted for 28% of FY26 revenue across products.

Vinati Organics — the upstream tell

Vinati does not make ibuprofen. It makes isobutyl benzene, the raw material every ibuprofen plant in the world consumes, and it is the largest producer of it: “65% global market share in ATBS and IBB” per its FY25 annual report and CARE [48][49], about 90% of India’s IBB exports, some 9 KTPA in CY24, 77% of it to the US [47]. Hendi’s own H1 2026 risk factor — that its IBB supplier is in India [30] — is consistent with Chinese ibuprofen makers as IBB customers, though Vinati is not named. The exposure is real but small and shrinking: IBB was 20–30% of revenue in the February 2023 telling [51] and 10–12% of FY26 revenue per the CFO on 27 May 2026 [50], roughly ₹223–267 cr on FY26 sales of ₹2,228 cr and PAT of ₹488 cr (derived from [52]). And the volume went the wrong way: IBB volume fell 20% in FY26 “due to unavailability of key raw materials” [50], after an FY25 annual report that described a “downtrend due to specific demand-side challenges” [49]. If Indian ibuprofen plants were being pulled harder by European buyers, the first place it would appear is in the tonnage of IBB they buy. It fell.

Shree Pushkar Chemicals & Fertilisers — not in this thesis

Shree Pushkar has no disclosed exposure to the ibuprofen chain. The 274-page Annual Report 2024-25 contains zero occurrences of “ibuprofen”, “isobutyl”, “IBB”, “API” or “active pharmaceutical” [77]. The company’s website lists reactive dyes, dye intermediates (H-acid, vinyl sulphone, K-acid, gamma acid, sulpho para base), fertilisers (SSP, NPK, SOP, WSF), acids (sulphuric, oleum, chlorosulphonic) and a cattle-feed supplement [78]. Its Q1 FY27 call on 13 August 2026 covered fertilisers (51% of ₹281 cr) and chemicals (49%), sulphur and ammonia shortages from the Hormuz closure, and a capex programme of 450,000 MTPA of fertiliser and 72,000 MTPA of chemicals — and not one pharmaceutical product [79]. The desk’s 8 September Shree Pushkar update, rated Hold at CMP — Re-Rated on the Sector, Not on a Number, treats it as what it is: a dye-intermediates and fertiliser company moving with the H-acid cluster. It does not belong to this thesis and is in this note only because it was asked about. Any move in the name on ibuprofen news would be flow it did not earn.

Scenarios

Three paths, with the evidence that would move the reading between them and the dates on which it can arrive. Probabilities are stated in words; no percentages are invented.

A — The thesis proves right
An API-level shortage materialises. What would show it: a Union-list revision or BfArM Beirat minute naming ibuprofen [8][12]; a Xinhua or Hendi disclosure of a stoppage or export restriction (Xinhua’s Q3 report, late October 2026, is the next scheduled filing) [28]; Mobidoctor entries multiplying with API-supply causes rather than resolving on their 30 Sep 2026–31 Mar 2027 prognoses [3]; IOL citing scarcity rather than cost at its Q2 FY27 call around November 2026 [53]; Solara’s utilisation rising instead of its business being sold [64]. Beneficiaries in that order: IOL on volume and its own IBB, SMS as Vizag ramps toward 800 t/month by March 2027, Vinati on IBB volume, and Solara only if it still owns the plant. Least consistent with the evidence in hand; nothing observed today points here.
B — Cost-push only (base case)
Prices stay firm while feedstock stays dear and no shortage of the API appears. Integrated makers hold or widen margins — IOL and SMS — while Vinati gains modestly on IBB price against a shrunken volume base [50]. The register entries resolve on their stated prognoses; Xinhua’s H2 “pressure” [28] and Hendi’s “saturated” market [30] keep Chinese prices from running; Solara’s strategic review concludes by end-H1 FY27 with a sale, restructuring or continued losses [61][63]. Most consistent with every filing, register and tracker read for this note. It is also already inside the quarter each company has reported.
C — Oversupply reasserts
The Chinese makers’ own outlook plays out: demand “continues weak”, competition “intense” [28], Xinhua keeps cutting price to defend share, NHU’s 5,400 t comes on if it is built [36][37], and the feedstock support fades if the Middle East conflict eases. Prices fall back toward or below the levels the trackers already describe as at or under 2022 [33][40]. IOL’s ibuprofen share falls faster than planned; SMS’s 800 t/month lands into a soft market; Solara faces impairment rather than exit; Vinati’s IBB volumes stay down. Plausible — the Chinese filings lean this way — and it is the scenario the 2018–2022 capacity additions in §04 were built for.

The pivot between B and C is the feedstock, not the API: the only 2026 support for ibuprofen prices that any source documents is the cost of isobutylbenzene and petrochemicals under the conflict [40][70]. The pivot between B and A is a regulator’s document, not a tracker’s, and no such document exists as of 11 September 2026.

Xinhua cut prices in 2025 and H1 2026 “to consolidate and raise market share” and expects H2 to face “considerable pressure” [26][28]; Hendi describes international prices as “continuing to run at low levels” [31]; nameplate of more than 32,000 t stands against ~21,500 t of consumption on one tally [36], and NHU’s 5,400 t remains a live filing of unconfirmed status [36][37]. Every Indian ibuprofen maker in this note is a price-taker against that backdrop.
07
Feedstock reversal if the conflict eases
The only documented 2026 support for ibuprofen prices is isobutylbenzene and petrochemical cost under Middle East disruption [40][70]. If the conflict eases, the cost floor falls, the +4–7% quarterly firming reverses, and the cost-pass-through margins that make IOL and SMS the relative winners of §09 compress with it. Procurement Resource already read Q2 2026 Asia as “soft-to-stable… as upstream chemical costs declined” [42].

Data conflicts inside the evidence base
Solara’s FY26 result is a ₹7.41 cr loss in one source and a ₹17 cr profit in another [65][69], and its FY25 result flips sign the same way; its ibuprofen share is 20.9% in the annual report and “~30%” in a May 2025 write-up [67][68]. ChemAnalyst’s China series does not reconcile with its own percentages, and the trackers disagree on levels by ~40% [39][40][41]. IOL’s FY26 revenue appears as ₹2,319 cr and ₹2,340.3 cr [55]. Web summaries of the Chinese filings mis-scale units; only PDF-read figures are used here. Each conflict is shown rather than resolved, and each limits how precisely any conclusion above can be stated.

Shree Pushkar — a name mislabelled into the thesis
No exposure to the chain [77][78][79]. Any move in the stock on ibuprofen headlines is flow it did not earn and can lose as quickly; its real risks — a cluster trade in which it was not named, a capex programme pointed at fertilisers, and a Q2 FY27 print that is the whole H-acid case — are set out in the desk’s 8 September update and are unrelated to anything in this note.

Summary

VERDICT: THESIS AUDIT — THE PREMISE FAILS AS FRAMED; NOT A RECOMMENDATION

The claim that reached this desk — a European ibuprofen shortage caused by Chinese supply cuts, with five Indian names as beneficiaries — partly holds at its narrowest and fails as framed. Twenty-two finished-dose presentations are genuinely listed short in five national registers, with prognoses running to 31 March 2027 [3]. Everything above that fact was added by the telling. The registers attribute the entries to product-level manufacturing problems, say alternatives are available, and in the UK and France list nothing at all [3][4][5][6][7]. Ibuprofen is on no EU or German critical list and has not been mentioned in five sittings of Germany’s shortage board to June 2026 [8][10][12]. Some Italian entries date from 2022–2025 [4].

The China leg is refuted by the Chinese makers’ own audited reports. Shandong Xinhua’s analgesic API revenue fell 16.42% in H1 2026 with group profit down 33.35%, prices cut to defend share, and an outlook of weak demand and intense competition [28]. Hendi cancelled a 5,000 t expansion because the market was saturated and repeats that wording in its H1 2026 report [30][33]. The trackers describe Chinese plants at nameplate and prices firming 4–7% a quarter on feedstock costs from the Middle East conflict [39][40][41]. No 2026 evidence of a Chinese plant shutdown, inspection or export curb was found, and the two articles that launched the theme never claimed one [1][2].

The one real exposure finding is the map in §07. Four of the five names touch the chain and one does not. IOL is the volume leader and is deliberately shrinking ibuprofen from 82% to 57% of pharma revenue [53][54]. SMS is about 20% ibuprofen on a capex-led volume story and attributes the 2026 price rise to war-driven feedstock, not scarcity [70]. Solara’s ibuprofen loses money at a −12% to −23% EBITDA margin and is under a bankers’ review of “strategic options” that could end in its sale [61][63][64] — the opposite of a beneficiary. Vinati is upstream, with IBB at 10–12% of revenue and volumes down 20% for raw-material reasons [50]. Shree Pushkar has no exposure whatsoever — not one mention of ibuprofen, IBB or an API in a 274-page annual report [77] — and is in this note only because it was asked about.

The framework agrees with the audit, in an unusual way. The purest upstream play on ibuprofen volumes, the sole-source IBB maker, fails every technical check the system makes; if the market believed a European shortage were pulling Indian supply, the raw-material maker would show it first, and it shows the opposite. The two strongest tapes belong to names whose trends the desk’s own 8 September reports explain by other stories, and the one tape that did move on this theme belongs to the name that cannot profit from it.

What is actually happening is cost-push, and it is already in the reported quarter. Under cost-push the relative winners are the backward-integrated makers that pass feedstock through — IOL with its own IBB, SMS by its own account — Vinati gains on IBB price rather than volume, Solara cannot capture a price it sells below cost, and Shree Pushkar is unaffected. For the thesis proper to become true, a regulator would have to name ibuprofen, a Chinese maker would have to report an outage or a curb, Indian exports to the EU would have to rise, IOL or SMS would have to cite scarcity rather than cost, and Solara’s utilisation would have to climb. None of those is observed as of 11 September 2026. This note carries no rating; the readings it surfaces are the framework’s own and are not a recommendation on any of the five.
