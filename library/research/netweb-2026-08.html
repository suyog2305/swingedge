Netweb designs, manufactures and deploys high-end computing systems in India — supercomputing clusters, GPU-accelerated AI systems, private cloud and hyperconverged infrastructure, parallel-file-system storage and data-centre servers — under its own Tyrone hardware brand and Skylus.ai software stack. It describes itself as India's leading Indian-origin, Indian-owned and Indian-controlled OEM in the HCS space, and is one of a small number of firms worldwide holding an OEM design-and-manufacture partnership with NVIDIA for AI GPU systems.

Installed-base credentials are real rather than promotional: 600+ supercomputing systems, 7,000+ accelerator/GPU-based AI systems and 60+ private-cloud/HCI installations, with Netweb-built machines appearing 15 times across three entries in the global Top 500 supercomputer list. Manufacturing is concentrated at the Faridabad facility, commissioned in May 2024 under the PLI 2.0 IT-hardware scheme, which carries in-house PCB design and a surface-mount-technology (SMT) line for servers, storage and switches.

House view: Netweb is the only clean listed way to own India's sovereign-AI compute build-out at the system-manufacturing layer. It is also a hardware assembler whose reported growth is dominated by two named strategic orders, whose top-10 customers were 87.7% of Q1 FY27 revenue, and which trades at roughly 118x trailing earnings. Everything in this report follows from holding both of those facts at once.

03 —

Where Netweb Sits in the AI Compute Value Chain

Every AI cluster deployed in India flows through the same physical sequence. Netweb occupies the system design, integration and manufacturing stage — the layer that turns silicon into a working, racked, cooled, orchestrated cluster. It is a real and defensible position. It is also structurally not where the profit pool sits.

STAGE 01
Silicon & Memory
GPUs and accelerators, CPUs, HBM/DRAM, NAND — the scarce, allocated, price-setting inputs
NVIDIA · AMDIntel · Samsung
STAGE 02
Boards & Subsystems
PCB design and SMT assembly, power delivery, liquid/advanced cooling, chassis and interconnect
NETWEB (in-house SMT)Vertiv (cooling)
STAGE 03
System Design & Manufacture
GPU-system architecture, cluster middleware, integration, burn-in, qualification — the "Make in India" layer
NETWEB (Tyrone)Dell · HPE · SMCI
STAGE 04
Deployment & Operators
Sovereign-AI compute providers, government and academic HPC centres, enterprise on-prem clouds
NETWEB (software/services)Yotta · Neysa · E2E

Netweb straddles Stages 02–03 with a thin but real toe-hold in Stage 04 through Skylus.ai, cluster management and managed services. The commercial consequence is arithmetic: when an AI system is sold, the GPU is the overwhelming share of the bill of materials, it is bought in dollars from a supplier with pricing power, and it is passed through to the customer. What Netweb keeps is the integration, software, qualification and support layer.

That is precisely why revenue growth and gross-margin direction move in opposite directions in this business. FY26 revenue grew 90% while gross margin fell from 23.2% to 20.8%. The bigger the AI mix, the more of each rupee of revenue is somebody else's silicon. Management is candid about the mechanism, saying it prices to show value rather than to exploit scarcity, and that new orders carry price pass-through — a fair description of a business that is a margin-taker on inputs and a margin-defender on integration.

The Organic Growth Test

Headline Q1 FY27 revenue growth was +172.1%. Strip out the ~₹430 Cr of strategic-order execution management said was booked in the quarter and base-business revenue was roughly ₹390 Cr against ₹301 Cr in Q1 FY26 — about +30% YoY, a shade below the 35–40% organic growth management has guided to. That is not a bad number in absolute terms. It is a materially different number from 172%, and it is the number that matters for FY28 and beyond.

The structural risk inside the strength: the ₹2,184 Cr of strategic orders is finite and largely completes by Q3 FY27. At least one covering broker (Equirus, May 2026) explicitly models FY28E revenue of ₹3,260 Cr below FY27E of ₹3,898 Cr — a revenue decline — precisely because the strategic order runs off with nothing yet booked to replace it. Whether the ₹10,401 Cr pipeline converts in time is the entire FY28 debate.

The Policy Engine: IndiaAI, PLI 2.0 and NSM 2.0

Netweb's addressable market is being created substantially by policy rather than by an autonomous private capex cycle. That is both the opportunity and the concentration risk.

Dec 2025
National Supercomputing Mission 2.0 launched — the successor HPC tendering pipeline behind the AI wave

Four policy vectors converge on Netweb's P&L. IndiaAI Mission funds GPU capacity procurement and subsidises compute for startups and researchers to roughly $1 per GPU-hour, well below commercial cloud rates — it is the reason the ₹1,734 Cr Blackwell order existed at all. PLI 2.0 subsidises domestic IT-hardware manufacture and is the reason the Faridabad SMT line was built. NSM 2.0 re-opens the academic and research supercomputing tender pipeline that is Netweb's original franchise. And data-localisation and sovereign-AI policy creates a structural preference for an Indian-origin, Indian-controlled OEM in government, defence and PSU procurement.

An Honest Reading of the PLI Contribution

PLI is widely assumed to be Netweb's margin engine. The disclosed evidence says otherwise. Broker analysis of FY25 puts gross margin at 23.2% as reported versus 22.8% excluding PLI — roughly 40 basis points of benefit. PLI matters as a capex offset and as a qualification credential for government tenders; it is not what makes the business profitable, and its withdrawal or delay would not break the model. Investors should size the PLI narrative accordingly.

The flip side of policy-driven demand: ordering is lumpy, tender-cycle dependent and politically sequenced. Government and government-adjacent customers were roughly 42.5% of the customer split at the last comparable disclosure. A budget reprioritisation or a slow empanelment round does not reduce demand permanently — it just moves several hundred crore of revenue out of a fiscal year, which for a stock at these multiples is the same thing.

Is the 13–15% EBITDA Margin Real?

This is the question that decides the multiple, and it deserves a direct answer rather than a hedge. Netweb's EBITDA margin genuinely exceeds what Dell or Supermicro earn at the operating line, and the explanation is mostly structural rather than magical. Employee cost was ~5.4% of revenue in FY25 and is modelled below 4% as revenue scales; there is no global sales force, no worldwide service network, no R&D budget of Dell's magnitude. Management's own explanation — that the integrated full-stack approach of designing, manufacturing and supplying both software and hardware commands better margins than competitors who bundle third-party products — is credible for the HPC and private-cloud franchise, where the software and cluster middleware genuinely differentiate.

What is not yet proven is that the margin survives an AI-dominated mix at much larger scale. Two disclosed facts push against it: strategic AI orders carry margins roughly 150–200 basis points lower at the PBT level than base business, on management's own guidance; and gross margin has already fallen from 23.2% (FY25) to 20.8% (FY26) as AI mix rose, with broker models putting raw material at 80–81% of revenue in FY26–FY27 against 75–77% historically. The 13–14% EBITDA guidance is being defended by operating leverage on a fixed cost base, not by pricing power on the bill of materials.

The FY23–FY26 revenue CAGR of roughly 70% is the honest headline number, and it is the reason the market is willing to underwrite a very large multiple.

Three things are happening at once. Receivables improved — 86 to 78 days — which is genuinely good and unusual for a business selling into government-linked customers. Inventory rose sharply — 86 to 110 days — which management frames as a deliberate raw-material build to secure GPU and memory supply and hedge an execution timeline that has stretched from 8–12 weeks to 16–20 weeks. And the combination flipped the balance sheet from net cash at March to net debt of roughly ₹200 Cr (0.25x equity, 0.41x annualised EBITDA) by June.

That flip is the honest explanation for the ₹1,200 Cr QIP completed on 20 August 2026 at ₹4,790 per share, taken up by Goldman Sachs Asset Management, Nomura, Amundi and domestic mutual funds. Management had earlier characterised the enabling resolution as being for working capital "if needed"; the Q1 balance sheet shows it was needed. Pro-forma, the company swings to roughly ₹1,000 Cr of net cash, book value per share rises to about ₹334 and P/B falls from roughly 38x to about 16x — a genuine de-risking, at the cost of 4.2% dilution.

The right way to read the QIP: not as a growth-capex raise. Management has said explicitly that the business is "capability-driven, not capacity-driven," with facilities already able to sustain turnover above ₹3,000 Cr and no major capacity capex anticipated. The ₹1,200 Cr is balance-sheet ammunition to fund inventory and receivables for larger, longer-cycle AI orders — which tells you the working-capital intensity of the AI mix is higher than the HPC business it is replacing.

Credit and Governance Markers

  • CRISIL upgrade: Long-term rating raised to CRISIL A+/Stable from A/Stable, short-term reaffirmed at A1, with total bank facilities enhanced from ₹260 Cr to ₹700 Cr — a meaningful expansion of committed liquidity ahead of larger order execution.
  • Promoter selling: The four promoters sold 20,79,935 shares (~3.65%) in open-market transactions, taking collective holding from 70.64% to 66.98%. Control is untouched, but selling into a re-rating deserves noting.
  • Customer concentration is worsening, not improving: Top-10 customers were 57.8% of revenue in FY23, 65.8% in FY25, 76.4% in FY26 and 87.7% in Q1 FY27. Repeat-customer revenue of 98.9% and average top-10 tenure of 7.68 years mitigate this — these are long relationships, not one-night stands — but the trend line is unambiguous.
  • FX and input exposure: GPUs and memory are USD-denominated imports. FY26 saw MTM losses on payables offset by hedging gains booked in other income, and Q4 FY26 PAT came in below one broker's estimate specifically on forex losses. Memory and chip price inflation is a live 2026 issue that management says it plans around rather than reacts to.
09 —

Growth Drivers & Management Guidance

What Management Has Committed To

  • Organic revenue growth of 35–40% over the next couple of years — explicitly excluding strategic-order revenue. This is the number to hold management to, and Q1 FY27's implied ~30% organic growth is marginally short of it.
  • FY27 EBITDA margin of 13–14%, reaffirmed at the Q1 FY27 call with margins described as "well within" the guided range. Q1 delivered 14.7%.
  • Strategic order execution completing across Q1–Q3 FY27, with roughly ₹430 Cr booked in Q1 and ~₹1,190 Cr remaining.
  • Pipeline conversion of ~60% over 18–24 months on the ₹10,401 Cr bid book — the arithmetic that has to work for FY28 to avoid the step-down.
  • No major capacity capex — the Faridabad plant is stated to support turnover above ₹3,000 Cr, so incremental revenue should be largely working-capital funded rather than capex funded.

Structural Demand Drivers

  • Sovereign AI compute: IndiaAI Mission empanelment rounds, subsidised common-compute capacity and a policy preference for indigenous, in-country compute infrastructure.
  • NVIDIA OEM partnership: Netweb is one of a select set of OEMs globally designing and manufacturing NVIDIA-based AI GPU systems, with Blackwell/GB200-class platforms, an AI edge system, and a Vertiv collaboration for advanced cooling.
  • NSM 2.0 and academic HPC: The original franchise — IITs, IISc, IIIT, IISER, C-DAC, ISRO, Ministry of Defence and the Indian Army — reopening for a fresh procurement cycle.
  • Enterprise on-prem repatriation: Private cloud and HCI grew 28% in FY26 with customers including Zoho, Samsung, Infosys, Yotta, Neysa and Graviton Research Capital — a non-government, non-AI leg that grows steadily and carries better working-capital characteristics.
  • Software as margin defence: Skylus.ai, cluster middleware and managed services are small today but are the only structural answer to gross-margin compression from rising GPU content.
The guidance is coherent and management has not over-promised — 13–14% EBITDA has been delivered, and they have been careful to separate organic growth from strategic-order revenue. The gap between guidance and price is not a credibility problem. It is that the market appears to be capitalising the strategic-order-inflated FY27 revenue base as though it were the new normal.

Key Risk Factors

01
Valuation / Multiple Risk
HIGH
~118x TTM and ~85x our base FY27E EPS. Both retrievable broker targets (₹3,725 and ₹4,110) sit 20–30% below the market and consensus implies ~0–2% upside. A stock priced this way has no cushion for an ordinary miss, and India's electronics complex has already shown 20–30% drawdowns on lesser news.
02
Strategic-Order Cliff
HIGH
₹2,184 Cr of named strategic AI orders substantially complete by Q3 FY27. Equirus explicitly models FY28E revenue (₹3,260 Cr) below FY27E (₹3,898 Cr). Unless the ₹10,401 Cr pipeline converts on schedule, FY28 is an earnings-decline year against a 90x multiple.
03
Customer Concentration
HIGH
Top-10 customers rose from 57.8% (FY23) to 65.8% (FY25) to 76.4% (FY26) to 87.7% of Q1 FY27 revenue. Long tenure (7.68 years average) and 98.9% repeat revenue soften it, but a single deferred government tender now moves a full quarter.
04
Gross-Margin Compression
MED–HIGH
Gross margin fell 240 bps to 20.8% in FY26 as AI mix rose, with raw material modelled at 80–81% of revenue versus 75–77% historically. Strategic orders carry 150–200 bps lower PBT margin. The GPU is the product; the assembler keeps the integration layer.
05
Working Capital & Inventory
MED–HIGH
Inventory days rose 86 → 110 and the balance sheet flipped from net cash at Mar-26 to ~₹200 Cr net debt by Jun-26, prompting the ₹1,200 Cr QIP. Execution timelines stretched from 8–12 to 16–20 weeks. Larger AI orders are structurally more working-capital hungry than the HPC business they replace.
06
GPU Allocation, Memory Inflation & FX
MEDIUM
Revenue is gated by NVIDIA/AMD allocation, and inputs are USD-denominated into a depreciating rupee. FY26 carried MTM losses on payables offset by hedging gains; Q4 FY26 PAT missed one broker's estimate on forex. Memory and chip price inflation is a live 2026 pressure.
07
Competitive Entry
MEDIUM
Dell, HPE and Supermicro compete directly for Indian AI/HPC tenders; domestically, Dixon and other EMS players could localise server assembly under the same PLI framework. Netweb's moat is design IP, software and government qualification — not manufacturing scale, which is replicable.
08
Policy Dependency & Promoter Selling
LOW–MED
Demand is heavily policy-sequenced (IndiaAI, NSM 2.0, PLI 2.0); a reprioritised budget shifts hundreds of crore between fiscal years. Separately, promoters sold ~3.65% (holding 70.64% → 66.98%) and the QIP diluted a further 4.2% — control is safe, but insider supply into a re-rating is worth watching.

Investment Summary & Recommendation

Netweb is the listed domestic proxy for India's sovereign-AI compute build-out — the only Indian-origin, Indian-controlled full-stack OEM designing and manufacturing NVIDIA-based AI systems at scale, with a fifteen-year HPC franchise underneath, 90% FY26 revenue growth, 32.8% ROE, 48% Q1 FY27 ROCE and a ₹10,401 Cr bid pipeline. It is also a hardware assembler whose gross margin fell 240 bps as AI mix rose, whose top-10 customers are 87.7% of revenue, whose balance sheet needed a ₹1,200 Cr QIP after inventory days went to 110, and whose FY27 revenue is inflated by ₹2,184 Cr of finite strategic orders that largely complete by Q3 FY27. At ~118x trailing and ~85x our base FY27E — with both covering brokers 20–30% below the market and at least one modelling an FY28 revenue decline — the AI narrative is not merely priced in, it is priced through. We rate NETWEB Reduce / Avoid at CMP and would begin engaging near ₹4,000, meaningfully below ₹3,500.

The Three Reasons to Own NETWEB

  • 1. A genuine, non-replicable position: One of a select set of OEMs worldwide with an NVIDIA design-and-manufacture partnership, 600+ supercomputers installed, 15 Top-500 listings, in-house PCB/SMT under PLI 2.0, and an owned software stack. Not a trading house with a badge — a designer and manufacturer.
  • 2. Policy demand that is funded, not aspirational: ₹10,300 Cr IndiaAI outlay, 38,000+ GPUs empanelled, ₹17,000 Cr PLI 2.0, NSM 2.0 launched. The ₹1,734 Cr Blackwell order is proof the money converts into purchase orders for this specific company.
  • 3. Financial quality that is rare in Indian manufacturing: FY26 revenue +90%, PAT +81%, ROE 32.8%, ROCE 48% annualised in Q1 FY27, net cash pro-forma post-QIP, CRISIL upgraded to A+ with bank lines tripled to ₹700 Cr, and a Q1 FY27 EBITDA margin recovery to 14.7%.
And the reason not to buy it today: every one of those three strengths is already in the price, and then some. The stock is up roughly 74% year-to-date and 150%+ over twelve months. Owning the business and owning the stock at ₹5,403 are different decisions.
