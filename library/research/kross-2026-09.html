02 —

The Business — What Kross Actually Makes and Sells

Kross sells into two end-markets from one manufacturing base: medium & heavy commercial vehicles and tractors. Within those, the product line splits into the trailer axle-and-suspension systems the company is best known for, and a wider basket of forged, cast and machined driveline parts — axle shafts, companion flanges, differential spiders, bevel gears, planet carriers — that go into the OEM supply chain rather than the aftermarket trailer-fabrication channel [4]. The RHP-era description is explicit that customers include both large OEMs and their tier-one suppliers and domestic dealers, fabricators and trailer-axle/suspension manufacturers directly [3][4] — two different sales motions under one roof. The RHP and DRHP themselves are named in this report’s source list as attempted primary sources [3]; the direct PDF fetch was blocked (see §13), so the detail in this section is carried at one remove, via secondary summaries that this report cross-checked against each other where more than one existed.

Manufacturing footprintFive facilities, all in Jamshedpur, Jharkhand — forging, precision machining and heat-treatment lines including forging presses, high-pressure moulding and robotic welding [4]
Installed capacityApproximately 60,000 trailer-axle-and-suspension assemblies per annum on a secondary summary of the RHP [4], consistent with a separately cited run-rate of 4,500–5,000 axles a month
Q1 FY27 capacity utilisationForging 65%, machining 72%, casting 75% [8] — the headroom the current capex programme (§06) is being built against
Q1 FY27 volumes~9,500 axle units and ~3,200–3,300 suspension units produced in the quarter, axle-and-suspension volumes up ~30% year on year [8]

Who actually buys this, and how concentrated is it

Two OEM names recur across sourcing for this report: Ashok Leyland, and a Tata Group entity — one secondary summary names it as Tata Motors (the M&HCV OEM itself), another names “Tata International” (a Tata trading/export house) as the marquee client [4][8]. This report could not resolve which is the more accurate description of the direct commercial relationship from a primary disclosure, and shows both rather than silently picking one — the kind of name confusion this desk checks for deliberately.

Concentration is real and, on the numbers available, improving but still high. The RHP-era record states the top five customers contributed more than 66% of revenue in each of the previous three fiscal years, with the top three alone close to half of revenue [4]. A secondary analysis of the Q1 FY27 investor presentation puts the top-five figure at 59.47% of revenue for that quarter [8]. Read together, that is a company visibly working to broaden its customer base — and one that is still, on the latest disclosed quarter, getting three-fifths of its revenue from five counterparties. The RHP itself names the flip side of this structure as risk factors: no long-term customer contracts, customer pricing pressure, raw-material and labour cost volatility, and counterparty credit risk from OEM/dealer payment delays [4] — all standard for a component supplier of this kind, and all worth holding in mind when §07’s working-capital numbers are read.

The point this report was asked to check

The stock’s entire trading history, across roughly 24 months, has been a channel between about ₹150 and ₹271 — and both boundaries of that channel were effectively set within its first seven months of trading. The all-time high is not a level the stock climbed to after two years of compounding; it is an intraday print from the first day it ever traded, which the market has spent most of the two years since failing to hold above and is only now, again, approaching. The all-time low arrived seven months later, in the April 2025 drawdown. Every “52-week high,” “up 70% from the low” and “within 5.5% of the all-time high” framing this stock generates — including in this report’s own masthead and §08 — describes movement within a range the stock has never left, not a breakout into new territory. That does not make the current move meaningless; a stock finally reclaiming a two-year-old high after a real improvement in the underlying numbers is a legitimate technical event. But it is a different event from a stock making a genuine new high on a business that has grown for two years, and the two should not be described the same way.

No stock split, bonus issue or other corporate action affecting the share count was found in the record; the face value has remained ₹5 since listing, so the price series above is directly comparable across its full history without adjustment.

What the trend actually says

Growth was flat for two years and then inflected. Revenue went essentially nowhere between FY24 and FY25, grew a modest 8.5% in FY26, and then accelerated sharply — Q4 FY26 grew 21.6% year on year and Q1 FY27 grew 32.3% year on year [7][8]. Two consecutive quarters of double-digit acceleration after two flat years is the growth leg of the current re-rating, and it is real.

The margin has not inflected in the same way — it has been rangebound. Across all nine quarters above, operating margin has moved between roughly 11% and 15% with no quarter yet clearing 15%. India Ratings’ own June 2026 rationale describes exactly this pattern as “rangebound EBITDA margins over FY24–FY26” [13]. Q4 FY26’s 14.9% was the best quarter on record; Q1 FY27’s 12.2% gave back most of that improvement in a single quarter, with PAT margin falling from the low-to-mid teens implied by Q4 FY26 to 7.22%. Management’s own explanation is a mix of seasonal monsoon demand moderation in May–June and higher depreciation from the capex programme now underway [8][9] — both plausible, and neither yet confirmed by a second data point. That confirmation is a Q2 FY27 question, not something this report can resolve from Q1 alone.

The diversification narrative, and how far along it actually is

Management is explicit about two glide paths: exports from ~4.5% of revenue toward ~8% within two years, citing secured orders from a Sweden-based company and a European tier-one player [8]; and tractor components from ~9–10% of revenue toward 15% over the next two years [8][11]. Tractor volumes grew roughly 40% year on year in Q1 FY27 [8], consistent with the sector-wide tractor demand tailwind discussed in §09. Both targets describe real diversification in direction; both also describe a company that is, on the most recent disclosed quarter, still around 83–88% dependent on domestic M&HCV-and-trailer revenue by any reasonable reading of the mix above — broadly consistent with the RHP-era FY24 split of 88.87% M&HCV, 9.02% farm equipment, 2.11% other [4]. This is the same pattern this desk has already documented at both of Kross’s closer listed peers: Ramkrishna Forgings’ railway share moving from 1.7% to 6% of revenue, and Happy Forgings’ push into non-auto industrial demand [20]. In every case the direction is genuine and the current scale is still small. Kross is earlier in this process than either peer, not further along.

Capacity Expansion and the Capex Programme

Five capital projects are running concurrently as this report is written, which is unusual for a company of this size and is the central operational fact behind both the bull and bear cases.

Axle-beam extrusion plantIndia’s first of its kind, per the company. ₹25 Cr; capacity 7,500 units/month; shifts axle-beam manufacture from a fabricated (welded) to an extruded design — lower material cost, lighter weight, no weld joint. Commercial production guided for August 2026; margin benefit expected once utilisation clears roughly 50% [8]. This report could not confirm whether commercial production actually started on schedule.

As of Q1 FY27: construction shed complete, foundation nearing ready, the piercing mill received, sizing/straightening mills in transit [8].
Foundry — high-pressure moulding lineWill double existing casting capacity; completion guided for September 2026 — this month, as this report is written [8].
Robotic forging line (axle shafts)Commissioning guided for September 2026; an additional 1,000-tonne forging press on order [8].
Tipping jacks for trailersNew product line for dumper/tip trailers; installed capacity 800 kits/month against Q1 production of only 220–226 kits; utilisation guided to reach 65–70% by Q4 FY27; margin potential cited around 15% [8].

Two of these five milestones fall inside the current month. Neither this report nor the sources it reached could confirm whether the foundry line or the robotic forging line has actually been commissioned on schedule as of 23 September 2026 — both are live, checkable items rather than settled ones, and both are carried into §13’s monitor list rather than assumed complete.

How this is being funded, and the counter-evidence

Management’s own framing is debt plus internal accruals [8]. Screener’s balance-sheet series shows why that matters: free cash flow turned negative in FY26, at roughly −₹72 Cr, despite a positive and growing PAT, and working-capital days rose from 86.9 to 124 over the same window [2] — a lengthening cash-conversion cycle sitting underneath a capex programme that is about to get heavier, not lighter, in FY27. Borrowings have already moved from ₹34 Cr (FY25) to ₹54 Cr (FY26) as the post-IPO deleveraging (§07) begins to reverse [2]. None of this is disqualifying on its own — India Ratings explicitly built its June 2026 affirmation around a company investing in capacity from a deleveraged starting position [13] — but it does mean the capex is being funded increasingly from the balance sheet rather than purely from the business’s own cash generation, at the same time the reported margin (§04) has just stepped down.

A live, unresolved capital raise

A preferential issue — 15,00,000 equity shares and 15,00,000 warrants, both at ₹212, together worth up to ₹63.6 Cr — was put to a postal ballot voting from 1 to 30 September 2026. As reported, the warrants go to promoters Sumeet Rai and Kunal Rai, with the equity shares to four non-promoter allottees; the stated purpose is capacity expansion, automation and working capital [12]. The outcome was not yet known at the time this report was written — the vote closes at the end of this month. Fully converted, the two tranches together are roughly 4.6% dilution against the current ~6.45 Cr-share base (derived). The ₹212 issue price sits below the current market price, which means the warrants are already in the money at today’s CMP — a fact that flatters the “promoter confidence” reading of the raise less than it would if the stock were trading below ₹212.

Credit rating

India Ratings & Research affirmed Kross’s long-term rating at IND A / Stable and short-term rating at IND A1 on 2 June 2026 [13]. The rationale cites steady revenue and resilient, rangebound EBITDA margins over FY24–FY26 (§04), a deleveraged post-IPO balance sheet and healthy cash balance, and capacity/product investment expected to benefit the top line from FY27 — with the deleveraged base specifically cited as support in the event of an auto-sector downturn [13]. Separately, CARE Ratings withdrew its ratings on Kross’s bank facilities effective around 4 December 2025, after the company surrendered those facilities and cleared the outstanding amounts [14] — consistent with the IPO-funded debt paydown rather than a credit event, but worth stating plainly since a rating withdrawal can otherwise look alarming out of context.

Promoter holding, and a reconciliation worth showing

The June 2026 shareholding pattern shows promoters at 68.6% (Sudhir Rai individually 37.8%), DIIs 6.1%, FIIs 2.5% and public shareholders 22.9%, with no promoter pledge [2]. The scan this report otherwise relies on carries a differently-labelled holding field that reads 77.13% [1] — on its face a contradiction. Working through the arithmetic: the scan’s own free-float count is 1,47,51,150 shares against a total share count of roughly 6.45 Cr derived from CMP and market cap [1], which is 22.87% — matching the 22.9% public figure above to within a rounding point, and 100% minus that free float is 77.13%, matching the scan’s field exactly. In other words, the raw counts fully reconcile; the scan’s field is simply not measuring “public holding” the way the exchange shareholding pattern uses that term. This report relies on the shareholding-pattern figures (68.6% / 6.1% / 2.5% / 22.9%) throughout rather than the scan’s differently-defined field.

09 —

What’s Actually Driving the Re-Rating

Four candidate explanations were checked against dates. One holds up well, two are real but modest, and one is simply absent.

The Q1 FY27 print — and the timing lines up almost exactly

The board approved Q1 FY27 results on 24 July 2026, with an earnings call on 27 July [7]. The provider’s Stage 2 “Continues Trend” base is dated from 25 July 2026 [1] — the day immediately after the board meeting. That is about as close as this kind of dated cross-check ever gets without an exact timestamped filing, and it is the most defensible single explanation this report can offer: a best-ever Q1 print (revenue +32.3%, EBITDA +39.5% year on year) landed on a stock already inside a basing pattern, and the market re-rated it from there. The same print, on a sequential basis, also showed the margin compression covered in §04 — a detail that gets less attention in the momentum coverage than the year-on-year headline does.

GST rationalisation — a real tailwind, but not a September event

Management repeatedly cites “GST rationalisation benefits” as a demand driver [8]. The underlying reform — tractors cut from 12% to 5%, trucks and buses from 28% to 18%, a uniform 18% on auto parts — took effect on 22 September 2025 [21], a full year before this report and roughly nine months before the Q1 FY27 quarter it is credited with helping. This is a standing structural tailwind that management continues to reference each quarter, not a new trigger for the recent share-price move — the kind of timing check this desk applies deliberately, since a demand driver that is a year old cannot be the reason a stock moved in the last eight weeks, even where it is a genuine and ongoing support to volumes.

PARIVARTAN — real, but regional and small relative to the company’s footprint

The PARIVARTAN scheme — Cabinet-approved 3 June 2026, launched 14 July 2026 — incentivises scrapping and replacing old commercial vehicles, with a target of scaling to 25,000 registrations by October 2026 toward an eventual scope of roughly 2.07 lakh vehicles and a ₹9,585 Cr outlay [22]. It is genuinely a new-CV demand driver inside the Q1 FY27/Q2 FY27 window. It is also specific to Delhi-NCR, not a national programme [22] — a geographically narrow tailwind for a Jharkhand-based national supplier, worth citing as one supportive factor among several rather than as a standalone explanation for a national re-rating.

What this report could not find

No index-inclusion event, no sell-side coverage initiation, and no institutional bulk or block deal were found in any source this report reached that would explain the move. The most honest summary is that a genuinely strong, best-ever Q1 print landed on a stock already inside a technical base, and the subsequent advance has been trend-following momentum layered on top of that one data point — reinforced by, rather than caused by, a standing GST tailwind and a regional scrappage scheme neither of which is new news. Read against §03, part of what is being described as a “surge toward all-time highs” is also, mechanically, a two-year-old stock finally reapproaching where it opened for trading.

Valuation

Kross’s own current multiples, DMA distances and returns are generated into §08 and are not repeated here. What this section can add is how much of the current multiple is new evidence versus repriced sentiment, and how Kross compares with its closest listed peers.

The multiple has moved more than the earnings have

A result-day analysis published around 28 July 2026 — the week of the Q1 FY27 print — put Kross’s trailing earnings multiple at 24.02x, describing it as a 35% discount to a 37x sector average [9]. The scan behind this report’s masthead puts the same trailing-earnings multiple at 28.6x as of 22 September 2026 [1]. The trailing twelve months of earnings behind both figures are the same — Q1 FY27 is the only new quarter reported in that window, and it is already in both calculations. Roughly eight weeks of multiple expansion, on an unchanged earnings base, is sentiment re-pricing the same numbers rather than new numbers arriving. That does not make 28.6x expensive in absolute terms — it is still below the 37x sector figure cited in July — but it does mean the cushion behind the current price is thinner than it was when the Q1 print first landed.

Scenarios

Bull
Q2 FY27 shows EBITDA margin recovering back toward the 14–15% band as the seasonal monsoon effect passes and the axle-beam extrusion plant’s ramp starts contributing; the seamless-tube backward-integration plant stays on schedule for Q4 FY27 commissioning; export share moves visibly toward the ~8% target and tractor share toward 15%, both genuinely reducing OEM concentration; the pending postal-ballot raise clears and reads as funding growth rather than distress; the stock decisively clears ₹271 for the first time in its listed history on confirmed rather than hoped-for margin recovery, and the multiple re-rates further on real evidence.
Base
Q2 FY27 margin lands between Q1's 12.2% and Q4 FY26's 14.9%, consistent with a business whose margin has been genuinely rangebound for three years rather than trending in either direction. Revenue growth continues at a healthy pace on CV/tractor volumes and the standing GST-linked tailwind, the five concurrent capex projects proceed roughly on their guided timelines with debt continuing to rise moderately, and the stock spends time consolidating inside the same ₹150–271 channel it has occupied since listing while FY27's full-year numbers accumulate enough evidence to settle the margin question either way.
Bear
Q1's margin compression turns out to mark the start of a trend rather than a seasonal dip, as ramp-up costs on five simultaneous new lines and rising input costs outpace the revenue growth. The CV/tractor cycle — the dominant driver for all three listed forging peers in §10 — turns while Kross is mid-way through its heaviest capex programme to date, increasingly funded by debt against already-negative free cash flow (§06). The pending warrant/equity issue prices further dilution into a weaker print. ₹271 caps the stock for a third time in two years, and the 28.6x multiple compresses back toward the ~24x this stock traded at on the Q1 print itself, purely by giving back the recent re-rating.
12 —

Key Risks

01
The multiple has already re-rated ahead of confirmation
Trailing earnings multiple moved from roughly 24x (late July, on the Q1 print itself) to 28.6x (22 September) on the same trailing-twelve-month earnings base — sentiment re-pricing, not new numbers. See §10.
02
The just-reported quarter’s margin compression is unresolved
EBITDA margin fell to 12.2% in Q1 FY27 from a record 14.9% in Q4 FY26, and PAT margin to 7.22%. Management attributes this to seasonality and capex-linked depreciation; neither explanation has a second data point behind it yet. Only Q2 FY27 can confirm or deny it. See §04.
03
“Near all-time high” describes a two-year-old listing-day print, not a breakout
₹271 traces to 16 September 2024, the stock’s first trading day. Across roughly 24 months of trading the stock’s full range has been about ₹150–271; it has spent much of that time below its own opening-day levels. See §03.
04
Customer and OEM concentration remains high
Top-five customers were above 66% of revenue in each of FY22–FY24 and 59.47% in Q1 FY27. Named OEM relationships are few, the RHP itself flags an absence of long-term contracts, and this report could not even confirm from primary sourcing whether the marquee Tata relationship is with Tata Motors or Tata International. See §02.
05
Heavy concurrent capex, funded increasingly by debt against negative free cash flow
Five capital projects are running at once (§06). FY26 free cash flow was roughly −₹72 Cr despite positive PAT, working-capital days rose from 86.9 to 124, and borrowings have already risen from ₹34 Cr (FY25) to ₹54 Cr (FY26) as post-IPO deleveraging reverses.
06
Least margin cushion of its immediate peer set
Kross’s ~12–13% EBITDA margin sits well below Happy Forgings' ~30–31% and Ramkrishna Forgings' post-recovery ~18%. The smallest, thinnest-margin name in the comparison has the least room to absorb a CV/tractor downturn. See §10.
07
A dilutive capital raise is pending as this report is written
15 lakh equity shares plus 15 lakh warrants at ₹212 (up to ₹63.6 Cr) went to a postal ballot voting 1–30 September 2026. The outcome was unknown at the time of writing; full conversion would dilute the current share base by roughly 4.6% (derived). See §06.
08
Working-capital deterioration and payment-delay exposure
Working-capital days rose from 86.9 to 124 (Screener). The RHP itself names counterparty credit risk from OEM/dealer payment delays as a risk factor — consistent with the pattern in the numbers rather than a new concern.
09
Segment disclosure does not reconcile cleanly across quarters
A five-way Q1 FY27 product/export split and a two-way Q4 FY26 split use different apparent bases and do not add up against each other. The underlying investor-presentation PDFs could not be fetched directly to resolve this. See §05.
10
Information gaps this report could not close
Order-book value, related-party-transaction detail, and the business and scale of the BSPL subsidiary were not obtainable from any source this report reached. No dividend has been paid despite consistent profitability. See §07.
11
Surveillance status not independently verified beyond the scan
The 22 September 2026 scan shows no ASM/GSM/ESM flag, but this report could not independently retrieve the NSE/BSE surveillance lists to confirm that directly — the same access limitation this desk has documented on other names.
12
No single discrete catalyst was found behind the move
No index inclusion, coverage initiation, or institutional bulk/block deal was found. The advance looks like a strong Q1 print reinforced by trend-following momentum rather than one identifiable event — which means it can unwind on sentiment as readily as it built. See §09.

Summary

RATING: HOLD AT CMP — RE-RATED AHEAD OF THE MARGIN PROOF

Kross is a real, thirty-five-year-old manufacturer of safety-critical forged and machined components, not a story built on narrative alone. Revenue grew 32.3% year on year in its best-ever Q1, EBITDA 39.5%, and the company is mid-way through five concurrent capacity projects — an axle-beam extrusion plant, a ₹167 Cr seamless-tube backward-integration line, a foundry doubling, a robotic forging line and a new tipping-jack product — funded from a deleveraged, India Ratings IND A/Stable balance sheet. None of that is invented, and none of it should be dismissed.

But the same quarter that produced the headline growth also produced a sequential EBITDA-margin decline to 12.2%, inside a band the company has occupied without a clear upward trend for three full years (§04). The 28.6x trailing multiple the stock now carries is not new evidence of that margin resolving — it is roughly eight weeks of sentiment re-pricing the identical trailing earnings that supported a 24x multiple when the Q1 results first landed (§10). And the “near all-time high” the framework rightly rewards (§08) is, on the record established in §03, a return toward a price the stock first touched on its own listing day two years ago, not a breakout into genuinely new territory.

Customer concentration is improving but still high (§02), free cash flow turned negative in FY26 against a lengthening working-capital cycle (§06), and a dilutive preferential issue was awaiting a shareholder vote, unresolved, as this report was being written (§06). None of these is disqualifying on its own, and several — the deleveraged balance sheet, the credit-rating affirmation, the genuine (if early) export and tractor diversification — are constructive. But together they describe a business whose growth story is ahead of its margin proof, at a price that has already moved to reflect the growth story.

Hold at CMP is the honest rating for a company where the trend-following framework and the headline growth numbers agree completely, and the operating-margin evidence underneath both has not yet caught up. The case for paying today for a margin recovery that has not happened, or for dismissing a business with a deleveraging balance sheet and a genuinely improving order profile, are both thinner than the price action alone would suggest.
