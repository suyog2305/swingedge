02 —

The Turnaround Is Genuine

HFCL is not a growth story that got faster. It is a company that was losing money one year ago and is now highly profitable, and the distinction matters for how you read every number in this report.

Q1 (quarter to June)Q1 FY26Q1 FY27Change
Revenue₹871.02 Cr₹1,914.98 Cr+119.9%
EBITDA₹42.93 Cr₹445.27 Cr+937%
PAT−₹29.3 Cr (loss)₹245.64 Crloss → profit

This is why the screener shows a nonsensical −938% “profit growth” figure for HFCL: percentage change from a negative base is not meaningful. The underlying fact is a swing from a ₹29 crore loss to a ₹246 crore profit.

EBITDA rose from ₹42.93 crore to ₹445.27 crore — a tenfold increase on a doubling of revenue. That is operating leverage of an unusual order, and it tells you the business was running well below the level at which its fixed cost base breaks even. The turnaround is not an accounting effect; it is volume filling an under-utilised plant.

Management attributes the swing to demand from hyperscale data centres, export growth and economies of scale from higher production. All three are checkable in the segment data, and all three are consistent with what the rest of the industry is reporting.

04 —

The $1.1 Billion Contract — Read This Carefully

A multi-year global optical fibre cable supply contract valued at approximately USD 1.1 billion (₹10,159 crore) sits inside that order book. Alongside it, exports account for roughly ₹12,250 crore of the total, or 58%.

This is transformative if it executes, and it deserves three specific caveats that a buyer should hold consciously:

  • It is multi-year. ₹10,159 crore spread across several years is a materially smaller annual number than the headline suggests. Against FY26 revenue of roughly ₹5,300 crore, it is large; against the ₹26,665 crore book it is 38% concentrated in a single contract.
  • Single-contract concentration is a two-sided fact. One contract worth 38% of the book means one counterparty, one set of delivery schedules, and one renegotiation risk carrying an outsized share of the visibility.
  • Export contracts carry currency and logistics exposure that domestic orders do not, and they price in a global OFC market where capacity has historically been added quickly when margins are good.

None of this makes the contract less real. It makes the difference between the order book and delivered revenue the thing to watch each quarter, rather than the headline value.

Treat the December 2026 capacity date as the gating item on FY28 revenue, not FY27.

Defence — The Optionality, Not the Thesis

Defence revenueFigureStatus
FY26 actual₹77 Crreported
FY27₹400 Crmanagement target
FY28₹1,200 Crmanagement target
FY29₹5,000 Crmanagement target

Only the FY26 figure of ₹77 Cr is an actual. The FY27–FY29 numbers are management targets, not orders, and the current defence order book stands at approximately ₹300 Cr.

Be honest about the arithmetic here. A path from ₹77 crore to ₹5,000 crore in three years is a 65-fold increase, against a defence order book today of ₹300 crore. Defence procurement in India runs on multi-year qualification, trials and tender cycles; a target of that shape requires order wins that have not yet happened.

The company did secure a ₹102 crore Indian Army order through a subsidiary, which is a real and encouraging data point. Treat defence as free optionality on a story that already works on fibre. If you find yourself paying for the FY29 number, the position is being underwritten on a target rather than a book.

The trailing P/E of 67× is misleading in both directions. It is calculated on twelve months that include the loss-making quarters, so it overstates the multiple on current earnings power. Equally, annualising a single ₹246 crore quarter to claim the stock trades on ~39× understates the risk that the quarter is not repeatable. Neither number is the truth; the honest position is that HFCL does not yet have enough profitable quarters for a trailing multiple to mean anything.

08 —

Exports

Export sales were ₹1,063.30 crore in Q1 FY27 — 55.5% of total revenue, and exports account for roughly 58% of the order book.

HFCL has structurally shifted from a domestically-driven telecom supplier into an export-led manufacturer within roughly a year. That is the real change in the business, and it is more important than any single contract. It also means the revenue line is now exposed to global OFC demand and pricing rather than to Indian telecom capex — a different, and arguably better-diversified, risk, but a new one that has not been observed through a downcycle.

Guidance

FY27 revenue growth — earlier guidance~20%
FY27 revenue growth — raised to40% and above

Management has doubled its own growth guidance mid-year. That is the strongest signal in the report — companies raise guidance when the order book is converting faster than they modelled, and it is the opposite of the Jyoti CNC situation where guidance has run ahead of delivery.

The counterweight: a guidance raise from 20% to 40% after a quarter that grew 120% means management itself expects the growth rate to decelerate sharply across the remaining three quarters. That is normal and honest, but it is worth internalising before Q2 lands.

Scenarios

Bull
The order book converts at the pace the raised 40%+ guidance implies. Capacity reaches 33.9 million fkm by December, unlocking FY28. The $1.1bn contract delivers on schedule and defence begins converting toward the ₹400 Cr FY27 target. At 1.44× the order book, this is the name in your book with the most room if execution holds.
Base
Growth decelerates from 120% toward the guided 40%, which is what management is already signalling. Margins normalise below the exceptional Q1 level as the mix shifts. The business is permanently better than it was — export-led, profitable, with real visibility — and the stock consolidates the 319% move while earnings accumulate a track record.
Bear
Q1 FY27 proves to be a peak quarter rather than a new base. Global OFC pricing softens as capacity is added industry-wide, the $1.1bn contract slips or is renegotiated, and the defence targets stay targets. With one profitable quarter behind it, 72% above the 200-DMA and an LT-ASM flag, a de-rating would be fast and the ₹146 200-DMA is 42% below.

Key Risks

01
One quarter is not a track record
The company lost money in the year-ago quarter. A single ₹246 Cr profit quarter establishes that the business can earn at this level, not that it will. Q2 and Q3 are the tests.
02
Single-contract concentration
The $1.1bn OFC contract is ~38% of the order book. One counterparty, one delivery schedule, one renegotiation risk carrying a disproportionate share of the visibility.
03
Global OFC pricing cyclicality
With 55.5% of revenue exported, earnings now track global fibre pricing. That market has historically added capacity quickly when margins are attractive, which is exactly the current condition.
04
Defence targets are not orders
₹77 Cr actual against a ₹5,000 Cr FY29 target and a ₹300 Cr current defence book. Any valuation that leans on the defence ramp is leaning on an aspiration.
05
Extended technical position
+319% off the 52-week low, +72% above the 200-DMA. The most stretched chart in your holdings. First real support is 21% below at the 50-DMA.
06
Long-Term ASM surveillance
Surveillance-listed scrips typically attract raised margin requirements and are commonly excluded from broker margin-funding approved lists. Confirm eligibility before assuming this can be held on margin.
13 —

Summary

RATING: HOLD — A REAL TURNAROUND ALREADY PRICED

HFCL has the best set of facts of any name in this batch: an order book at five times revenue, a swing from loss to a ₹246 crore quarterly profit, exports at 55% of sales, management doubling its own growth guidance, and a market cap at just 1.44× the order book — the cheapest coverage ratio in your entire holdings list.

It also has the shortest track record and the most extended chart. One profitable quarter, 319% off the low, 72% above the 200-DMA, an LT-ASM flag, and a defence narrative whose targets exceed its actual order book by more than sixteen times.

The business case and the entry case point in different directions here, more sharply than for any other holding. If the thesis is the order book at 1.44×, that thesis is measured in quarters of conversion, not in the next few weeks of price action. What would settle it: two more quarters at anything close to Q1 profitability.
