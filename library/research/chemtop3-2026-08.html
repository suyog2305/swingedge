Eight companies were researched in full following the long-term breakout scan and the circulating dye-intermediates note: Bodal Chemicals, Bhageria Industries, Kiri Industries, Oriental Aromatics, Shree Pushkar, I G Petrochemicals, Gujarat Industries Power and Foseco India. Each has a standalone report in this library.

This note ranks all eight on the evidence those reports established, and then examines the top three side by side.

The Punchline: The Thesis Names Lost

The circulating note named four direct beneficiaries of the H-acid squeeze. Three are in this cohort — and here is where they finished:

A power utility and a foundry-chemicals subsidiary beat the H-acid names. That is not an argument against the thesis — the thesis is real and verified. It is a statement about what the evidence currently supports, and the reason is the timing point that runs through every report in this cluster:

Q1 FY27 covers April to June. H-acid spiked on 28 July. None of the reported numbers contains the thesis. Bodal’s dye intermediates segment actually fell 4% in the quarter and its margin compressed. The thesis names are being rated on results that predate their catalyst — while GIPCL and Foseco are being rated on results that already happened.

Bhageria is the exception that proves the point. It is genuinely in the dye chain and it delivered — revenue +82%, margin +255 bps — before the squeeze began. It is the only name here that is both exposed to the thesis and already performing without it.

1st — Gujarat Industries Power: The Value Case

Score18.2 — earnings 5, valuation 4.4, durability 4
CMP / market cap₹203.75 / ₹3,163 Cr
P/E · P/B6.3× · 0.83× — below book
Operating margin48.40% — the highest in this entire library
Q1 FY27Revenue +35%, EBITDA +112%, PAT +175%, margin +1,807 bps

Why it wins: it is the only name in this exercise that is simultaneously the cheapest and among the best on earnings quality. That combination is rare, and it comes from one fact — the margin expansion is structural, not cyclical.

600 MW of commissioned solar at Khavda generates at near-zero marginal cost. There is no lignite to buy, no gas, no fuel-price risk. The move from a 30.3% to a 48.4% operating margin is a mix shift from burning fuel to not burning it, and mix shifts do not mean-revert the way commodity spreads do. Every other high margin in this cohort is cyclical; this one is not.

It is also the third consecutive quarter of transformed profitability, and the FY27–28 earnings driver is already built or under construction — 600 MW live, 500 MW commissioning post-monsoon in Q3 FY27.

The single thing that could break it: a ₹7,000 crore lignite plant — 2.2× the market capitalisation — that would push the mix back toward fuel-burning generation and has no disclosed funding structure. See Section 09.

Why it wins: it is the only name that gives you the H-acid thesis as optionality on top of a business that is already working, rather than as the whole case.

Seven consecutive quarters of sequential revenue growth cannot be a base effect, a restocking spike or a single order. The streak began long before Chinese EHS enforcement tightened. If the squeeze delivers, it is additive; if it does not, the underlying improvement continues.

3rd — Foseco India: The Quality Case

Score14.8 — durability 5, earnings 4, valuation 1.3
CMP / market cap₹6,528 / ₹4,920 Cr
P/E · P/B47.2× · 4.77× — the most expensive in the cohort
Operating margin22.98% — highest of the chemicals names, and structural
Q1 FY27PAT +78% on sales +14%; margin up 250 bps sequentially

Why it places despite the worst valuation score in the cohort: it has the only genuinely defensible business model here.

Foundry consumables — binders, coatings, filters used up in every casting cycle — recur with the customer’s production volume rather than with their capex. Foundries qualify a binder system technically and tune their process around it, so switching risks scrap rates. And the consumables are a small share of the foundry’s cost but determine defect rates, which is the classic profile that supports pricing power.

That is why its 22.98% margin scores as structural while Bhageria’s 14.1% and Bodal’s 9.7% score as cyclical. It does not depend on a Chinese shutdown or a commodity spread.

The limitation is entirely price. 47.2× earnings and 4.77× book, 4.6% from its all-time high, 22% above its 50-DMA, growing sales 14%. There is no recovery discount and no cheapness argument. And it has zero exposure to the thesis that lifted the sector — part of its recent move is flow it did not earn.

10 —

Where All Three Could Be Wrong Together

A ranking of three names invites the assumption that they are independent bets. They are not entirely.

  • Two of the three are Indian industrial-cycle plays. Foseco’s foundry consumables track casting output, which tracks automotive and engineering production. Bhageria’s dye demand tracks textiles. A broad Indian industrial slowdown hits both.
  • All three have run hard into this ranking. They sit 15–22% above their 50-day averages after weekly gains of 6.9% to 16.1%. Part of that is the sector rotation into chemicals described in the scan report, and rotation reverses.
  • Two of the three are small. Bhageria at ₹1,042 crore and GIPCL at ₹3,163 crore have the liquidity profile that makes exits slow precisely when they matter.
  • The scoring itself is not neutral. Weighting earnings quality at 1.2 and valuation at 0.9 embeds a preference. A different weighting produces a different top three — Section 07 shows it flipping third place on its own.

GIPCL is the genuine diversifier of the three: a regulated utility with contracted offtake, whose earnings do not depend on industrial demand at all.

13 —

Verdict

TOP THREE: GIPCL 18.2  ·  BHAGERIA 16.6  ·  FOSECOIND 14.8

Eight companies were rated on trend, earnings quality, valuation and durability. The three that won did so on entirely different grounds — GIPCL is cheapest with a structural margin, Bhageria has the best operating record, and Foseco has the only genuinely defensible business model despite the worst valuation score in the cohort.

The finding that matters most is that the H-acid names lost. Bodal finished 5th, Shree Pushkar 6th, Kiri 8th. That is not a verdict on the thesis — which is verified and real — but on timing. Q1 FY27 ended in June; the price spike came on 28 July. The thesis names are being scored on results that predate their own catalyst, while GIPCL and Foseco are scored on results already delivered.

Bhageria is the one name that resolves the tension: genuinely in the dye chain, and already delivering seven consecutive quarters of sequential growth before the squeeze. The thesis is optionality on top rather than the whole case.

Two caveats stated plainly. Third place was decided by 0.2 points between Foseco and I G Petrochemicals — opposites in every respect, and a different weighting flips them. And Q2 FY27, around November, will re-rank this cohort more than anything else on the calendar. Bodal and Kiri could move up sharply when the quarter that contains the squeeze finally lands.
