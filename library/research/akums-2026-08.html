Akums is the manufacturer behind the label. It does not primarily sell medicines under its own brands — it makes them for other people's brands, on contract, at a scale no other Indian company matches. The company reports over 30% share by value of India's domestic CDMO market, is a manufacturing partner to 26 of the top 30 Indian pharmaceutical companies, serves more than 1,500 customers, and has commercialised over 4,100 formulations across 60+ dosage forms. Company disclosures put its output at more than 12% of the medicines consumed domestically in India.

That is a genuinely unusual industrial position — and it comes with a genuinely unusual economic profile. FY26 operating margin was 12%; Divi's Laboratories, the market's reference CDMO, earned 33%. Akums' three-year ROE screens at ~8.5% and ROCE at ~14.9%. This is a volume-and-share business, not a margin business. The entire equity debate reduces to one question: can a company that manufactures a tenth of India's medicine supply convert that share into returns on capital that justify a 3.6x book value?

Post-IPO Lock-in & Float Status

All IPO lock-ins have lapsed — the longest-dated (the 18-month promoter minimum-contribution lock-in from the August 2024 listing) would have expired around February 2026, with anchor and other pre-IPO lock-ins well before that. Promoters have not sold a single share: holding has printed at exactly 75.26% in every quarterly filing from September 2024 to June 2026. The float story has nonetheless changed materially — FII holding has collapsed from 7.34% (Sep 2024) to 1.91% (Jun 2026), while DIIs went the other way, from 7.53% to 13.44%. The stock is now largely a domestic-institution and retail book with a thin ~24.7% free float.

House view: Akums is the clearest listed expression of "own the factory, not the brand" in Indian pharma. It is a Tier-1 asset on market position and a Tier-3 asset on returns — which is exactly why the post-IPO price action has been so violent in both directions.

The FY26 full-year mix was broadly similar — CDMO ~80%, domestic branded 10.2%, international branded 3.3% — so the Q1 FY27 shape is structural, not seasonal. Read the mix honestly: Akums is a single-segment company with four appendages. The diversification narrative — branded, API, exports — has so far cost more than it has earned. Every rupee of the FY27 margin story so far comes from CDMO volume plus better API pricing on the input side.
03 —

Where Akums Sits in the Pharma Value Chain

Indian pharma splits cleanly into four economic layers. Brand owners capture the pricing power and the multiples; manufacturers capture the volume. Akums has deliberately colonised the layer everyone else outsources — and then made a modest, so-far-unprofitable attempt to climb into the layers on either side.

STAGE 02

STAGE 03

Chemists · HospitalsGovt tenders

Stage 02 is a structurally low-margin layer — a client can, in principle, re-tender its production. What protects Akums is not contract law but physics and regulation: 12 manufacturing units, 60+ dosage forms, 4,100+ commercialised SKUs and 1,056 cumulative DCGI approvals mean a brand owner can source almost its entire portfolio from one qualified vendor. Re-qualifying that at a competitor is expensive, slow and regulatorily risky. 38 of Akums' 50 largest customers had relationships longer than five years as of FY24. That stickiness is the moat; it is a switching-cost moat, not a pricing-power moat, which is precisely why it produces share without margin.

The structural tension in one line: Akums' clients at Stage 03 are also, via Akumentis, its competitors. Every rupee Akums invests in its own brands risks irritating the brand owners who supply 82.6% of its revenue. Management has kept Akumentis small — ~10% of mix — and that restraint looks deliberate.

The single most important number in this report is the gap between high-teens CDMO volume growth and 2–3% domestic industry volume growth. Akums is not riding the market; it is taking share within it. The mechanism is straightforward — as Indian pharma companies chase asset-light models, capacity that used to sit on their own balance sheets migrates to specialists, and the specialist with the widest dosage-form range and the deepest regulatory file collects most of it.

Client Concentration — the Honest Read

The top-10 CDMO clients contributed 39.3% of CDMO revenue in FY24 (38.9% in FY23), per ICRA's rating rationale. That is meaningful but not alarming for contract manufacturing, and the shape of the risk matters more than the level: these are large, financially sound Indian pharma majors on multi-year relationships, not a handful of fragile counterparties. The real concentration exposure is different — it is bargaining concentration. When your top ten customers are Sun, Cipla, Mankind and their peers, and your product is a manufactured tablet rather than a proprietary molecule, price negotiations run in one direction. That is the structural reason a 30%-share business earns 12–15% EBITDA margins rather than 25%.

What has genuinely improved: CDMO segment EBITDA rose 36.8% YoY in Q1 FY27 to ₹163 Cr on revenue growth of 18.6% — operating leverage of roughly 2x. That is the first clean evidence since listing that scale is translating into profit rather than just tonnage.

Capacity of 49.21 bn formulation units / 737.38 MT APIs and the 12-unit count are as stated on the company's own disclosures; these are cumulative installed figures and may not reflect the very latest additions. Utilisation of ~50% is management commentary from the Q1 FY27 earnings call.

The ~50% utilisation figure is the most consequential operating datum in the file. It means the FY27 growth Akums is guiding to does not require a capex cycle — the plants already exist and are half-idle. In a fixed-cost-heavy manufacturing business, incremental volume onto paid-for assets is the highest-margin revenue there is, which is exactly what the Q1 FY27 print showed: revenue +13.9%, EBITDA +35.4%.

The counterpoint is equally important. Half-idle capacity is also half-idle capital. It sits in the denominator of ROCE, and it is a large part of why a company with 30% market share earns a 14.9% return on capital employed. The utilisation number is simultaneously the bull case (operating leverage available) and the bear case (capital already sunk into assets that are not yet earning).

Operational fragility to note: Akums' manufacturing is heavily concentrated at Haridwar. In FY26, labour unrest disrupted production at the Haridwar sites, with full 100% capacity restored only on 18 May 2026. A single-cluster footprint is efficient right up until it isn't.

Competitive Positioning & Peer Comparison

Akums competes in two arenas that the market persistently conflates. In domestic-formulations CDMO it is the dominant player and its true peers are Innova Captab and Windlas Biotech. In global/regulated CDMO — the arena the market pays 60–80x for — it is a new entrant with one signed European contract and no track record.

The Read-Across

  • Against Innova Captab: Akums is 2.7x the revenue but earns a lower operating margin (12% vs 15%) and a much lower ROE (8.45% vs 13.7%) — while trading at a marginally higher P/E. On these numbers, scale has bought Akums share, not superior unit economics. This is the most uncomfortable comparison in the table.
  • Against Windlas Biotech: 4.8x the revenue at an identical 12% operating margin, a lower ROE, and a 30% higher P/E. Windlas is the value alternative in the same industry.
  • Against Divi's: a different business entirely. Divi's earns 33% margins and 22% ROCE from custom synthesis and generic APIs for innovator clients; Akums earns 12% making other people's tablets. The 80x vs 40x multiple gap is not a mispricing — it is a correct reflection of two different economic models.
  • Against Piramal Pharma and Cohance: the cautionary exhibits. Both are larger-ambition CDMOs trading on hope rather than delivered returns — Piramal at a loss, Cohance at 164x on 7% ROE. Akums at 39.5x with 14.9% ROCE is, at least, not the worst-capitalised story in the sector.
The fair conclusion: Akums is the best-positioned and worst-returning-per-rupee-of-scale name in domestic-formulations CDMO. It deserves a premium to Windlas for franchise durability and optionality. Whether it deserves a premium to Innova Captab — which out-earns it on every profitability metric — is the argument the market is currently having.

Two structural observations. First, the cash is doing real work in the ROE denominator: strip ₹1,616 Cr of net cash out of a ₹12,048 Cr market cap and the enterprise value is ~₹10,432 Cr — which puts the business on ~20x FY26 adjusted EBITDA, not the ~40x the headline P/E implies. Second, that same cash is a capital-allocation question the company has not yet answered. It is earning fixed-deposit rates while the equity is priced for growth. The ₹56 Cr Oriflame acquisition and the ~₹300 Cr FY27 capex plan are steps; they do not deploy ₹1,616 Cr.

The most likely path to an ROE inflection is not margin alone — it is capital deployment. Either the cash goes into capacity and acquisitions that earn above the cost of capital, or it comes back to shareholders in size. A ₹3/share dividend on ₹1,616 Cr of net cash is neither.

Put differently, an investor buying today is being paid nothing for the base case and is underwriting a coin-flip between a full re-rating and a full de-rating. This is not a comment on business quality. Akums' operating trajectory over the last three quarters is unambiguously improving. It is a comment on entry price — the market has priced the improvement before it is annualised.

Key Risk Factors

01
Structurally Modest Margins & Returns
HIGH
12% FY26 operating margin, 8.45% three-year ROE, 14.9% ROCE — against 3.63x book. Contract manufacturing for larger, more powerful counterparties has limited pricing power by construction. If the 14–15% margin guide proves a cyclical peak rather than a new floor, the multiple has no support.
02
Valuation / Re-rating Already Done
HIGH
₹765 is +87% off the ₹409 52-week low and 2.4% below the ₹784 high, on ~40x trailing and ~28.6x base-case FY27E. Base-case fair value offers +1% to +5%. The margin of safety has been spent.
03
Single-Cluster Manufacturing (Haridwar)
MED–HIGH
Production is heavily concentrated at Haridwar, Uttarakhand. Labour unrest disrupted output during FY26, with full capacity restored only on 18 May 2026. A repeat — labour, regulatory, or environmental — hits a large share of group revenue at once. The Baddi and Noida/Roorkee additions dilute this only marginally.
04
Client Concentration & Channel Conflict
MEDIUM
Top-10 CDMO clients were 39.3% of CDMO revenue in FY24. Those clients are large pharma majors with structural bargaining advantage — and, via Akumentis, Akums competes with them in domestic branded formulations. Scaling the branded arm too aggressively risks the CDMO franchise that funds it.
05
API Price Tailwind Is Not Durable
MEDIUM
Management explicitly attributed part of Q1 FY27's margin strength to favourable API input prices, and flagged that Q1 sat at the upper end of the 14–15% range for that reason. API pricing is a cycle, not a moat; a reversal removes a chunk of the margin inflection the market has just paid for.
06
FY28 Execution: Europe & Zambia
MEDIUM
The ₹1,760 Cr Europe contract requires European regulatory approval of the oral-liquid site and only begins commercially in FY28. Zambia's ~₹240 Cr H2 FY27 shipment depends on a sovereign counterparty; the JV facility is a 2028 project. Neither is de-risked, and both are already partially in the price.
07
Loss-Making Non-Core Segments
MEDIUM
API revenue fell 29.7% YoY in Q1 FY27 and is still loss-making (₹4 Cr); trade generics fell 9.5% at breakeven EBITDA; international branded declined 1.5%; domestic branded EBITDA fell 25.4% on field-force spend. Four of five segments are currently value-neutral or worse.
08
Thin Float, FII Exodus & Tax Overhang
LOW–MED
Promoters hold 75.26% leaving a ~24.7% float; FII holding has fallen from 7.34% to 1.91% since listing, concentrating the register in domestic hands and amplifying moves in both directions. Separately, a ₹133.75 Cr block-period income-tax demand (FY18–FY25) is under appeal; the company expects no material impact, but it is an unresolved contingent claim.

Investment Summary & Recommendation

Akums manufactures more than a tenth of the medicine India consumes and holds over 30% of the domestic CDMO market, supplying 26 of the country's 30 largest pharma companies from 12 plants running at roughly 50% utilisation. That is a real, physical, hard-to-replicate moat — and for two years after its August 2024 IPO the market refused to pay for it, marking the stock down 40% from its ₹679 issue price through a run of earnings disappointments that bottomed with Q2 FY26's 9% operating margin and ₹43 Cr of profit. The last three quarters have changed the argument: CDMO volumes have compounded in the high teens against an industry growing 2–3%, and Q1 FY27 delivered a record ₹1,167 Cr of revenue at a 15.0% EBITDA margin with PAT up 56%. The problem is that the stock has already re-rated 87% off its ₹409 low to sit 2% below its all-time high, on ~40x trailing and ~28.6x our base-case FY27E — a full multiple for a business that still earns an 8.45% ROE on 3.6x book, still loses money in API, and whose two genuinely transformative contracts (the ₹1,760 Cr European agreement and the Zambia partnership) are largely FY28 events. This is a high-quality franchise at a price that already assumes the guidance is met. Own the business; wait for the price.

The Three Reasons to Own AKUMS

  • 1. An irreplaceable share position: >30% of India's domestic CDMO market, 26 of the top 30 pharma majors as clients, 4,100+ commercialised formulations across 60+ dosage forms, and 38 of the top 50 customers on relationships longer than five years. You cannot build this in a decade.
  • 2. Operating leverage sitting in plain sight: ~50% capacity utilisation means growth needs volume, not capex. Q1 FY27 proved the drop-through — revenue +13.9%, EBITDA +35.4%, CDMO segment EBITDA +36.8%.
  • 3. A fortress balance sheet with unpriced optionality: ₹1,616 Cr net cash (13% of market cap), effectively no debt, IPO proceeds fully deployed per CRISIL. Strip the cash and the enterprise trades at ~14.4x base-case FY27E EBITDA — far less demanding than the headline. The ₹1,760 Cr Europe contract and the Zambia programme are free options on FY28.
