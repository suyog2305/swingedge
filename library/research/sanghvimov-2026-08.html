Revenue +39%, EBITDA +30%, profit +29% — a good quarter, with one flaw: earnings grew more slowly than revenue. That is the signature of margin compression, and it is developed in Section 04.

A 35% EBITDA margin remains exceptional in absolute terms — the third-highest in this import batch behind Sudeep Pharma (34.7% operating) and comfortably above most manufacturers here. Crane rental is an asset-heavy business where the asset, once bought, generates high-margin recurring revenue.

04 —

The Margin Slip — 40.1% to 35%

PeriodEBITDA margin
FY26 (full year)40.1%
Q1 FY2735.0%
Change−510 bps

A 510 basis point margin decline against the FY26 full year is the most significant negative in this update, and it is easy to miss behind 39% revenue growth.

For an asset-heavy rental business, the plausible causes are specific:

  • Mix. Wind EPC and Project EPC are lower-margin than pure crane hiring — EPC involves subcontracted work and materials, crane hire is almost pure asset utilisation. Growth weighted toward EPC dilutes the blended margin.
  • New fleet not yet fully utilised. FY26 capex of ~₹629 Cr adds depreciation and cost before those cranes reach mature utilisation.
  • Overseas ramp. Saudi, Botswana and Qatar operations at sub-scale utilisation dilute group margin while they build.

All three are consistent with a growing business rather than a deteriorating one, and the Saudi datapoint in Section 07 supports the third. But margin is the entire investment case in crane rental — a 40% margin business and a 30% margin business are valued very differently — so this is the number to watch above all others.

One commentary source characterised the quarter as “robust revenue growth masks margin pressure and rising debt costs”. The margin point is confirmed by the figures above; the debt-cost element is consistent with ~₹629 Cr of FY26 capex but the current interest charge is not established in the sources reviewed.

05 —

The Capex Engine — And Its Arithmetic

FY26Value
Revenue₹1,070 Cr (+36.8%)
EBITDA₹429 Cr (40.1% margin)
PAT₹184 Cr (17.2% margin, EPS ~₹20)
Capex~₹629 Cr

Read those last two lines together. Sanghvi spent roughly ₹629 crore on fleet and overseas build-out in a year when it earned ₹184 crore — capex at 3.4× profit, and 59% of revenue.

That is the most aggressive capex-to-profit ratio in this entire library. For comparison: Happy Forgings spent ₹460 crore against ₹302 crore of profit (1.5×), and that was flagged as a significant cycle bet.

Crane rental genuinely works this way — the fleet is the business, and growth requires buying cranes. A 1,600 MT crawler crane is a multi-year asset generating high-margin revenue for a decade or more. But the arithmetic means growth is debt-funded or cash-consuming, which is why the margin slip and any rise in interest cost matter more here than at an asset-light company.

The book grew ₹197 crore in a quarter while ₹380 crore of revenue was recognised — orders arriving faster than execution, which is the right direction.

The critical qualifier is “fully executable within FY27”. Against guided FY27 revenue of ₹1,400–1,500 crore, a ₹1,250 crore book covers roughly 85% of the year. That is strong near-term visibility and no visibility beyond it. Like Man Industries and Paramount, this is a short-cycle book that must be continuously replenished — not a multi-year backlog like HFCL’s.

07 —

Saudi Arabia Turns Positive

The Saudi operations under the Middle East subsidiary transitioned to positive monthly EBITDA in early FY27, improving international margins.

This is a genuinely important milestone and it deserves more weight than its size suggests. Overseas expansion in crane rental is capital-intensive and slow — cranes must be shipped, depots established, and local relationships built before utilisation reaches breakeven. A subsidiary crossing into positive EBITDA marks the point where it stops consuming group margin and starts adding to it.

It also partially explains, and partially resolves, the margin question in Section 04: if overseas sub-scale utilisation was diluting the blended margin, Saudi turning positive is the first evidence that dilution is ending.

Saudi Arabia is a well-chosen market — heavy infrastructure and energy construction on a large scale, with limited local heavy-lift capacity. Botswana and Qatar remain smaller and their status is not disclosed in the sources reviewed.

The guidance implicitly forecasts a margin recovery. Q1 delivered 35%; the guided midpoints imply roughly 37.5% for the full year. Management is therefore signalling that the Q1 compression is temporary — consistent with the new-fleet-utilisation and overseas-ramp explanations.

On revenue, ₹1,400–1,500 crore against FY26’s ₹1,070 crore implies 31–40% growth. Q1’s 39% is at the top of that range, so the guidance is achievable on the current run-rate without acceleration.

The reiterated 16.25–16.5% return on capital is the most useful disclosure, because in an asset-heavy business that is the number that actually matters. It tells you what ₹629 crore of capex is expected to earn.

09 —

The Wind Bet

The June report described Sanghvi as a wind-capex proxy, and that framing remains correct. Erecting a wind turbine requires very large mobile cranes — few operators in India own enough of them, and Sanghvi’s 485+ fleet with capacity up to 1,600 MT is the scarce asset.

Management attributes the operational acceleration to consistent utilisation in domestic heavy-lift rental and scaling wind power installations.

The dependency runs both ways. India’s renewable build-out is policy-supported and multi-year, which is a strong tailwind. But wind installation is lumpy, seasonal and tied to policy auctions and tariff decisions — a slow year in wind capex would leave a large, expensive fleet under-utilised, and utilisation is exactly what drives the 35–40% margin. This is the operating leverage that makes the business work and the exposure that makes it risky.

On earnings, Sanghvi is the cheapest name in this import batch — 18.7× trailing and ~14.7× on annualised Q1 profit, for a business earning a 35% EBITDA margin with a guided 16.25–16.5% return on capital.

The discipline is to ask why an asset with those characteristics trades at 2.91× book. Two honest answers: the fleet is depreciating capital that must be continuously replaced — ₹629 crore of capex against ₹184 crore of profit is the cost of standing still and growing — and the business is a leveraged bet on one lumpy end-market. The ~40% drawdown from the 2024 peak is the market’s memory of what happens when wind capex slows.

That said, cheap-with-a-reason is different from cheap-and-broken. Unlike Aurionpro at RS 10 with collapsing growth, Sanghvi is growing 39% with a 35% margin and a book covering 85% of guided revenue. The valuation reflects the capital intensity, not a failing business.

India’s wind build-out runs multi-year, keeping the large-crane fleet fully utilised. At ~14.7× annualised earnings and 2.91× book with a 16%+ return on capital, this re-rates meaningfully.

Margin falls further from 35%, and in an asset-heavy business with rising debt cost that flows straight to the bottom line. The short-cycle order book offers no cushion beyond FY27. The stock has already shown, in the ~40% drawdown from ₹741, how far it can fall when utilisation disappoints.

Summary

RATING: HOLD — CHEAPEST QUALITY HERE, MARGIN SLIPPING

Sanghvi Movers is Asia’s largest crane-rental operator, running 485+ cranes up to 1,600 MT, and it grew Q1 revenue 39% with a 35% EBITDA margin and profit up 29%. Its Saudi subsidiary turned positive on monthly EBITDA in early FY27 — a real milestone in a slow, capital-intensive overseas build. FY27 guidance of ₹1,400–1,500 crore was reiterated, backed by a ₹1,250 crore book covering 85% of it.

On valuation it is the cheapest name in this import batch: 18.7× trailing earnings, ~14.7× annualised, 2.91× book, for a business guiding to a 16.25–16.5% return on capital.

Two things keep it at Hold. EBITDA margin has fallen 510 basis points from FY26’s 40.1% to 35% — and in crane rental the margin is the investment case. And capex of ~₹629 crore against ₹184 crore of profit is the most aggressive ratio in this library: the fleet must keep being bought, which means growth consumes cash and levers the business to wind-capex utilisation. Management guides to a margin recovery toward ~37.5%; Q2 is where that gets tested. Your own framework currently has it failing the trend template, 16.8% below its high and still ~40% under the 2024 peak.
