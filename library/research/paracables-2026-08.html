04 —

The Profit Puzzle — Operating Profit +129%, PAT +3.7%

This is the most important thing in the quarter and it is easy to miss. Operating profit more than doubled — up 129.2% — while profit after tax rose just 3.7%. Something between the operating line and the bottom line absorbed almost the entire gain.

Note the phrasing in the disclosure: operating profit is stated “excluding other income”. That is the clue. If the prior-year quarter carried substantial other income and this one did not, then:

  • Prior year: low operating profit (₹15.1 Cr) plus meaningful other income → PAT ~₹19.0 Cr
  • This year: much higher operating profit (₹34.7 Cr) minus that other-income support, plus higher interest and depreciation → PAT ₹19.7 Cr

Read that way, the quarter is considerably better than the PAT line suggests. The underlying business — the part that actually manufactures and sells cable — roughly doubled its profitability, while the reported bottom line was flattered a year ago by items that are not operations.

The exact composition of other income, interest and depreciation is not established in the sources reviewed. This report flags the gap and its likely cause rather than asserting the split. It is directly checkable in the Q1 filing and is the single most valuable thing to verify on this name.

The Tariff Story — A Complete Round Trip

PeriodExports as % of revenueEvent
H1 FY2643%Pre-shock normal
H2 FY2616%August 2025 US tariff shock
Q4 FY2615%Trough
Q1 FY2729.3% (₹155 Cr)Recovery as tariff uncertainty eased

This is one of the cleanest illustrations in the library of how a single policy event reshapes a business. Export share fell from 43% to 16% within six months of the August 2025 US tariff action, and has recovered to 29.3% as uncertainty eased.

Two conclusions follow, and they pull in opposite directions:

  • The recovery is real and is driving the margin. Exports carry better realisations than domestic B2C cable. Export share nearly doubling from Q4 FY26 to Q1 FY27 is the primary reason operating margin went from 3.4% to 6.6%.
  • The exposure has not gone away. Paramount is the largest Indian LV-cable exporter to the US, which is a genuine competitive achievement and a concentration. A further tariff change would repeat 2025 exactly.

Exports at 29.3% are still well below the 43% pre-shock level. The recovery is partial, and the path back to 43% depends on trade policy no company controls.

In Q1 FY27, power cables accounted for 57% of revenue, concentrated in the higher-margin B2B institutional business.

The mix matters because these are structurally different businesses. B2B industrial and B2G/PSU cable is specification-driven and tender-based — competitive, but with technical qualification barriers. B2C retail cable is brand-and-distribution driven, competing against much larger branded players. Paramount’s margin comes from the institutional and export side; the retail business at 9% of revenue is a distribution investment rather than a profit engine today.

Order Book

Standing order book (30 June 2026)~₹615 Cr
Coverage of annualised Q1 revenue~0.29× — roughly one quarter of sales

This is the thinnest order-book coverage of any name in this library. ₹615 crore against ₹2,118 crore of annualised revenue is less than four months of visibility.

Cable manufacturing runs on short cycles and repeat institutional ordering rather than long backlogs, so the comparison to HFCL at 5× or MTAR at 3.6× is not entirely fair. But it does mean there is no contracted cushion. A soft quarter in export demand or domestic tendering shows up in revenue almost immediately, with nothing to absorb it — the same structural point flagged for Man Industries, but on far thinner coverage.

08 —

The ₹300 Crore EHV Bet

Investment₹300 Cr greenfield EHV facility
LocationNarmadapuram, Madhya Pradesh
TimingPartial commissioning expected Q1 FY28
Scale check~15% of market capitalisation, ~14% of annualised revenue

Relative to the company’s size, this is a large bet. ₹300 crore against a ₹2,022 crore market capitalisation is roughly 15% — a far bigger proportional commitment than most capex programmes in this library.

The strategic logic is sound and mirrors what Atlanta Electricals is doing in transformers: extra-high-voltage cable is the value ladder in this industry. EHV commands better margins, has fewer qualified suppliers, and rides the same T&D build-out. For a company earning 6.6% operating margin, moving up-voltage is the most credible route to a structurally better business.

The risk is the timeline and the funding. Partial commissioning in Q1 FY28 means roughly two years of capital committed before meaningful revenue, at a company with thin margins and less than four months of order coverage. The May report noted the promoter had been buying in the open market, which is a supportive signal about management’s own confidence — but the capex must still be financed through a period of tariff-exposed earnings.

The US distributor count going from 2 to 8 in four years is the most concrete evidence that the export franchise is built rather than opportunistic — and it explains how exports recovered from 15% to 29% of revenue so quickly once tariff uncertainty eased. The channel was still in place.

The domestic retail network — 600 outlets and 10,000+ electricians — supports the B2C business that is currently only 9% of revenue. It is an investment in a segment that has not yet become material, and it should be judged on whether B2C share grows from here.

At 2.45× book and under 1× sales, Paramount is inexpensive on assets and revenue. That is appropriate for a business earning 6.6% at the operating line — thin-margin manufacturers should trade at low revenue multiples, and 0.95× is not a bargain so much as a fair reflection of what a rupee of cable revenue is worth.

The interesting question is what the 6.6% margin becomes. If operating margin holds at 6.6% or improves toward the EHV mix, then 25.7× annualised earnings on a recovering business is reasonable. If exports fall back toward 16% on another tariff move, margin returns to 3.4% and the earnings roughly halve. The valuation is entirely a bet on which of those two states persists — and that is a trade-policy question, not a company one.

Summary

RATING: HOLD — MARGIN DOUBLED, PROFIT BARELY MOVED

Paramount reported Q1 revenue up 17.4% and operating profit up 129.2%, with margin going from 3.4% to 6.6% — driven by exports recovering from a trough of 15% of revenue in Q4 FY26 to 29.3%, after the August 2025 US tariff shock had cut them from 43% to 16%.

Yet profit after tax rose only 3.7%. The gap between a 129% operating gain and a 3.7% bottom-line gain is the most important unresolved item on this name, and the disclosure phrase “excluding other income” points at the likely cause — a prior year flattered by non-operating items. If that reading is right, the underlying business is doing considerably better than the headline PAT suggests. It is directly checkable in the Q1 filing and is worth doing before any decision.

Against that: RS 12, 18.4% below its high, an order book of ₹615 crore covering under four months, and a ₹300 crore EHV capex worth 15% of market capitalisation that contributes nothing until FY28. At 2.45× book and 0.95× sales it is genuinely inexpensive — appropriately so for a 6.6%-margin manufacturer whose export earnings depend on trade policy it does not control.
