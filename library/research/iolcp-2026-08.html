02 —

The Ibuprofen Problem

For most of its listed history IOL has been a single-molecule story, and that has been the reason it trades at a low multiple. A dominant share of a commodity API is a mixed blessing:

  • Ibuprofen is off-patent, high-volume and price-competitive. Scale gives cost leadership, not pricing power.
  • Earnings track the API price cycle, which is driven by global capacity additions — particularly Chinese — rather than by anything management controls.
  • Concentration risk is structural. One molecule, one price curve, one set of competitors.

The market has historically priced IOL accordingly: as a cyclical commodity chemical company rather than a pharmaceutical business. The entire re-rating case rests on that characterisation becoming outdated.

The Diversification — The Actual Thesis

Non-Ibuprofen share of pharma revenueQ1 FY26Q1 FY27
Share of pharmaceutical revenue36%43%
YoY growth in non-Ibuprofen revenue—+67%

Management’s stated target is a 50-50 split between Ibuprofen and non-Ibuprofen products, and at 43% the company is close to reaching it.

This is the most important trend in the business and the reason the stock deserves attention beyond its momentum. Every percentage point of mix shift reduces the single-molecule concentration that has capped the multiple. A diversified API maker with six or seven meaningful products earns a structurally higher rating than a one-molecule producer, because the earnings stream is less exposed to any single price curve.

The products doing the work — Paracetamol, Clopidogrel, Pantoprazole, Metformin, Fenofibrate, Levetiracetam — are themselves generic, high-volume APIs. This is diversification of commodity exposure, not an escape from it. It reduces variance; it does not create pricing power. That distinction matters for how much re-rating is reasonable to expect.

05 —

Exports and the China Approval

Exports rose to 28.5% of revenue in Q1 FY27 from 24.4% a year earlier, and the company secured NMPA approval for Clopidogrel in China.

The China approval is strategically more interesting than its likely near-term revenue. China is the world’s largest API-producing country; an Indian API maker winning regulatory approval to sell into it is a genuine validation of manufacturing quality, and it is a reference that helps with other regulators.

The FY27 guidance places exports at 25–30% of revenue, which means the company is guiding to roughly hold the current level rather than expand it further. Treat export share as a quality indicator rather than a growth driver for now.

Margins have expanded roughly 390 basis points over two years. Management attributes this to cost optimisation, improved product mix and higher capacity utilisation.

Keep the absolute level in perspective. A 14.6% EBITDA margin is respectable for a commodity API producer and modest against specialty pharmaceutical businesses — Happy Forgings, a forging company on this same watchlist, earns more than double it. IOL is not becoming a high-margin business; it is becoming a better commodity business. That is a real and investable improvement, but it defines the ceiling on any re-rating.

08 —

Capex and Balance-Sheet Discipline

FY26 capex was approximately ₹164 crore, directed at capacity expansion, product and infrastructure development, process automation and operational improvement — and funded entirely through internal accruals.

This is the quiet strength of the story, and it is worth contrasting explicitly with the other names on this watchlist. IOL spent ₹164 crore against ₹138 crore of annual profit and funded it from cash flow. Happy Forgings spent ₹460 crore against ₹302 crore of profit. Jyoti CNC has ₹450 crore near-term and over ₹1,000 crore approved.

IOL is the least financially stretched of the three by a wide margin, and at 3.18× book versus 10.1× and 11.8× for the other two, it is also the cheapest on assets. In a portfolio where leverage is being used, the balance-sheet quality of the underlying holding matters more than usual.

09 —

FY27 Guidance

Revenue growth15% – 20%
EBITDA margin14% – 15%
Exports~25% – 30% of revenue

Read this guidance carefully, because it is the most important paragraph in the report. Q1 FY27 delivered 37% revenue growth. Full-year guidance is 15–20%. Management is guiding to roughly half the growth rate it has just reported.

There are two readings. The charitable one is conservatism — the same posture Happy Forgings takes, and a good sign. The less charitable one is that management does not expect the Q1 pricing environment to persist, which for a commodity API producer is an entirely reasonable expectation to hold.

Either way, the implication for a holder is the same: the guided full-year number is well below the run-rate the stock has just re-rated on. If growth decelerates from 37% toward 15–20% over the next three quarters, that is management delivering exactly what it promised — and it may still disappoint a price that is at an all-time high.

Scenarios

Bull
The 50-50 mix target is reached within FY27. API pricing holds, margins settle at 15%, and the market re-rates IOL from a commodity chemical multiple toward a diversified API multiple. At 32.4× on a rising earnings base with 3.18× book, this is the name on the watchlist with the most room for the multiple itself to expand.
Base
Growth decelerates from 37% to the guided 15–20% as the pricing environment normalises. Margins hold at 14–15%. The business is genuinely better than it was two years ago, and the stock consolidates the 191% move while earnings catch up to the price.
Bear
The API pricing cycle rolls over — the recurring pattern in this industry, usually driven by Chinese capacity. Q1’s 14.6% margin proves to be a cycle peak rather than a new base. A stock 191% off its low and at an all-time high with no support within 27% would give back a large part of the move quickly, and the LT-ASM flag suggests the regulator already regards the price action as unusual.

Key Risks

01
API price cyclicality
The dominant risk. Generic API pricing is set by global capacity, largely Chinese. Neither IOL’s cost position nor its product mix protects it from a broad price decline — diversification spreads the exposure across more molecules, it does not remove it.
02
Guidance implies deceleration
FY27 guidance of 15–20% against a Q1 run-rate of 37%. The company can hit guidance and still disappoint a price set at an all-time high.
03
Residual Ibuprofen concentration
Even at the 50-50 target, half of pharmaceutical revenue remains tied to a single molecule where the company is a global-scale producer in a price-taking market.
04
Extended technical position
191% off the 52-week low, 65% above the 200-DMA, at an all-time high. No overhead resistance, but equally no support within 27%.
05
Regulatory and quality risk
API manufacturing is regulator-dependent across multiple jurisdictions. A single facility observation can interrupt a large share of revenue.
06
Long-Term ASM surveillance
The stock carries an LT-ASM flag. Surveillance-listed scrips typically attract raised margin requirements and are commonly excluded from broker margin-funding (MTF) approved lists. Confirm eligibility with your broker before assuming this can be held on margin.

Summary

RATING: ACCUMULATE ON DIPS — OWN THE MIX SHIFT

IOL is the most interesting of the three companies covered in this batch, because it is the only one where the fundamental story and the valuation are not yet fully reconciled. Jyoti CNC and Happy Forgings both trade at 66–74× earnings; IOL trades at 32.4× and 3.18× book while growing profit 90% and expanding margins 220 basis points.

The reason for the discount is the single-molecule history, and the reason to be interested is that the discount is being earned away in real time — 36% to 43% non-Ibuprofen in a year, with a 50-50 target in sight. That is a specific, measurable, falsifiable thesis, which is exactly what a research report should be able to give you.

The counterweight is that the price has already moved 191% and sits at an all-time high, and management is guiding to half the growth rate it just delivered. The business case is the strongest on a relative basis; the entry point is the least comfortable. Those two facts are not in conflict — they simply mean the thesis is about the mix shift over several quarters, and the chart is about the last twelve months.
