01 —

What This Note Is

A request came to this desk for a report on “the recent merger that is happening between Birla Cable and Vindhya Telelinks”, two M.P. Birla group companies, with Universal Cables noted as the group’s third listed cable company. The house rule is to test the premise before writing the report, and the desk’s last thematic note found that its premise failed. This one does not fail. The merger is real, it is documented in primary filings from both companies, and it is part-way through its approvals. The premise does need four corrections, and they are made here, in the title and in the verdict.

What this note is and is not

It is a merger note on three listed names: the two parties and the group company that owns large stakes in both. It carries no rating and no recommendation; the framework’s own signals for each name are generated into §08 from the 18 September 2026 close and refresh with each scan. It does not size positions, allocate capital, or advise on entering, holding or exiting any of the three.

The deal is real and its terms are fixed. The market is no longer pricing those terms. That disagreement is the finding, so it comes first.

The gap was 39.6% the day before the announcement, closed to zero on 27 May 2026, and has reopened to its widest since BCL reported a Q1 FY27 profit of ₹30.69 crore against ₹1.34 crore a year earlier on 2 August 2026 [14][19].
Does it simplify the group?Only a littleOne listed company and one cross-holding (VTL’s 19.33% of BCL) disappear [1][18]. The larger loop stays: UCL owns 29.15% of VTL and the VTL group owns 30.34% of UCL, and the VTL group’s 31.68% of Birla Corporation is untouched [13][18]. The filing’s own share counts imply that Birla Corporation becomes a roughly 3.4% shareholder of VTL after the merger (derived from [1][18]).
The strongest part of the story is that the merger exists and has cleared the exchanges and SEBI’s comment stage without an adverse observation [8]. The weakest part is the assumption that it completes at 10 for 115. The ratio was struck on financials to 31 December 2025 and on management projections of about 10% a year revenue growth for BCL to FY2030 [4]; BCL’s Q1 FY27 revenue grew 51% and its profit before tax of ₹41.08 crore exceeded VTL’s entire standalone profit before tax of ₹37.79 crore [10][14]. The holders who have to approve the scheme are the ones the ratio now costs.

What a reader should hold on to. Anyone who owns BCL today owns, if the scheme completes as drafted, 10 VTL shares for every 115 BCL shares and nothing else. At the 18 September closes that is ₹26,600 of VTL stock for ₹47,092.50 of BCL stock (derived from [19]). Either BCL’s price, VTL’s price, or the ratio has to move for those two numbers to meet, or the scheme does not complete. §11 sets out which evidence would point to which.

The stated rationale

Both filings give the same reasons: the two companies make the same products from neighbouring plants in Rewa, so combining them consolidates capacity, removes customer overlap, shares procurement, logistics, IT and administration, and removes one listed entity from the group. They also say a single larger balance sheet raises VTL’s pre-qualification and net-worth credentials for large multi-state EPC tenders that need bank guarantees [1][2]. No synergy is quantified in the board filings, the audit-committee report or the valuation report, and the valuers state that they valued both companies without synergies [4][6]. VTL’s managing director repeated on 7 August 2026 that the company expects the amalgamation to strengthen its competitive position, subject to approvals [11].

Who counts as promoter afterwards

The board filings give the post-scheme split without names: promoter-group shares rise by 5,98,120 and public shares by 15,06,119 [1][2]. Working each BCL promoter-group holder through the ratio, the promoter increase matches the entitlements of UCL, Punjab Produce & Trading, East India Investment, Baroda Agents, Punjab Produce Holdings and three 100-share holders, which together come to 5,98,216 shares. The filing implies 5,98,220 once the 100 VTL shares held by BCL, which are cancelled, are added back; the difference of four is fraction rounding (derived from [1][18]). It does not leave room for Birla Corporation (4,68,566 shares), Hindustan Medical Institution (1,48,979) or the trustee holding (10,803). The arithmetic therefore implies that these three, promoter-group holders in BCL today, are counted as public shareholders of VTL after the scheme. That is an inference from the totals, not a statement in the filings; SEBI has asked for the holder-by-holder classification and its justification to be put in the notice to shareholders [8].

Is a holding-company discount being unwound or created?

Both, depending on which share you hold. For VTL’s existing shareholders a small part is unwound: a 19.33% listed minority stake worth ₹237.5 crore, the kind of asset the market discounts inside a holding structure, becomes full ownership of an operating business. For BCL’s shareholders a discount is created. BCL today is close to a pure operating company: its one meaningful investment, 7,85,967 UCL shares, was worth ₹116.1 crore on 18 September 2026, about 9.4% of its own market value of ₹1,228.5 crore (derived from [15][19]). The VTL share they receive is a claim on a company whose listed group holdings, at ₹3,680.4 crore after the scheme, equal 99% of its pro forma market value at the current VTL price (derived; see §10). RBSA, BCL’s own valuer, says in the report that market participants generally apply a holding-company discount to such investments, and gives that as a reason for not using net asset value [4].

The loop that matters is left alone. UCL and VTL own roughly 30% of each other before and after, and each equity-accounts the other’s profit [10][16]. The scheme neither collapses that circle nor touches Birla Corporation. Whether a second step involving UCL is planned is not stated anywhere in the documents read, and none is assumed here.

The three subsidiaries that hold most of the Birla Corporation stake

August Agents, Insilco Agents and Laneseda Agents are wholly owned VTL subsidiaries that between them hold 23.39% of Birla Corporation and 6.50% of UCL, worth ₹1,816.6 crore at the 18 September closes, which is 48% of the VTL group’s listed holdings (derived from [13][18][19]). VTL did not consolidate them for five years, FY22 to FY26. Its FY26 results filing explains that the subsidiaries’ books and statutory records were being withheld by former directors, that civil and criminal proceedings to recover them were pending in Calcutta courts, that petitions about the subsidiaries’ affairs were dismissed by the NCLT Kolkata on 7 February 2023 with appeals at the NCLAT not yet admitted, and that the auditors drew attention to the matter [12]. On 7 August 2026 VTL said it had since received approved, audited statements for all five years, prepared on the records available, and it restated its consolidated accounts: FY26 consolidated profit moved from ₹220.18 crore to ₹227.23 crore, with ₹33.65 crore taken to opening equity [10]. UCL restated in step [16]. This sits inside the long-running dispute over the M.P. Birla estate, in which a Calcutta High Court division bench in December 2023 barred the court-appointed administrators from interfering in the companies’ day-to-day running and the Supreme Court admitted appeals against that ruling in March 2024 [23][24]. The later course of those appeals was not established; one uncited May 2026 report of a Supreme Court order on how the estate’s trustees vote could not be verified [25].

Birla Cable — mostly a copper LAN-cable company, with a fibre quarter

The market treats BCL as a fibre stock. Its own annual report says otherwise for FY26: structured copper LAN and telecom cable brought in ₹663.1 crore, 86% of revenue, its highest ever; optical fibre cable brought in ₹105.6 crore, 14%, down from ₹115.2 crore, and three-quarters of that OFC revenue was export (derived from [15]). The directors describe FY26 as a year of prolonged weakness in the OFC market at home and abroad, carried by LAN-cable volume, and FY27 as showing early signs of an OFC recovery [15]. Then came the June quarter: revenue up 51% and profit before tax of ₹41.08 crore against ₹1.84 crore, more in three months than in the whole of FY26 (₹22.59 crore) [14]. BCL gives no product split in its quarterly results and issues no press release, so how much of that jump is fibre pricing, how much LAN volume and how much inventory gain is not disclosed. One quarter is not a run-rate, and none is assumed here.

Vindhya Telelinks — an EPC contractor with a cable plant and a share portfolio

Three-quarters of VTL’s revenue is EPC, and EPC is where its problems are. Revenue fell 12% in FY26 and another 21% in Q1 FY27 because state payments under the Uttar Pradesh Jal Jeevan Mission water programme slowed: receivables on that one programme were ₹956 crore at 31 December 2025, ₹706 crore at 31 March 2026 and ₹733 crore at 30 June 2026 [11][12][13]. Total trade receivables were ₹2,163.9 crore at 31 March 2026, about 221 days of FY26 revenue (derived), operating cash flow was negative in both FY25 (−₹590.3 crore) and FY26 (−₹168.4 crore), and borrowings rose to fund it [12]. The December 2025 quarter, the last one inside the valuation cut-off, was a standalone loss of ₹19.7 crore [12]. Against that, the cable segment is in the same upswing as BCL: its segment result nearly tripled to ₹39.5 crore in Q1 FY27 on flat revenue [10], and management attributes this to a richer mix with more optical fibre cable [11]. Most of VTL’s consolidated profit is neither: ₹60.5 crore of the ₹101.5 crore consolidated profit before tax in Q1 FY27 was its share of UCL, Birla Corporation and Punjab Produce Holdings [10].

Universal Cables — the power-cable company that owns the fibre

UCL is the largest of the three by market value and the only one with no part in the scheme. Its Q1 FY27 was its highest first-quarter revenue on record, driven by extra-high-voltage cable, capacitors (+63%) and exports (₹120 crore, 12.7% of revenue), and it is guiding to revenue growth of about 25% or more for FY27 [16]. It matters to this note for two reasons. It is the largest single shareholder of VTL, so it votes as a promoter, and its BCL shares are swapped at the same ratio as everyone else’s. And it holds 26.14% of Birla Furukawa Fibre Optics, the joint venture with Furukawa Electric that draws the optical fibre itself, which UCL says had its most profitable year since inception in FY26 and whose partners approved a roughly USD 500 million expansion on 7 August 2026 [16][17]. UCL’s 26.14% share of that venture’s FY26 profit was ₹19.9 crore [17]. The group’s most direct exposure to fibre scarcity is therefore in a company the merger does not include.

The Fibre and Cable Demand Backdrop

The most likely reason BCL’s profit jumped, and the reason the ratio now looks stale, is the optical-fibre cycle. Two kinds of source are used: CRU, the commodity-price agency that publishes the industry’s fibre price index, as independent data; and the three companies’ FY26 annual reports and August 2026 releases, as the companies’ own statements.

ClaimWhat the source saysSource
Fibre prices have had a step changeCRU’s global index for standard G.652.D bare fibre hit a record low of 78.6 in March 2025 after 32 straight months of decline, stood at 88.7 in November 2025 and 107.9 in January 2026, and reached 263.0 in March 2026. Which side of the fibre price each company is on

A rising bare-fibre price is revenue for a fibre maker and a cost for a cable maker. BCL and VTL’s cable division buy fibre and sell cable: BCL’s annual report lists optical fibre among the raw materials whose price it is exposed to [15], and VTL says it is relatively insulated by a long-term supply agreement [13]. The company that sells fibre is Birla Furukawa Fibre Optics, which belongs 26.14% to UCL [17]. A cable maker’s margin widens in a shortage only while cable prices run ahead of its fibre cost, which is easiest when fibre is contracted or already in stock. Neither BCL nor VTL discloses its fibre purchase terms, so how much of the Q1 FY27 margin is repeatable and how much is a timing gain on supply bought earlier could not be established.

The timing against the valuation is awkward. The valuers’ financial cut-off was 31 December 2025 and their report is dated 21 March 2026 [4]. CRU’s index was 107.9 in January 2026 and 263.0 in March 2026 [29], and CRU had published on the preform squeeze on 26 February 2026 [28]. The step change in fibre prices was therefore under way before the boards met. Whether management’s projections, which show BCL’s revenue growing about 10% a year [4], took it into account is not disclosed.

The data-centre link, stated no more strongly than the filings do

All three companies name hyperscale and AI data centres as a driver of the fibre cycle [11][15][16]. What they disclose about their own exposure is thinner. VTL lists data-centre system integrators among the customer types its cable division serves, and on 23 May 2026 it raised the outlay on speciality optical fibre cable capacity at Rewa from ₹36.70 crore to ₹101.70 crore, naming hyperscale and cloud data centres among the target markets, with capacity due in phases during FY27 [1][12]. Neither VTL nor BCL discloses any revenue, order or customer from data centres, BCL’s FY26 revenue was 86% copper LAN and telecom cable [15], and the capacity VTL is building has not yet produced a reported rupee. The honest reading is that these companies benefit from a tight fibre market that data-centre demand helped create, mainly through better pricing and mix on the telecom and export cable they already sell. They are not data-centre suppliers on the evidence of their own filings.

The timing check, as the house rules require

Does the reported quarter contain the catalyst? Yes, and that is the problem for the ratio. Fibre prices turned in late 2025 and stepped up between January and March 2026 [28][29], but the effect on these companies’ reported profit comes after the valuation cut-off of 31 December 2025: BCL’s directors still describe FY26 as a weak OFC year [4][15]. The effect is inside Q1 FY27, the only reported quarter that contains it and the quarter that moved BCL’s price. The next test is Q2 FY27, which the listing regulations require within 45 days of the quarter-end, that is by 14 November 2026; Q1 was reported on 2 and 7 August [9]. If BCL’s margin holds, the distance between the ratio and the market hardens; if it was a one-quarter pricing gain, the gap can close from BCL’s side.

Scenarios

Three paths, with the evidence that would move the reading between them and the dates on which it can arrive. Likelihood is stated in words; no percentages are invented.

A — Completes as proposed, 10 for 115
The companies file at NCLT Indore before 14 February 2027, the Tribunal orders meetings, a majority of public votes cast in each company is in favour, and the scheme is sanctioned [3][8]. For that to be consistent with market prices, the 77% gap has to close first, because a holder can sell in the market rather than accept the swap: BCL falls toward VTL ÷ 11.5, VTL rises toward BCL × 11.5, or both. It closed once before, on 27 May 2026, from VTL’s side [19]. What would point here: an NCLT filing with the ratio unchanged; a Q2 FY27 result in which BCL’s margin falls back toward FY26 levels; the gap narrowing ahead of the vote. VTL’s public float includes one mutual fund with 8.22% and group-linked societies, classed as public but acting in concert with a promoter, with 10.59% at 30 June 2026; in BCL’s public float the group-linked societies hold 0.30% and all institutions together 0.03% [18]. The documents support this path; the market price, today, does not.
B — Delayed or amended
The boards revisit the ratio, or the process slows while they decide. The scheme lets either board agree modifications, SEBI’s written consent is needed for any change not ordered by a regulator or the Tribunal, and a new ratio would need fresh valuation work on newer financials and, in substance, a return to the exchanges [3][8]. What would point here: no NCLT filing as February 2027 approaches; a board meeting called to consider the scheme; an addendum to the valuation report, which SEBI’s comments expressly contemplate [8]; shareholder or proxy-adviser objection to the ratio once the notice is out. Plausible — it is the path on which the ratio and the market are reconciled by the companies rather than by the price. A better ratio for BCL is a worse one for VTL, whose public shareholders vote too.
C — Fails or is withdrawn
BCL’s public shareholders vote it down, or a board withdraws it under the clause that allows withdrawal where the scheme would have an adverse implication for the company [3]. BCL then remains listed and is valued on its own earnings; VTL keeps its 19.33% stake, worth ₹237.5 crore at the 18 September close, as one more listed holding. What would point here: the gap staying wide into the vote; turnout among BCL’s 46,829 small individual holders, who own 25.26% of the company out of a 33.65% public float (30 June 2026) [18]; the observation letter lapsing on 14 February 2027 without a filing [8]. Cannot be ruled out: on 18 September 2026 a BCL public shareholder voting in favour would be voting to exchange ₹409.50 for ₹231.30 (derived from [19]).

The pivot between A and the other two is one number that anyone can compute daily: BCL’s close divided by VTL’s close, against 1 ÷ 11.5 = 0.0870. On 18 September 2026 it was 0.1539. The pivot between B and C is whether the boards act before the public shareholders have to.

A court order in that dispute could bear on who votes the promoter stakes, on the subsidiaries’ holdings, or on the scheme itself. SEBI has asked that all pending actions against the companies, promoters and directors be disclosed to shareholders and the NCLT [8].

After the scheme, listed group holdings are about 99% of VTL’s pro forma market value at the current price (derived, §10). The UCL–VTL cross-holding stays at about 30% each way and Birla Corporation joins VTL’s register [1][18]. VTL’s price is therefore tied to UCL and Birla Corporation as much as to cables, and the discount that narrowed from 55% to 17% between March and September 2026 (derived, with operating businesses at nil) can widen again.
08
Timetable and process
The observation letter lapses on 14 February 2027 if the scheme is not before the NCLT by then (derived from [8]). After that come Tribunal-directed meetings, lender no-objections from at least 75% of secured creditors by value, regulatory notices and a sanction hearing, none with a date [3][8]. With an appointed date of 1 April 2026, every month of delay is a month in which BCL’s results are reported separately but belong, if the scheme completes, to the merged company at the old ratio [3].
09
The fibre cycle turning
CRU’s fibre index fell for 32 consecutive months to a record low in March 2025 before more than tripling by March 2026 [29]; the companies themselves describe fibre as historically cyclical, and the group’s joint venture is adding capacity into 2028 [13][16]. For BCL and VTL’s cable division fibre is a purchased input, so the squeeze can turn from a margin gain into a cost if cable prices stop outrunning it [15]. BCL’s report also flags energy, freight, helium and preform costs from the Middle East conflict as risks to contracts and tenders [15]. A softer fibre market would hit BCL’s margin, VTL’s cable segment and the joint venture’s profit together.

Summary

VERDICT: MERGER NOTE — THE DEAL IS REAL, THE MARKET PRICE IS NOT THE RATIO; NOT A RECOMMENDATION

The merger the request described exists. On 21 March 2026 the boards of Birla Cable and Vindhya Telelinks approved a scheme under which Birla Cable is absorbed into Vindhya Telelinks and dissolved, with 10 VTL shares issued for every 115 BCL shares, an appointed date of 1 April 2026, and VTL’s own 19.33% of BCL cancelled [1][2][3]. Two registered valuers and SBI Capital Markets stand behind the ratio [4][5]. NSE and BSE issued their observation letters on 14 August 2026 after SEBI’s comments, and no complaint was recorded [7][8]. Universal Cables is not a party. The NCLT has not yet been approached, and each company’s public shareholders still have to approve it by majority [3][8][9].

The terms were set on one set of facts and are being judged on another. The valuers worked from financials to 31 December 2025 and from projections of about 10% annual growth for BCL. They valued VTL at 57–60% above its market price and BCL at 13–15% above its own, which turned a market ratio of 8.2 into a scheme ratio of 11.5 [4][19]. Since then BCL has reported a quarter in which its profit before tax exceeded VTL’s standalone figure [10][14], and the market has repriced it. At the 18 September 2026 closes BCL stands 77.0% above what the swap pays, the widest gap of the 123 sessions since the announcement [19].

The structure is only slightly simpler afterwards. One listed company and one cross-holding go. UCL and VTL still own about 30% of each other, the VTL group still owns 31.68% of Birla Corporation, and the filing’s arithmetic implies Birla Corporation becomes a roughly 3.4% public shareholder of VTL [1][13][18]. For BCL’s holders a holding-company discount is created, not unwound: after the scheme, listed group shares make up about 99% of VTL’s pro forma market value [19]. Nearly half of those shares sit in three subsidiaries that VTL could not consolidate for five years [10][12].

The businesses are in different places. BCL and VTL’s cable segment are both in a fibre upturn that began after the valuation cut-off; VTL’s EPC business, three-quarters of its revenue, is shrinking, cash-negative and owed ₹733 crore by one state programme [10][11][12]. The group’s fibre maker, where scarcity shows up most directly, belongs 26.14% to UCL and is outside the merger [16][17]. None of the three discloses revenue from data centres.

What settles it is observable. Either the gap closes before the vote, or the boards change the ratio, or BCL’s public shareholders are asked to approve an exchange that costs them 43.5% at today’s prices. This note carries no rating; the readings it surfaces are the framework’s own and are not a recommendation on any of the three.
