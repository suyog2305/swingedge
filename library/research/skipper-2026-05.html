Skipper is among the world's leading manufacturers of power transmission & distribution (T&D) structures — lattice towers, monopoles, distribution poles, and angles — supplied to utilities such as Power Grid Corporation (PGCIL), state transmission companies, and international clients. The company runs three lines of business: a dominant Engineering Products division (towers and structures), an Infrastructure / EPC arm (turnkey transmission lines and substations), and a Polymer division (PVC and HDPE pipes & fittings).

The investment case rests on a simple structural fact: India is in the middle of a multi-year transmission build-out, with the National Electricity Plan targeting more than 50,000 circuit-kilometres of new transmission lines over the next five years, alongside renewable-evacuation corridors, HVDC links and grid-strengthening. Skipper sits at the towers & EPC stage of that value chain — a structurally cheaper entry point than the equipment names, with similar end-demand exposure.

House view: Within our grid-infrastructure coverage, Skipper is a Tier-1 "pure bottleneck play at a reasonable forward multiple" — the cleanest listed proxy for the tower/overhead-line leg of the T&D capex cycle, trading well below the 30–70x multiples of the HVDC equipment trio.

Where Skipper Sits in the T&D Value Chain

Every transmission corridor flows through the same physical sequence. Skipper occupies the towers & EPC stage — the point with lower margins than equipment manufacture, but with the broadest, most repeatable demand and the lowest technology-obsolescence risk.

Skipper straddles Stage 03 (its core, high-share business) and Stage 04 (EPC). It is not directly exposed to the HVDC converter-equipment bottleneck — that is the domain of Hitachi Energy, Siemens Energy and GE Vernova T&D — but it is a direct beneficiary of the volume of lines those corridors require. The trade-off is margin: tower fabrication is closer to a processing/engineering business (mid-single-digit to low-double-digit EBITDA margins) than the 15–22% margins of equipment OEMs.

The record ₹8,502 Cr backlog is the single strongest pillar of the thesis: at ~1.5x FY26 revenue, it underwrites roughly 18 months of execution and removes most near-term revenue risk. The ₹33,000 Cr bidding pipeline is the optionality — even a modest win-rate sustains the order book through FY28. The strategically important shift is the rising EPC and exports mix, which carries better realisations than plain tower supply.

The risk inside the strength: the book is ~90% domestic and heavily PGCIL/state-utility linked. That concentrates Skipper on the pace and tendering discipline of Indian transmission ordering — a tailwind today, but a single-customer-type dependency to watch.

Capacity Expansion

Skipper's growth is capacity-led — output scales with installed tonnage, which is why the capex roadmap matters as much as the order book. The company added ~75,000 MTPA in Q1 FY26 and is on a path to materially expand engineering capacity.

By ~Jun 2026 (target)~4,50,000 MTPANear-term debottlenecking + brownfield additions
By FY28 (aspiration)~6,00,000 MTPAWould rank Skipper among / as the world's largest tower manufacturer by capacity

Capacity figures are management aspirations sourced from disclosures/concall commentary; phasing depends on capex execution and demand absorption.

The strategic logic is sound — capacity is the binding constraint in a demand-rich environment, and being a low-cost, high-volume supplier at scale is a real moat in tower fabrication. The financial tension is equally clear: capacity growth requires capex, and tower margins do not self-fund expansion at the same rate as the order book grows. This keeps Skipper modestly leveraged and makes return on capital and working-capital discipline the variables that separate a good outcome from a great one.

Its edge is cost, scale of tonnage, galvanising integration and a long track record of high-voltage qualification; its limitation is that tower fabrication has modest pricing power and the EPC business pits it against far larger balance sheets.

Balance Sheet & Working Capital

This is where the scrutiny belongs. Skipper's profit growth is strong, but capital efficiency and the cash-conversion cycle are the structural watch-items.

Return on Equity (3-yr avg)~13.7% — modest for the valuation; the key metric the re-rating depends on improving
Debtor DaysIncreased from ~79 to ~98 days — a deterioration driven by EPC mix and project-billing cycles
Operating Cash Flow (FY26)₹277.4 Cr, up from ₹153.3 Cr — encouraging, partly offsets the receivables drag
LeverageModestly geared; capacity expansion is partly debt-funded, so interest cost as a % of revenue is a monitorable
P/B~3.4–4.3x — full relative to a ~14% ROE, which is why ROE improvement is central to the thesis

The tension is straightforward. A capacity-led, project-heavy business with rising receivables needs working capital to grow, and tower margins don't generate enough internal cash to fund both expansion and a lengthening cycle without leverage. FY26's near-doubling of operating cash flow is the right direction; sustaining it while debtor days normalise is what would justify a higher multiple. Until then, the ~14% ROE caps how aggressively the stock can re-rate.

Growth Drivers & Management Guidance

Management Guidance (Q4 FY26 Concall)

  • FY27 revenue growth ~15% — explicitly moderated down from the prior ~20%, with geopolitical/export frictions cited as the reason for caution.
  • FY27 PAT growth ~30% — margin expansion and operating leverage are expected to drive profit faster than revenue.
  • Long-term EBITDA margin target ~12% — with margin improvement expected through FY27 (vs a historically mid-single-digit to ~10% range).
  • FY28+ : return to 20–25% growth — predicated on the export build-out (Brazil/UAE/US) and conversion of the ₹33,000 Cr bidding pipeline.

Structural Demand Drivers

  • India transmission build-out: 50,000+ ckm of new lines planned over five years; renewable-evacuation corridors and grid-strengthening drive sustained tower demand.
  • EHV / HVDC mix-up: Higher-voltage lines carry richer realisations; Skipper's ~5,000 ckm of EHV/HVDC execution and composite cross-arm capability move it up the value curve.
  • Exports as the second engine: Localised subsidiaries to win international tenders — the swing factor for the FY28 re-acceleration, currently held back by trade frictions.
  • Polymer scale-up: A higher-margin diversifier that reduces dependence on government tower ordering as it scales.
The guidance cut from 20% to 15% revenue growth is the most important single data point of the quarter. It is not a demand-collapse signal — the order book is at a record — but it tempers the near-term growth narrative and is the reason this report sets a measured "Accumulate," not an aggressive "Buy."

Investment Summary & Recommendation

Skipper is the cleanest listed proxy for the tower and overhead-line leg of India's transmission build-out — a capacity-led, low-cost manufacturer entering FY27 with a record ₹8,502 Cr order book, a ₹33,000 Cr bidding pipeline, and a demonstrated FY26 in which profit and cash flow grew roughly twice as fast as revenue. It is structurally cheaper than the HVDC equipment trio while sharing the same end-demand. The case is not without friction: a guidance cut from 20% to 15% revenue growth, debtor days stretching to ~98, and a ~14% ROE against a full price-to-book all temper the enthusiasm. This is a quality grid-capex compounder to accumulate on weakness rather than chase — the order book de-risks the business, but the valuation already discounts much of the margin and capital-efficiency improvement that management has guided to but not yet delivered.

The Three Reasons to Own SKIPPER

  • 1. Record, visible order book: ₹8,502 Cr (~1.5x revenue, ~18 months) underwrites near-term execution; the ₹33,000 Cr pipeline is the optionality for FY28.
  • 2. Cheap exposure to a real capex super-cycle: 50,000+ ckm of planned transmission lines, EHV/HVDC mix-up, and a world-scale capacity roadmap — at a meaningful discount to equipment-OEM multiples.
  • 3. Operating-leverage proof point: FY26 PAT +42% on +20% revenue and operating cash flow up ~81% — evidence the business can convert volume into profit, if working capital is contained.
