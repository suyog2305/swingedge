Ola Electric is India's most vertically integrated electric two-wheeler company and, until early 2025, its largest. It designs and builds its own vehicles, its own motors and battery packs, and — uniquely among listed Indian E2W makers — its own lithium-ion cells. The company sells the S1 scooter family (S1 X through S1 Pro+) and the Roadster series of electric motorcycles, and has extended into home energy storage under the Ola Shakti brand.

The investment question is not whether the technology works. It is whether the company can sell enough of it. Ola's own stated breakeven threshold is 20,000–25,000 vehicles per month. Its best month in the last fifteen was 16,183 registrations (June 2026), and July 2026 fell back to ~14,100. Everything in this report — the margins, the burn, the cell ramp, the funding runway — sits downstream of that single number.

House view: this report is built deliberately around Vaahan registration data rather than company-disclosed "sales", because Ola's own volume disclosures have been the subject of a SEBI review after a February-2025 filing claimed 25,000+ units against roughly 5,341 Vaahan registrations in the same month. Registrations are the number a buyer of this equity should underwrite.

03 —

Where Ola Sits in the E2W Value Chain

Ola's strategic bet was to own every stage of the electric two-wheeler chain — cells, powertrain, vehicle, and the customer relationship. On paper this is the most integrated position in the Indian industry. In practice, the company is strongest exactly where the market is least being decided, and weakest where it is.

Ola dominates Stages 01–03 and is the only listed Indian E2W maker with an operating cell plant. But the Indian electric two-wheeler market in 2025–26 has been won at Stage 04. TVS, Bajaj and Hero arrived with decades-old dealer and service networks already paid for; Ather has methodically doubled its service network to 500 centres. Ola built 4,000 direct outlets in a rush, discovered that a little over 100 of ~3,400 had the trade certificates required under the Motor Vehicles Act, and has spent the last eighteen months dismantling them.

The uncomfortable structural read: vertical integration is a cost advantage that only pays off at volume, while distribution is a revenue advantage that determines whether volume happens at all. Ola optimised the half of the chain that requires scale to matter, and under-built the half that creates scale.

Vaahan Registrations & Market Share — The Spine of the Thesis

India's Vaahan portal records every vehicle actually registered with an RTO. It is the only volume series for this sector that cannot be presented as an order, a booking, or a "confirmed customer intent". For Ola in particular, that distinction is not academic: SEBI is reviewing a February-2025 exchange filing in which the company described 25,000+ units, against roughly 5,341 Vaahan registrations and revenue recognised on 2,848 units in the same month. Ola has since filed a settlement application. Whatever the outcome, the practical consequence for an analyst is simple — use Vaahan.

How to Read the 2026 Sequence Honestly

There are two defensible readings of the 2026 monthly series, and an even-handed report should state both.

  • The bull reading: February 2026 (3,968 units, ~3.5% share) was the bottom, caused by a store shutdown, a registration-process breakdown and a collapse in customer confidence. From there Ola went 10,117 → 12,171 → 15,229 → 16,183 — a fourfold recovery in four months. Q1 FY27 registrations rose 97% QoQ against 17% industry growth, and share recovered from 5.1% to 8.4%. The Gen-3 platform, the Bharat cell and the warranty reset are real; warranty cost fell from over ₹500 Cr in FY25 to ₹59 Cr in FY26. On that trajectory the 20,000-unit breakeven line is one or two good quarters away.
  • The bear reading: the recovery stalled in June and reversed in July (share 8.3% → 6.9%) and August month-to-date is tracking materially below June. The Q1 FY27 "97% QoQ growth" is measured off the worst quarter in the company's listed history — 22,252 registrations in Q4 FY26 — and Q1 FY27's 43,908 is still down 25% year-on-year. Every peer grew 70–175% YoY in the same quarter. Ola has now not gained a percentage point of annual share in three calendar years.
The single fact that settles the argument for now: Ola's best month in the last fifteen (16,183 in June 2026) was still below the bottom of its own stated breakeven band of 20,000–25,000 units. The company has never, since its share collapse began, produced a month that would have made it profitable.
05 —

Distribution, Service & the Network Reset

The volume story in Section 04 has a physical cause. Between 2022 and 2024 Ola opened stores faster than it could licence, staff or supply them — famously 3,200 in a single day. The consequences arrived in 2025 and are still being worked through.

PhaseOutletsWhat Happened
Peak build-out (2022–24)~4,000Direct-to-consumer land grab. Of roughly 3,400 outlets for which data was available, a little over 100 held the trade certificate required under the Motor Vehicles Act.
Regulatory reckoning (2025)ClosuresMaharashtra RTOs inspected 146 stores; ~121 (≈83%) lacked a valid trade certificate. 75 showrooms were shut and 192 vehicles seized. Separately, the CCPA logged 10,000+ consumer complaints between Sep 2023 and Aug 2024 and issued a show-cause notice under the Consumer Protection Act, 2019.
Structural reset (2025–26)~700Warehouse model abandoned in favour of a retail model; network cut to ~700 as part of a declared "structural reset". Cost savings of roughly ₹90 Cr per month were claimed from the broader cost programme.
Current target~550A further ~150 closures announced in February 2026, targeting ~550 outlets. Remaining company stores are to become brand experience centres; service to be consolidated into "Hyperservice Centres", starting in Bengaluru.
Strategic reversal (2026)Dealer-ledOla is now moving from D2C to independent dealer partners handling local sales, service and geographic expansion — an explicit acknowledgement that "technology may change the vehicle, but it does not necessarily eliminate the importance of automotive distribution."

Two things are true at once here. The reset was necessary: an unlicensed, undersupplied, unserviceable network was destroying the brand faster than any product could rebuild it, and the FY26 warranty collapse from ₹500 Cr+ to ₹59 Cr suggests the quality problem is genuinely being fixed. But the reset is also the mechanism of the share loss. Going from 4,000 points of sale to 550 while Ather doubles to 500 service centres and TVS/Bajaj/Hero deploy networks measured in thousands is not a neutral trade.

The dealer pivot is the right decision arriving late. It converts a fixed-cost problem into a variable-cost one and buys reach Ola cannot fund directly — but it means the next twelve months of volume depend on persuading independent dealers to back a brand whose share has fallen for three straight years. Dealer sign-ups are the leading indicator to watch, ahead of monthly registrations.

Runway Arithmetic — Author's Estimate

Taking the Q1 FY27 run-rate at face value: operating burn of ~₹215 Cr plus maintenance and cell-ramp capex of, conservatively, ₹100–150 Cr per quarter gives total cash consumption of roughly ₹315–365 Cr per quarter. On that basis the ₹780 Cr QIP alone funds a little over two quarters. Even if the burn halves as volumes recover, the company is likely to need either another equity raise or a further debt facility inside FY28 unless it crosses breakeven first.

Goldman Sachs, which has been constructive on the FY27 EBITDA-breakeven path, has nonetheless flagged that free cash flow remains negative and that no material cell revenue is expected in Q2 FY27. Both the bull and the bear on this stock agree on the mechanism: breakeven or dilution. They disagree only on which arrives first.

One genuine positive deserves equal weight. The promoter pledge overhang — a recurring source of forced-selling anxiety through 2025, when Aggarwal's pledged portion rose from ~8.1% to ~13% of his stake as the stock fell — has been fully cleared. In December 2025 he monetised a small part of his holding and released all ₹260 Cr of promoter-level pledges, covering ~3.93% of shares, and the company has indicated no further promoter pledges are expected. That removes one of the three ways this equity could have unravelled.

09 —

Platform, Cells & Management Guidance

Gen 3 and the 4680 Bharat Cell

  • Gen 3 platform: unveiled 31 January 2025 — mid-drive motor, chain drive, integrated motor control unit. Portfolio spans S1 X 2 kWh at ₹79,999 to S1 Pro+ 5.3 kWh at ₹1,69,999 (introductory prices). The FY26 warranty collapse from ₹500 Cr+ to ₹59 Cr is the best available evidence that Gen 3 fixed the reliability problem that destroyed the brand.
  • 4680 Bharat Cell: India's first indigenous large-format cell in mass production. ARAI-certified; mass deliveries began November 2025 with the S1 Pro+. Company states thousands of vehicles on 4680 cells have covered millions of real-world kilometres.
  • Gigafactory: ~6 GWh of capacity installation largely complete, of which ~2.5 GWh operational as of the Q4 FY26 update — against an original aspiration of 20 GWh by 2026. The gap between the 2024 roadmap and the 2026 reality is the single clearest illustration of this company's execution-versus-ambition pattern.
  • 46100 LFP cell: declared ready under Regulation 30; larger than the current NMC 4680 and slated for product integration "starting next quarter", subject to regulatory approvals. LFP is the cheaper, safer, longer-cycle chemistry — the right choice for both mass-market scooters and stationary storage.
  • Gen 3 Upgrade Programme: lets Gen 1 and Gen 2 owners switch to Bharat-cell S1 scooters and Roadster motorcycles — a direct attempt to convert an installed base of unhappy early customers into repeat buyers.

Key Risk Factors

01
Market Share Erosion
HIGH
Share has fallen from ~33.8% of CY2024 volumes to ~15.4% in CY2025 to ~7% CY2026-to-date, and from 18.7% in June 2025 to 6.9% in July 2026. Ola has lost share in a market that grew 68–75% YoY. This is the master risk from which every other risk in this table follows.
02
Funding & Dilution
HIGH
Negative reserves (−₹1,060 Cr), ₹2,763 Cr of gross borrowings, ~₹215 Cr quarterly operating burn plus capex. The ₹780 Cr QIP covers roughly two quarters at the current run-rate. If breakeven volume is not reached, another raise — likely dilutive and likely at a depressed price — becomes necessary within FY28.
03
Distribution Transition Execution
HIGH
The network is being cut from ~4,000 outlets to ~550 while simultaneously pivoting from D2C to independent dealers. Both moves are correct in principle; together they mean the entire FY27–28 volume plan depends on third parties choosing to invest behind a brand in decline.
04
Disclosure Quality & Regulatory
MED–HIGH
SEBI is reviewing the February-2025 sales disclosure (25,000+ claimed vs ~5,341 Vaahan registrations, revenue recognised on 2,848 units) and has reportedly examined insider-trading allegations from Oct–Dec 2024. A settlement application has been filed. Separately, the CCPA show-cause notice under the Consumer Protection Act and the state trade-certificate actions remain live reputational and operational exposures.
05
Competitive Pricing from Profitable Incumbents
MED–HIGH
TVS (~₹1.72 lakh Cr market cap) and Bajaj (~₹2.48 lakh Cr) can fund EV share gains from ICE profit pools indefinitely. Ola cannot match a price war it has to finance from equity raises. The ₹60,000 Roadster price cut shows the direction of travel on realisation.
06
Cell Ramp Delay / Capital Sunk
MEDIUM
Original roadmap: 6.4 GWh by 2025, 20 GWh by 2026. Actual: ~6 GWh installed, ~2.5 GWh operational. ₹3,352 Cr of fixed assets sits substantially underutilised, and Goldman Sachs expects no material cell revenue in Q2 FY27. Vertical integration only pays at volume Ola does not currently have.
07
Gross Margin Sustainability
MEDIUM
Gross margin stepped down from 38.5% in Q4 FY26 (33.5% ex-PLI) to 30.5% in Q1 FY27. With mix shifting toward cheaper S1 X variants and commodity headwinds flagged by management, the margin gains that underpin the breakeven maths are not yet proven durable.
08
Policy / Subsidy Dependence
MEDIUM
PLI benefits materially flattered Q4 FY26 gross margin (38.5% reported vs 33.5% ex-PLI). Changes to PLI, FAME-successor schemes or GST treatment of EVs would hit Ola's economics harder than better-capitalised peers.
09
Key-Person Concentration
LOW–MED
Strategy, capital allocation and brand are tightly bound to Bhavish Aggarwal, whose attention also spans Krutrim and Ola Consumer. Mitigating: promoter pledges were fully released in Dec 2025 and promoter holding remains a substantial ~33%, which aligns incentives.

Investment Summary & Recommendation

Ola Electric is a company whose cost problem is being solved and whose demand problem is not. Gross margin has more than doubled in five quarters, warranty cost has fallen ~88%, opex is down 22% QoQ, and the loss has narrowed for two consecutive quarters — genuine, measurable operational repair. But the Vaahan record is unambiguous: share has gone from ~33.8% of CY2024 volumes to ~15.4% in CY2025 to ~7% so far in CY2026, and in June 2026 — the single best month the Indian E2W industry has ever had, up 75.5% YoY — Ola's registrations fell 21.8% year-on-year while every top-four rival set a record. TVS has now overtaken Ola even on cumulative lifetime registrations. Management's own breakeven threshold is 20,000–25,000 units a month; Ola's best month in the last fifteen was 16,183, and July fell back to ~14,100. At ~₹38 the market already pays ~9.7x annualised sales for a business with a ~−43% operating margin, when Ather trades at ~12x with a −2.7% margin and rising volume. The bull case is real and large — a market growing 60–75% a year could carry a share-stable Ola to breakeven without it winning anything. But it has to stop losing share first, and it has not. Avoid at this price; watch the monthly Vaahan print, not the guidance.

Three Reasons Someone Could Reasonably Own OLAELEC

  • 1. The market does the work: Indian E2W registrations grew 68–75% YoY through mid-2026 and penetration hit a record 11.24% in July. If Ola merely holds ~8% share, industry growth alone can carry it to the 20,000–25,000/month breakeven band without any share gain at all.
  • 2. The cost reset is verifiable, not narrative: gross margin 13.7% → 30.5%, warranty ₹500 Cr+ → ₹59 Cr, opex −22% QoQ, loss narrowing two quarters running, and one operating-cash-flow-positive quarter already delivered (Q4 FY26, +₹91 Cr). The auto business reportedly turned EBITDA-positive in June 2026.
  • 3. Assets nobody else has, at a discount: the only operating in-house cell plant among listed Indian E2W makers (~2.5 GWh live of ~6 GWh installed), a 46100 LFP cell ready for integration, an energy-storage optionality in Ola Shakti — and a market cap per unit of monthly volume (~₹1.25 Cr) roughly 35% below Ather's.
