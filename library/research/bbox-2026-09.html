Black Box is a genuine, name-checked participant in the AI data-centre build-out — a record order backlog, a named new Tier-1 US hyperscaler win, guidance that leans explicitly on hyperscale demand. It is also a forty-year-old systems-integration business with high-single-digit EBITDA margins, a controlling promoter, a documented mid-year guidance cut inside the last twelve months, and no single disclosed figure for what share of revenue is actually AI-linked. Both things are true at once, and this report tries to hold them together rather than pick the more exciting one.

Black Box reports three segments. System Integration covers enterprise networking, data centres, digital workplace, connected buildings and cybersecurity, delivered as design-build, structured cabling, connectivity and managed-service engagements. Technology Product Solutions sells KVM switches, cabling, IoT and audio-video hardware. Others is training and consulting [5][10].

The Order Book, the Hyperscalers, and What Share Is Really AI

This is the section the whole report exists to write, and the honest answer is: the order book is real and growing fast, the hyperscaler wins are real and named-by-description, and there is no single clean, company-disclosed number for what share of revenue or backlog is AI-data-centre work. Two credible estimates exist, they measure different things, and they cannot be reconciled from what is public.

A press report at the time of the August hyperscaler order separately quoted the rupee-equivalent backlog at ~₹9,000 Cr [13], consistent with US$949M at prevailing rates.

The named proof points

In August 2026 Black Box announced a US$131 million (~₹1,240 Cr), three-year order from a new Tier-1 US hyperscaler for a data-centre build, alongside continued expansion with an existing, unnamed hyperscaler [7][13]. On its own, that single contract is roughly 14% of the entire US$949M backlog (derived) — a reminder that “record backlog” and “diversified backlog” are not the same claim. The same quarter also produced a large US connectivity and networking engagement with, in the company’s own description, “one of the world’s largest chip manufacturers” — unnamed, but plainly AI-supply-chain-adjacent [12]. Of the US$339M in new Q1 FY27 order bookings, management specifically attributed roughly US$80M (~₹757 Cr) to financial services, healthcare, public services and retail combined [12] — leaving roughly US$259M, or about 76% of the quarter’s new bookings, attributable to hyperscale/technology-sector demand by subtraction (derived, not a company-stated split).

Two estimates that will not reconcile

EstimateFigureBasis
Data-centre share of the order book~25%Secondary aggregator’s read of the Q4 FY26 concall, period to Mar 2026 [16]

Neither figure is one Black Box itself has published as a clean, dated disclosure in the sources this report could reach; both are secondary estimates, from different outlets, describing different periods and most likely different things. The 40–50%-of-revenue figure most plausibly covers the whole “data centre” solution line inside System Integration — enterprise, telco and colocation work that predates the AI cycle by years — while the ~25%-of-order-book figure may describe only the incremental, hyperscaler-flavoured share of new bookings. This report cannot verify that reading and does not assert it as fact; it names the most likely explanation for why two numbers this far apart both circulate, rather than picking one and discarding the other.

What can be stated with more confidence: the growth engine is real (backlog +83% YoY, a headline hyperscaler win, management explicitly framing hyperscale as a new growth engine alongside continued expansion with an existing one [7]), it sits entirely inside the System Integration segment with no separately disclosed margin, and management’s own characterisation — “the AI-led infrastructure cycle remains in its early stages,” citing 390-plus active projects across 55-plus North American players and colocation vacancy down to 2.3% [6] — is sourced to Black Box’s own market-research bundle rather than independently verified here, in the same way this desk’s own data-centre supply-chain research has flagged for the industry-wide India capacity forecasts these figures resemble [29].

How lumpy, and how exposed to a pause

This is not a hypothetical risk. In February 2026, at the Q3 FY26 call, management cut full-year FY26 revenue guidance by roughly 5–7% (~US$40–50M), attributing the shortfall explicitly to “industry-wide shortages of inputs like optical fibers and cables, particularly in the booming data center ecosystem,” which pushed execution and revenue recognition into the following year [15]. That is a direct, dated, company-attributed instance of the same AI-data-centre boom that is filling this order book also disrupting the physical inputs needed to deliver it — echoing the fibre-and-cable tightness this desk’s own supply-chain research has separately documented across STL, HFCL and the global producers [29]. Management’s Q1 FY27 remarks struck a more comfortable note — supply-chain conditions had “improved over the last quarter, particularly across cables and fiber, supporting faster project execution”, per the earnings-call transcript as carried in this desk’s own evidence base [29] — which is one buyer’s experience a quarter or two later, not proof the underlying tightness documented elsewhere has actually cleared.

Recurring, less lumpy revenue is the stated offset: managed services are roughly 30% of revenue today, with a US$382M managed-services and maintenance order book and a stated target of 40% near-term and 50% eventually [6]. That shift, if it happens, would make the revenue base steadier over time — but it is a target, not yet a result.

The record matters more than the target. Management guided boldly for FY26, cut that guidance by roughly a fourteenth of the original number when a data-centre-linked supply constraint bit, and then landed just under even the reduced bar. The FY27 targets are more aggressive on every line — a bigger percentage revenue jump, a much larger PAT jump, and a backlog target that assumes the hyperscale pipeline keeps converting at or above the current pace, all while absorbing a first full year of the Brazilian acquisition. None of that makes the FY27 guidance wrong. It does mean this is a management team with one recent, dated instance of promising more than the supply chain would let it deliver, and the FY30 aspiration — a doubling of revenue with $700M of acquisitions layered on top — is far enough out that almost none of it is independently checkable today.

The number that stands out most is cash conversion. FY26 operating cash flow was ₹84 Cr against reported PAT of ₹218 Cr [2] — roughly 39% of profit converting into operating cash in the year the company was telling the market its order book, and therefore its receivables and work-in-progress, was growing fastest. That is not on its own a red flag in a working-capital-intensive integration business with a backlog growing 83% year on year — growth of this kind mechanically consumes cash into receivables before it shows up as cash — but it is exactly the kind of gap this desk treats as a question rather than waving through, and it sits alongside a rising working-capital-days trend management itself has flagged as unresolved.

The ₹496.6 Cr 2S Inovações acquisition (§03), effective 1 May 2026, was funded through a mix management describes as “internal accruals plus selective debt,” with no large equity raise planned [16] — but that statement predates the deal closing, and neither the CRISIL debt figure (dated to fiscal 2026, i.e. on or before 31 March 2026) nor the Screener balance sheet used here reflects a full quarter of post-acquisition debt. How the acquisition was actually funded, and what it did to leverage, is a Q2 FY27 question this report cannot yet answer.

Warrants, dilution and the September 2026 AGM

In August 2024 the board approved a preferential issue of 98,32,123 fully convertible warrants at ₹417 each (₹410 Cr in aggregate). The conversion completed with 92,65,215 warrants converted for ₹386.36 Cr, of which Essar Telecom itself took 47,96,163 shares — roughly ₹200 Cr, or 51.76% of that tranche — lifting the promoter stake from 70.06% to 70.50% at the time [16][18]. A further, separately disclosed exchange filing records an allotment of 51.09 lakh shares for ₹213.07 Cr via warrant conversion [19]; this report could not reconcile that figure against the original 98.32-lakh warrant pool using the sources available, and states it as an individually disclosed tranche rather than forcing a single running total.

At its 16 September 2026 AGM, shareholders approved a capital reclassification that doubles authorised equity share capacity from 22.5 crore to 47.5 crore shares (face value ₹2), explicitly to increase equity issuance capacity for future fundraising flexibility, alongside a ₹1-per-share final dividend (record date 28 August, payout 23 September 2026) and the reappointment of Anshuman Ruia — a member of the family that controls Essar — as Executive Director by rotation [21]. No new issuance has been announced; this is headroom, not a raise, but it is fresh headroom created the same week as this report and belongs on the monitoring list in §13.

On related-party dealings specifically: CRISIL’s own rating rationale states that “no financial support is expected from the company to the group, and any such transactions will be monitorable” [17] — the rating agency flagging the Essar relationship as something to watch rather than reporting an adverse finding. This report could not extract a readable related-party-transaction schedule from the PDF disclosures on the company’s investor-relations site (they returned as encoded, digitally-signed binary content rather than readable text) and states that gap rather than guessing at what the disclosures contain.

09 —

What Actually Moved the Price, and Who Else Is in This Trade

The year in three moves

Black Box set an all-time high of ₹1,104.30 [1] on 5 June 2026 [24] — four days after its 1 June Capital Markets Day, at which the FY30 targets in §05 were first laid out [29]. The stock is up 72% over the trailing year and 59% over six months [1], both genuinely large numbers; it is also down 27% from that June high as of this report’s 18 September close [1], a serious, live drawdown sitting inside the same twelve months as the headline gain.

Three explanations are attested, none a single clean trigger: (a) broader Nifty IT weakness through 2026 on FII selling and worries about AI-driven pricing deflation across the sector [25]; (b) valuation compression from a reported ~58x trailing multiple near the highs toward today’s still-elevated level in §08 [25]; and (c) the stock’s own surveillance history. Black Box carried Short-Term ASM into early June and then Long-Term ASM Stage 1 from 4 June to 4 September 2026 [22] — a surveillance flag of one stage or another for almost the entire window between the all-time high and two weeks before this report, which would have raised margin requirements and commonly excluded the stock from broker MTF lists across exactly the period it was falling. As of 19–20 September 2026 it carries no ASM or GSM flag [22][23], and this report did not identify a single dated announcement, order cancellation or guidance cut coinciding with the start of the decline — the correction looks like a valuation and surveillance unwind layered on sector weakness, not a reaction to new negative company news.

Peers, honestly

Data providers bucket Black Box against TCS, Infosys, HCLTech, Wipro and Tech Mahindra on sector code alone [26]. That comparison is close to useless. Those are large-cap software and services franchises with double-digit-to-high-30s trailing earnings multiples built on 20%-plus operating margins [26]; Black Box is a hardware-linked systems integrator earning high-single-digit margins on physical build-and-connect work. Putting them in the same table answers a screener query, not an investment question.

On a same-methodology cross-check this desk has used before: Black Box’s market cap of ~₹14,241 Cr against a ~₹9,000 Cr rupee-equivalent order book is roughly 1.6x market cap to order book (derived) — in the same broad range as HFCL’s 1.44x when this desk covered it in August 2026, a stock that was itself carrying an ASM flag and a similarly order-book-driven re-rating [32]. That is a reference point, not a verdict: the two sit in different parts of the same AI-infrastructure supply chain, and this report has not re-underwritten HFCL’s numbers here.

10 —

Valuation

Current multiples, moving-average distances and returns are generated into §08 and not repeated here. Return on capital is genuinely strong — ROE 25.7%, ROCE 21.6% [2] — for a business earning high-single-digit EBITDA margins, which says the asset base is being turned over efficiently rather than that the margin itself is unusually rich.

This report could not obtain a multi-year trailing-multiple history for Black Box in the sources fetched, so no claim is made here about whether today’s multiple is rich or cheap against its own history — that is stated as a gap rather than estimated. Against the sector-bucketed “peers” in §09 the multiple looks unremarkable; against Netweb’s ~132x it looks cheap; on a PEG of 0.62 [1] it looks reasonable if the growth rate embedded in that calculation is trusted to continue — and §05 is exactly the reason for hesitation there, since the one full year of trackable guidance history included a mid-year cut.

The fairest summary: the market is pricing continued hyperscaler-led order-book compounding, on a business where that share of the order book is not a number the company discloses cleanly, coming off a guidance year that undershot even after being cut once. That is not a cheap stock by any conventional yardstick, and it is also not obviously overpriced next to the growth on offer if the growth holds. The multiple is a bet on the durability of §04’s story, not a statement the multiple alone can resolve.

Scenarios

Bull
The US$1.3–1.4bn FY27 backlog target is met or exceeded as the new hyperscaler relationship expands beyond its first US$131M order and the existing one keeps growing, while financial services, healthcare, public services and retail keep contributing a broad-based US$300M-plus a quarter alongside it. Managed services push through 40% of revenue, smoothing the lumpiness that produced the FY26 guidance cut. The 2S Inovações integration goes cleanly, India revenue starts compounding toward its stated multi-year target, and FY27 guidance is met in full rather than trimmed mid-year. The market gains confidence that the AI-linked share of the business is larger and more durable than any single disclosed number suggests, and the multiple holds or expands from here.
Base
Revenue and backlog keep growing but FY27 guidance is trimmed at some point during the year, much as FY26’s was — not because hyperscaler demand disappoints, but because a component, fibre or execution constraint again delays recognition, as it did in the December 2025 quarter. EBITDA margin drifts toward but does not quite reach the 10% long-term goal. The working-capital and cash-conversion questions in §06 remain open rather than resolved. The stock trades sideways-to-up as earnings catch up to a multiple that has already priced a good part of the story.
Bear
Hyperscaler capex growth decelerates or pauses — a live risk this desk’s own data-centre supply-chain research flags at the industry level — and the single named US$131M contract, worth roughly 14% of the backlog on its own, proves closer to a ceiling than a floor for new hyperscaler wins. A second supply-chain-driven guidance cut lands, this time reading as a pattern rather than a one-off, and the market re-prices the multiple down toward where the sector-bucketed peers already sit. Weak cash conversion catches up with the balance sheet just as the 2S Inovações debt (§06) shows up in a full quarter’s numbers, and the freshly doubled authorised share capital from the September 2026 AGM is used for a dilutive raise rather than staying as unused headroom.
12 —

Key Risks

01
Extreme concentration inside the “AI order book” itself
A single named hyperscaler contract (~US$131M) is roughly 14% of the entire US$949M backlog on its own (derived, §04). Management names two hyperscalers in total, one new and one existing, plus one unnamed large chip manufacturer. A handful of counterparties carry a disproportionate share of the growth narrative.
02
The AI/data-centre share of the business is not a number Black Box discloses cleanly
The two best available estimates — ~25% of the order book and ~40–50% of revenue — measure different things, come from different secondary sources, and cannot be reconciled here (§04). An investor buying this stock for its AI exposure is buying a story that is directionally true and numerically uncertain.
03
Proven fragility to the same supply chain the boom depends on
The FY26 guidance cut (§05) was caused specifically by optical-fibre and cable shortages inside “the booming data center ecosystem” — the identical input tightness this desk’s own research documents as a live, unresolved global constraint. It has already happened once inside the trailing twelve months.
04
Two of three reporting segments are loss-making
Technology Product Solutions and Others both ran at a segment loss in Q1 FY27 (§02); every rupee of consolidated profit came from System Integration, the segment that also carries all of the data-centre exposure and none of the separately disclosed margin.
05
Weak operating cash conversion
FY26 operating cash flow of ₹84 Cr against reported PAT of ₹218 Cr (§06) is roughly 39% conversion in the year the order book grew fastest. Plausibly explained by working-capital consumption from growth, but unresolved, and management itself has flagged rising working-capital days without yet stating a sustainable level.
06
Debt figures disagree across sources, and none reflect the Brazil deal
CRISIL’s ₹827 Cr gross debt and Screener’s ₹1,153 Cr borrowings figure (both dated to FY26) do not agree, and neither includes a full quarter of the ₹496.6 Cr 2S Inovações acquisition completed 1 May 2026 (§06).
07
Concentrated, Essar-controlled register with fresh dilution headroom
Essar Telecom holds 69.95% (§07); a related-party disclosure trail this report could not read in full; and the September 2026 AGM just doubled authorised share capital explicitly to create fundraising flexibility, with no raise yet announced.
08
A surveillance history that only just ended
Long-Term ASM Stage 1 from 4 June to 4 September 2026 spanned almost the entire decline from the all-time high (§09). No flag applies today, but the episode is roughly two weeks old, not two years old, and re-escalation would again raise margin requirements and curtail broker MTF eligibility.
09
Extended twelve-month move, unresolved drawdown, thin trend-template pass
+72% over one year against a still-open 27% drawdown from the 5 June high, and a trend template failing on exactly one check (§08). Neither a clean breakout nor a clean breakdown.
10
Single-geography and single-currency concentration
North America is roughly two-thirds of revenue (§02); every named hyperscaler and large-technology win is a US engagement. A US enterprise-capex slowdown, a tariff shock or a rupee-dollar move lands on the majority of the business at once, and India — the market this report’s reader trades in — is a single-digit share of revenue today.
13 —

Summary

RATING: HOLD — A REAL HYPERSCALE ORDER BOOK THE COMPANY WON’T CLEANLY QUANTIFY

Black Box is not a story stock manufactured out of thin air. The order backlog is up 83% year on year to a record US$949 million, a named Tier-1 US hyperscaler signed a three-year, US$131 million data-centre contract in August, and management is explicit that hyperscale demand is now a new growth engine alongside a broader, genuinely diversified order pipeline (§04). None of that is invented, and none of it should be dismissed.

But this desk cannot tell its reader what share of Black Box’s revenue or order book that story actually represents, because the company has not disclosed it cleanly, and the two secondary estimates in circulation — roughly a quarter of the order book, roughly four-tenths to a half of revenue — are far enough apart, and different enough in what they measure, that neither should be treated as settled (§04). The one full year of guidance history available shows management cutting its own FY26 numbers by a fourteenth mid-year because of a data-centre-linked component shortage, then landing just under even the reduced bar (§05). Cash conversion was weak in the same year the backlog grew fastest (§06), two of three reporting segments ran at a loss last quarter (§02), and the stock spent from its 5 June all-time high until 4 September under one stage of Additional Surveillance Measure or another (§09).

None of that is a reason to avoid the name outright — return on capital is genuinely strong, the credit rating has just been raised to a Positive outlook, the promoter put fresh cash in alongside minority warrant holders rather than diluting them alone, and the framework in §08 shows real underlying strength with only a single, narrow technical objection. But it is more than enough reason not to pay up for a number nobody has actually given this desk. A trailing multiple in the high-40s to low-50s and a book-value multiple above 11x are prices for a business whose AI exposure is confidently described and imprecisely measured.

Hold is the honest rating for a business that is a real, if imprecisely quantified, beneficiary of the AI data-centre build-out, already carrying a growth premium, one guidance cut and one surveillance episode into its short public track record on this theme. The case for buying more of the story than the company will confirm, or for exiting a genuinely improving business on a single ambiguous drawdown, is equally thin.

What to monitor

Whether FY27 guidance (₹7,800–8,000 Cr revenue, ₹725–750 Cr EBITDA, ₹300–325 Cr PAT) survives the year unrevised, given the FY26 precedent of a mid-year cut

Gross debt and gearing in the first post-2S-Inovações quarterly balance sheet, and whether the CRISIL (₹827 Cr) vs Screener (₹1,153 Cr) debt figures reconcile
Whether the September 2026 authorised-capital doubling is followed by an actual equity or warrant issuance
Promoter shareholding and pledge status in the September-quarter disclosure
Technology Product Solutions and Others segment results — whether either returns to profitability
Managed-services share of revenue, against the stated 30% → 40% → 50% glide path
Any named additional hyperscaler contract beyond the current two — the clearest evidence the AI share of this business is broadening rather than concentrating further
