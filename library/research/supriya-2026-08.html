Credit where due: management quantified the hit rather than gesturing at “cost headwinds”. That is better disclosure than most companies in this batch offered for comparable misses.

Now test whether it explains the gap. EBITDA fell from ₹52 crore to ₹47 crore — a ₹5 crore decline. Adding back the full ₹10.5 crore of stated additional costs would give roughly ₹57.5 crore, or a ~30% margin — better, but still below the ~35.9% of a year earlier and well short of the 33–35% the company guides to.

So the disclosed one-off costs account for the decline and then some, but they do not fully restore the historical margin. Either there is additional pressure not itemised, or the higher revenue base carries a structurally lower blended margin. That distinction is not resolvable from the disclosed figures and is the key thing to test in Q2.

06 —

The Water Problem — An Unusual Disclosure

Deferred sales~₹35 Cr
CauseWater shortages from a delayed monsoon
Scale~18% of Q1 revenue

This is one of the more unusual disclosures in the library and it is genuinely informative. API manufacturing is water-intensive — synthesis, cooling and cleaning all require large volumes — and Supriya’s plants are in the Konkan belt of Maharashtra, dependent on monsoon-fed supply.

Two readings, and both are true:

  • It is genuinely temporary. ₹35 crore of deferred — not lost — sales should recognise in Q2 once water supply normalised with the monsoon. Adjusting for it, Q1 revenue would have been roughly ₹225 crore, or +55% year on year.
  • It is a recurring structural exposure. Monsoons are variable, and a manufacturer whose output can be constrained by rainfall has a physical risk that no amount of demand solves. This will happen again in some future year.

Combined with the ₹10.5 crore cost shock, the two disclosed items make Q1 look substantially worse than the underlying business performed. That is the strongest argument for the bull case here — and it depends entirely on Q2 showing the deferred revenue arriving.

The residual risk is currency and the ordinary cyclicality of generic API pricing — the same structural exposure flagged for IOL Chemicals elsewhere in this library.

08 —

Backward Integration

72% of total revenues are fully backward integrated.

In API manufacturing this is the core competitive advantage. Making your own key starting materials and intermediates rather than buying them means lower cost, control over supply, insulation from Chinese intermediate pricing, and — critically for regulated markets — control over the full quality chain.

It is also the explanation for the historical 35% margin, which is high for a generic API maker. And it makes the Q1 margin collapse more surprising rather than less: a backward-integrated producer should be more insulated from input cost shocks, not less. The specific costs cited — solvents, fuel and power — are the ones backward integration does not protect against, since they are energy and utility inputs rather than chemical intermediates.

09 —

Guidance — Unchanged Despite the Quarter

FY27 revenue₹1,000 Cr — confidence reiterated
FY27 EBITDA margin33% – 35% — maintained
Q1 actual margin25%

Management is guiding to a 33–35% full-year margin after delivering 25% in Q1. That is a bold reaffirmation and it is the most checkable claim on this name.

The arithmetic: to average 34% for the year having done 25% in Q1, the remaining three quarters must average roughly 37% — above the top of the guided range and above the historical ~35%. That is a demanding requirement.

On revenue it is more achievable. ₹190 crore in Q1 against a ₹1,000 crore target needs ~₹270 crore per quarter for the rest of the year — a step up, but the ₹35 crore of deferred sales alone closes much of the gap in Q2.

Read together: the revenue target looks reachable, the margin guidance looks stretched. That is the single most useful conclusion in this report.

The gap between the trailing P/E of 32.5× and the annualised P/E of ~67× is the whole valuation story. Trailing earnings still contain three good quarters; the current run-rate is half that level.

So the stock is not cheap after a 26% fall — it is roughly as expensive as it was, because earnings fell about as fast as the price. The May brief noted 31× against a ~26× three-year average; at 32.5× it is still above that average despite the drawdown.

The bull case requires believing the ₹10.5 crore of costs and ₹35 crore of deferred sales made Q1 unrepresentative. Adjusting for both, the annualised multiple would fall back toward the low thirties — reasonable but not cheap. The bear case is that 25% is closer to the new normal margin, in which case ~67× is the real multiple and there is a long way down.

Summary

RATING: HOLD — QUALITY BUSINESS, WEAKEST SIGNAL IN THE LIBRARY

Supriya is a genuinely high-quality asset: net cash, 72% backward integrated, 81% exports well spread across Asia, Europe and LatAm, a clean USFDA record, and global leadership in several niche molecules. Q1 revenue grew 31%.

And profit fell 31%. EBITDA margin collapsed from roughly 35.9% to 25% — a 1,090 basis point decline, the largest single-year margin fall in this library. The stock carries RS 3, the weakest relative strength of any name reviewed, and the worst three-month return at −19.9%.

Management quantified the damage honestly — ~₹10.5 crore of solvent, fuel and power costs, and ~₹35 crore of sales deferred by monsoon water shortages. Adjusting for both, Q1 was considerably better than it reads, and that is the real bull case. But adding back the full ₹10.5 crore still leaves margin near 30%, short of the 33–35% guided — and to average that for FY27 the remaining quarters must run at ~37%, above the historical level.

The May brief warned this was a quality business at a not-cheap price. The pullback came, and at 32.5× trailing and ~67× annualised it is still above the ~26× three-year average. Q2 settles it: either the deferred revenue arrives with the margin, or 25% was the new base.
