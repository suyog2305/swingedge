Ramkrishna is nearly three times the size by revenue and earns half the profit. Happy Forgings makes more money on a third of the sales. That gap is the difference between a high-value machined-component specialist and a broader, more commoditised forging operation.

Ramkrishna’s advantages are scale, diversification across end-markets, and a castings business Happy Forgings does not have. Its disadvantage is margin — and in a capital-intensive industry, margin is what determines returns on the capital employed.

The 297% profit growth needs context before it is read as a growth number. The year-ago quarter produced just ₹11.79 crore of profit on roughly ₹1,015 crore of revenue — a net margin close to 1.2%. That was a poor quarter, and quadrupling from a low base is a recovery, not an expansion.

The genuinely important figure is the 332 basis point EBITDA margin improvement to 17.96%. That is the operating story, and it is real.

04 —

The Flat Sequential Line

Revenue grew 19.84% year on year and was flat quarter on quarter.

That single fact reframes the whole result. Year-on-year growth compares against a weak base twelve months ago; sequential comparison shows what is happening now. Ramkrishna is not currently growing its top line — it is holding it while improving what it earns on each rupee of it.

Margin-led recoveries are legitimate and often precede volume recoveries. But they have a ceiling: cost efficiency, mix improvement and price recovery can each be captured once. Once margin reaches its normalised level, earnings growth requires volume, and volume is currently flat. Watch whether Q2 breaks the sequential plateau — that is the number that determines whether this is a recovery with a second act.

Diversification — The Structural Improvement

Railway share of revenueFY22Q1 FY27
Railways1.7%6.0%

Railways have grown from a rounding error to 6% of revenue over five years. Management frames the Q1 profit surge as being driven by diversification, and the direction is right.

Keep the scale honest, though. At 6%, railways are not yet a second leg — they are a promising line item. The commercial-vehicle cycle still determines this company’s results, and will continue to for several years even if the railway ramp goes well.

The comparison with Happy Forgings is instructive here too: both companies are diversifying away from commercial vehicles, and both are early in that process. Neither has yet reduced its cyclicality in a way that shows up in the numbers.

06 —

The Titagarh Railway-Wheel Joint Venture

Ramkrishna is scaling a railway wheel joint venture with Titagarh Rail Systems, with commercial supplies to Indian Railways expected to begin in October 2026.

This is the most interesting forward development in the story, and it is a genuinely different kind of business from forging components for truck makers:

  • Import substitution with policy support. India has historically imported a significant share of its railway wheels. Domestic manufacture is actively encouraged.
  • A single large, creditworthy customer. Indian Railways buys in volume, on long horizons, and pays.
  • High barriers. Railway wheel manufacturing requires specific metallurgy, certification and capital that few can assemble.

The discipline: October 2026 is next month, and “expected to begin” is not “has begun”. Joint ventures with a state customer in a new product category slip routinely. This belongs in the monitoring list as a dated, checkable milestone — which is exactly what makes it useful. Either commercial supplies start in October or they do not, and you will know.

Both additions are already complete — a materially different position from Happy Forgings, which is mid-way through roughly ₹900 crore of spending, or Jyoti CNC, which has over ₹1,000 crore approved.

The company is past the point of maximum capital risk and at the point of maximum operating leverage — which is consistent with a margin-led recovery and with revenue that has gone flat while profitability improves.

09 —

The P/E Puzzle — 118× Earnings at 4.1× Book

Ramkrishna trades at 118.2× trailing earnings and only 4.13× book. That combination looks contradictory and is in fact highly informative.

A high P/E with a low price-to-book almost always means the same thing: earnings are depressed relative to the asset base. The company has a large balance sheet — plants, presses, casting capacity — that is currently generating a poor return. The market is pricing the assets at a modest 4.13×, while the trailing twelve months of profit, which include the weak ₹11.79 crore quarter, produce an inflated multiple.

Annualising the Q1 PAT of ₹46.88 crore gives roughly ₹188 crore, against which a ₹13,592 crore market capitalisation implies about 72× — still high, but a very different number from 118×.

Annualising one quarter is crude and assumes no seasonality. It is used here only to show how much of the headline 118× reflects a depressed trailing base rather than the current run-rate.

The investment question this frames is the right one: can Ramkrishna earn a normal return on the capital it has already deployed? At 4.13× book that recovery is not expensively priced. The 118× is largely an artefact of where earnings have been, not a statement about where they are.

Summary

RATING: HOLD — RECOVERING EARNINGS, NOT A GROWTH STORY

Ramkrishna Forgings reported profit up 297%, which sounds like the strongest result in this batch and is not. The year-ago quarter earned ₹11.79 crore on roughly ₹1,015 crore of revenue — a 1.2% net margin — so this is a recovery off a poor base. The real achievement is the 332 basis point EBITDA margin improvement to 17.96%, and it is genuine.

The fact that reframes the quarter is that revenue was flat sequentially. This is a company holding its top line steady while earning more from it, on capacity that is already built and paid for. That is a legitimate and often profitable phase — but it is a margin recovery, not growth, and margin recoveries have a ceiling.

What makes it worth holding rather than dismissing: 4.13× book and 24% above its 200-DMA make it one of the least expensive and least extended names you own, and the October railway-wheel milestone is a specific, dated, checkable event that could add a genuinely better-margin business. The bull case does not require the CV cycle to accelerate — only for the margin to keep climbing toward 20% while railways start.
