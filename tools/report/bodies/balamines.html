04 —

The Margin — And Why It Cuts Both Ways

An operating margin moving from 15.26% to 25.42% is the whole story, and it is essential to understand where it came from before extrapolating it.

Source 1 — realisationsAmine prices recovering as Chinese overcapacity is throttled. Price-driven and outside management’s control.
Source 2 — capacity utilisationBetter utilisation and operational efficiency following the DME commissioning. Volume-driven and structural.

The distinction matters enormously for what happens next. Margin gained through better utilisation is durable — the fixed-cost base is now covered by more volume, and that does not reverse unless volume does. Margin gained through commodity price recovery is rented, not owned: it persists exactly as long as the price environment does, and amine pricing has historically been cyclical.

This report cannot cleanly separate the two from the disclosed figures, and it will not guess at the split. What it can say is that a chemical company earning 25.4% operating margin is earning well above its own recent history, and mean reversion in commodity chemicals is the base case rather than the exception.

Note the date. Commercial production began five days after the May report was published, so Q1 FY27 is the first quarter carrying any DME contribution — and it carried roughly six weeks of it, not a full quarter.

That is the same structural point flagged for Man Industries and its Saudi acquisition: Q2 FY27 is the first quarter with a full contribution, and the step-up from six weeks to thirteen is mechanical rather than speculative.

DME is a genuine diversification. It is used as an LPG blendstock and aerosol propellant, and it draws on methanol feedstock the company already handles. At 100,000 TPA it is a meaningful volume addition, and it is the part of the margin story that is owned rather than rented.

06 —

The Three Forces — Revisited

The May report built its case on three simultaneous reversals. Scoring them three months on:

ForceStatus
China’s “anti-involution” policy throttling the over-capacity that depressed amine pricesConsistent with the realisation recovery visible in the margin. A policy-driven supply constraint, and policy can change.
Middle East disruption shutting in Iranian and Saudi methanol that kept Chinese producers underpricedA geopolitical input, by nature temporary and reversible. The least durable of the three.
The ₹750 Cr specialty-chemicals capex moving from cash drain to cash generator over FY27–28The most durable. DME commissioning on 20 May is the first visible evidence; this is company-controlled.

Two of the three forces are external and reversible. That is not a criticism of the original call — it was correct, and it worked — but it defines what a holder is now exposed to. If Chinese capacity discipline loosens or Middle East supply normalises, two of the three legs weaken simultaneously, and the 1,010 basis points of margin were built substantially on them.

The third leg — the company’s own capex — is the one that survives either way, and it is the reason to keep watching rather than simply booking the call as closed.

Annualising Q1 assumes the 25.4% margin persists for four quarters. That assumption is precisely what Section 04 questions, so treat ~26× as the optimistic bound rather than the expected multiple.

The valuation is genuinely reasonable — and it is reasonable because it is calculated on peak-cycle margins. At 3.88× book Balaji is not expensive on assets, and 37.8× trailing on a business that just doubled profit is not demanding.

The discipline to apply: if the margin reverts toward the 15.26% of a year ago, the annualised P/E is not 26× — it is closer to 44×. A commodity chemical company at peak margin always looks cheap on trailing earnings, and that is the standard trap in this sector. The DME contribution and the utilisation gains are the part of the margin that argues against full reversion.

The May report called an inflection and put a ₹1,950 twelve-month target on it. The stock reached ₹2,377 inside three months — 22% past target — and Q1 FY27 confirmed the operational case emphatically: revenue +27%, profit roughly doubled, and an operating margin expansion of 1,010 basis points, the largest in this entire library.

Balaji is also the strongest name in this import batch on your own signals: RS 89, passing the trend template, and 83 days in Stage 2 — the longest tenure of the ten, where seven others have broken down entirely.

What keeps this at Accumulate on Dips rather than a fresh Buy is the composition of that margin. Two of the three forces behind the thesis — Chinese capacity policy and Middle East methanol supply — are external and reversible, and a chemical company at 25.4% operating margin is earning well above its own recent history. The DME plant, which began commercial production on 20 May and has yet to contribute a full quarter, is the part that is genuinely owned. Q2 is the first quarter that shows what DME is actually worth.
