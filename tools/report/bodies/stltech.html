Prior-year revenue and EBITDA are derived from the reported ~87% and ~184% growth rates. The screener records net profit growth of +1,870% for the latest quarter, consistent with the move from ₹10 Cr to ₹197 Cr.

The margin figure is the one to weight. An EBITDA margin of 20.8% is described as the highest in nearly twenty quarters — five years. That is not a good quarter within a good run; it is the best quarter since before the last downcycle, and it tells you how depressed this business had become.

Net profit going from ₹10 crore to ₹197 crore is arithmetically dramatic and, as with HFCL and Shadowfax elsewhere in this library, reflects a company that was barely profitable rather than one that multiplied strong earnings. The distinction matters for extrapolation.

03 —

What Drove the Quarter

Management attributes the record performance to an improved product mix and operating leverage, with AI data-centre demand as the underlying driver.

Both mechanisms are credible and they compound:

  • Mix. Optical connectivity products for data centres carry higher realisations than commodity telecom cable. A shift in mix lifts margin without needing higher volumes.
  • Operating leverage. Fibre drawing and cabling are capital-intensive with high fixed costs. STL had spent years running below the level at which those costs are properly covered. Revenue rising 87% against a fixed base is why EBITDA rose 184%.

The caution that follows from the second point: operating leverage that produced +184% EBITDA on +87% revenue works identically in reverse. A business whose margin swings from ~13.7% to 20.8% on an 87% revenue move will give it back on a comparable decline.

ValueUS$1.11 billion (over ₹10,000 Cr)
ScopeOptical connectivity products for next-generation AI data centres
PeriodFY27 to FY29
Share of order book~54% of the ₹18,618 Cr book

This contract is the reason the stock has risen 751% in a year, and it deserves both credit and scrutiny.

What it genuinely establishes: AI data-centre construction requires enormous volumes of optical interconnect, and a three-year committed contract of this scale means STL has been qualified by a large buyer at the leading edge of that build-out. That is real, and it converts a cyclical telecom supplier into a structural participant in AI infrastructure.

What it concentrates: at roughly 54% of the order book, this is a majority-of-visibility dependency on one contract and one counterparty. Spread across FY27–FY29, roughly ₹3,300 crore a year — material against ₹7,640 crore of annualised revenue, but less transformative than the headline suggests when annualised.

The near-identical contract values were checked against separate sources and are two distinct agreements: HFCL’s is a five-year high-fibre-count OFC supply contract with an undisclosed global multinational running to December 2030, executed through its international subsidiary; STL’s is an FY27–FY29 optical connectivity contract for AI data centres. On the comparison above, HFCL is currently the cheaper of the two on both order-book coverage and book value.

07 —

Market Position

STL holds approximately 9% global market share in optical fibre cables excluding China.

The exclusion is doing significant work in that statistic and should be read carefully. China dominates global optical fibre manufacturing; a share figure that excludes it describes the addressable non-Chinese market rather than the world. That framing is legitimate — Western and Indian buyers increasingly source outside China for supply-chain reasons, which is precisely the tailwind — but 9% of a carved-out market is a more modest position than the headline implies.

What it does establish is scale sufficient to be a credible supplier to a hyperscale buyer, which is the qualification that matters for the $1.11 billion contract.

08 —

Guidance

FY27 EBITDA margin guidanceRaised to 23%
Q1 FY27 actual20.8%

This is an unusual guidance shape and a notably confident one. Almost every company in this library guides below what it has just delivered — HFCL to 40% after 120%, Shadowfax to 38–40% after 65%, IOL to 15–20% after 37%. STL is guiding to a margin above the 20.8% it just achieved, and that 20.8% was already the best in twenty quarters.

Guiding for further margin expansion from a five-year high implies management expects the mix shift toward data-centre connectivity to keep improving realisations. It is a specific, falsifiable claim, and it will be visible in Q2.

It also removes any margin of safety in the guidance. A company guiding above its own record has no room to disappoint gently.

Summary

RATING: HOLD — GENUINE TURNAROUND, EXTREME EXTENSION

Sterlite Technologies delivered its best quarter in five years: revenue up ~87% to ₹1,910 crore, EBITDA up ~184% to ₹397 crore, an EBITDA margin of 20.8% that is the highest in roughly twenty quarters, and net profit of ₹197 crore against ₹10 crore a year earlier. The order book is a record ₹18,618 crore, and management has raised margin guidance to 23% — the only company in this library guiding above its own record.

Two things demand attention before that reads as a conclusion. The stock is up 751% from its 52-week low and sits 89% above its 200-DMA — the most extended chart of any name across your holdings and watchlist — and carries LT-ASM Stage IV, the most severe surveillance status in this entire library.

And the portfolio point matters as much as the company one: you already own HFCL, which is the same business, at the same revenue scale, reporting the same turnaround in the same quarter, on the back of a contract of almost the same size. On order-book coverage (1.44× vs 1.99×) and book value (7.83× vs 15.5×), HFCL is currently the cheaper expression of an identical theme. Adding STL would concentrate that bet, not spread it.
