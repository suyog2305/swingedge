What This Note Is, and How It Was Checked

The owner asked four questions about twelve public-sector banks, identified here by NSE code to avoid any name-matching error: SBIN (State Bank of India), BANKBARODA (Bank of Baroda), PNB (Punjab National Bank), CANBK (Canara Bank), UNIONBANK (Union Bank of India), INDIANB (Indian Bank), BANKINDIA (Bank of India), MAHABANK (Bank of Maharashtra), CENTRALBK (Central Bank of India), IOB (Indian Overseas Bank), UCOBANK (UCO Bank) and PSB (Punjab & Sind Bank). Each is answered in its own section; §02 gives the short answers.

Timing: what the reported quarter does and does not contain

The house rule asks whether the reported quarter contains the catalyst. Here it does not, and the gap is stated up front. The latest reported asset-quality quarter is Q1 FY27, which ended on 30 June 2026. The September fall in the sector, the rise in crude oil, the rise in global bond yields and the debate over an RBI rate hike all came after it. The Q2 FY27 business updates ended on 30 September 2026 but carry only volumes — advances, deposits and, for some banks, CASA — and no asset-quality, margin or treasury figure. So no reported number in this note contains the September events; the asset-quality evidence describes the position before them. None of the twelve had reported Q2 results by 4 October.

What this note is and is not

It is a comparison and a ranking against criteria stated before the ranking is drawn. It carries no rating on any of the twelve, no price target, no view on when or whether to buy, and no position size. The relative-strength ratings, trend-template verdicts, surveillance status and price-to-book readings sit in the generated blocks in §08, which refresh from the scan; this note’s prose does not restate them. Price-to-book is not quoted anywhere in the prose because a dated book-value figure was not taken from a filing for all twelve.

What the eight show

Advances grew faster than deposits year on year at six of the seven banks with both figures. The exception is Bank of India, where deposits (+21.60%) outgrew advances (+20.43%). The widest gaps are Union Bank (11.7 points) and UCO Bank (10.6 points); Canara Bank is next at 6.2. Bank of Baroda is the closest to balance among the larger names (1.5 points), with deposit growth up from 13.81% in Q1 to 16.75% in Q2 [1][14]. Deposits at Union Bank grew 6.87%, the lowest of the seven, and its CASA ratio slipped 17 basis points on the quarter, from 35.10% to 34.93% [5]. Credit-deposit ratios run from 75.56% (PNB) to about 86% (Bank of Baroda, derived).

A caution on the deposit figures. Between 8 June and 31 August 2026 the RBI ran a forex-swap window under which banks raised foreign-currency deposits and sold the dollars to the RBI. Banks mobilised $136.4 billion under the schemes by 2 September 2026, of which $127.23 billion was FCNR(B) deposits [34]. SBI’s chairman said in August he expected the September-quarter deposit pick-up to come from FCNR(B) [36]. None of the summaries above breaks out how much of any bank’s Q2 deposit growth came from this window, so it cannot be removed here. The sequential deposit jumps at Bank of India (+8.3%), Bank of Baroda (about +7.2%) and Canara Bank (+5.3%) fall in the window, but no source attributes them to it. §05 therefore tests the ranking with and without the deposit-growth criterion.

What can be said

Gross NPA fell year on year at all nine banks where a year-ago comparison was found (SBIN, BANKBARODA, PNB, CANBK, UNIONBANK, INDIANB, BANKINDIA, CENTRALBK, UCOBANK) [11]–[18][20]. The level runs from 1.33% (IOB) and 1.45% (MAHABANK) to 2.78% (PNB). Net NPA is 0.65% or lower at all twelve, and 0.15% or lower at INDIANB and MAHABANK. Credit cost is lowest at BANKINDIA (0.15%), INDIANB (0.23%) and PNB (0.25%) and highest at CANBK (0.49%) and PSB (0.44%) [11][13][15][17][18][22]. The weaker readings are in margins: NIM fell year on year at SBIN (whole bank, −3 bp), CANBK (−3 bp), BANKINDIA (−3 bp) and MAHABANK (−16 bp), and PNB’s global margin sat below its guided range; it rose at UNIONBANK, INDIANB, IOB and UCOBANK. PSB’s credit cost rose from 0.09% to 0.44%. SBI’s domestic CD ratio was 74.23% and its stressed book (SMA-1 plus SMA-2 above ₹5 cr) was ₹4,174 cr at June 2026, against ₹5,025 cr a year earlier [11]. Treasury income gives the scale of the bond-yield exposure discussed in §11: PNB’s Q1 treasury and trading income of ₹1,081 cr was about 21% of its Q1 net profit of ₹5,253 cr (derived) [13].

The Fall Against the Evidence

What a fall in price has and has not changed

Two different things can lie behind a share that is 27% below its 52-week high. In the first, the price has fallen against a business whose book and earnings are unchanged: the same book is priced at a lower multiple, and a lower trailing P/E follows mechanically. In the second, the fall anticipates a real deterioration: slower earnings, higher credit costs, or a smaller or riskier book. The price table cannot tell the two apart. The reported evidence can, but only for the period it covers.

What the evidence in hand shows. The reported quarter (June 2026) shows improvement, not deterioration, in gross NPA at every bank with a comparison (§04). The deterioration the sector is being sold for is forward-looking: a possible rate hike, higher bond yields, a weaker rupee and costlier deposits (§06). None of it is in a reported number yet. The fall therefore reflects expected future earnings effects rather than a measured worsening of the books, and the first reported test is the Q2 FY27 results. The evidence that does point to weakness is narrower: margins slipping at four banks, a funding gap at Union Bank and UCO Bank, and a one-off charge at Bank of Baroda.

The desk’s trailing P/E sharpens the point. It runs from 5.0 to 10.7, and the three lowest are BANKINDIA (5.00), BANKBARODA (5.40) and CANBK (5.41), but the three deepest falls (PSB −35.45%, UCOBANK −32.95%, BANKBARODA −29.10%) are not the same three. The Pattern Underneath: Loans Outrunning Deposits

The one pattern visible across the eight September filers is that lending has grown faster than deposits. The gap ranges from 1.5 percentage points at Bank of Baroda to 11.7 at Union Bank (§03), with Bank of India the exception. A higher credit-deposit ratio means a bank has less room to lend without raising funding, and a bank that needs to attract deposits in a hiking cycle tends to pay more for them. For banks whose loans reprice with the repo rate faster than their deposits do, a hike can first help the margin and later squeeze it as deposits reprice; for banks that must raise deposit rates immediately to fund growth, the help is smaller. That mechanism is the one §11 traces. The repo-linked share of each bank’s loan book was not found in any source read and is not asserted here; without it the direction of the margin effect for any single bank cannot be stated.

Two facts qualify the reading. First, the foreign-currency deposit window of June–August inflated system liquidity: banks mobilised $136.4 billion, the RBI bore hedging costs of roughly 2.5–3% a year according to one economist, and analysts warned that deploying the money profitably would be hard and could lower loan yields [34][35]. Some of the September-quarter deposit growth may therefore be one-off and carry its own margin cost. Second, bank credit growth was above 19% in July [29], so loan demand, not supply, is setting the pace.

Scenarios for the Policy Meeting

Three outcomes for 7 October, traced to PSU banks through the three channels the owner named: bond yields and mark-to-market on government-securities books, repricing of repo-linked loans against deposit costs, and credit growth. These are scenarios, not forecasts; no probabilities are invented, and the only probabilities cited are the economist polls in §07. This section informs the reader about event risk and does not say whether or when to buy.

A — Hike, 25 bp to 5.50%
The outcome a majority in both polls expects (38 or 35 of 61 in Reuters; eight of ten in Business Standard) [29][30][32]. Bond yields and mark-to-market: a hike that is already priced may move yields little; a hike with firmer guidance, or no signal of a pause, could push yields higher, and rising yields reduce the value of government securities held in the available-for-sale and trading books. The sector’s exposure was already visible in March 2026, when the 10-year yield touched 6.93% and the index fell nearly 16% in the month [39]. The scale of a bank’s exposure depends on its trading and available-for-sale book and its duration, which were not sourced; the one scale marker found is PNB, whose Q1 treasury and trading income was about 21% of net profit (derived) [13]. Repo-linked loans against deposits: loans linked to the repo rate reprice upward, typically within a quarter, while term deposits reprice only as they mature, so the first effect on margin is timing-dependent; banks that need deposits to fund growth (UNIONBANK, UCOBANK, CANBK in §03) may pass on more of the benefit to depositors. Credit growth: higher lending rates act on demand with a lag; credit growth was above 19% in July, so the starting point is strong [29].
B — Hold at 5.25%
The minority view in the October polls, and the unanimous outcome of the last four meetings. Bond yields: a hold against a hiking expectation could ease yields; a hold with explicit tightening guidance would keep a hike in the price and in yields. After the 5 August hold the 10-year yield was reported at 6.79% and the Nifty PSU Bank index rose 0.72% [28], but that hold was read as lowering the odds of a near-term hike, which is not the setup now. Repricing and credit: no change to repo-linked loan rates; the funding-cost pressure from loans outrunning deposits (§10) remains. Several named economists expect the first hike in December, February or April rather than October, so a hold does not remove the hiking question; it moves it [31].
C — Cut
No source found treats a cut as the expected outcome in October; the only poll that did (January 2026) pre-dates the rise in inflation and crude [33]. It is included for completeness: a cut would reverse the channels in A, lowering repo-linked loan yields faster than deposit costs fall.
Liquidity and CRR measures
Liquidity measures can accompany any of the three outcomes. The RBI’s press-release list for 1 October includes an overnight variable-rate reverse-repo auction [26], and one roundup says it may intensify absorption of the surplus created by foreign-currency inflows [31]. A tighter liquidity stance would raise short-term funding costs at the margin; a cash-reserve-ratio increase would raise the share of deposits that earns nothing. The current CRR and the size of the liquidity surplus were not sourced, so neither can be quantified here. What would show it: the governor’s statement and the accompanying liquidity announcements on 7 October.

Key Risks

01
The asset-quality evidence pre-dates the events the sector fell on
The latest reported quarter ended on 30 June 2026. The September fall, the crude-oil and bond-yield moves and the rate-hike debate came later. A reader relying on the June ratios is reading a position that may have changed; the first reported test is the Q2 FY27 results.
02
The 7 October decision is a dated event with a split forecast
A majority in two October polls expects a hike; the market-implied path varies by source from 50 bp to 90 bp over twelve months; named economists disagree on timing. The market reaction may depend as much on the guidance as on the rate.
03
Mark-to-market on government-securities books
PSU banks hold larger bond books than private banks, and a rise in yields is recorded as a loss in treasury income. The 10-year yield reached 6.93% in March 2026 and 6.79% on 5 August; no October level was sourced. PNB’s treasury income was about a fifth of Q1 profit.
04
Loans outrunning deposits, and one-off deposit growth
At Union Bank and UCO Bank advances growth exceeds deposit growth by more than 10 points. Part of the September deposit growth at several banks may come from the one-off foreign-currency deposit window, which no source breaks out bank by bank, and from which a margin cost may follow.
05
Margins
NIM fell year on year at SBIN, CANBK, BANKINDIA and MAHABANK in Q1, and PNB’s was below guidance. A hike could help repo-linked loan yields, but the repo-linked share of each loan book was not found.
06
Comparability and one-offs
Bank of Baroda’s reported return on assets includes a ₹5,680 cr one-time charge. Provision coverage, slippage and credit-cost definitions differ across banks. The ranking is sensitive to these choices, as the variants in §05 show.
07
External shocks: crude, the rupee and foreign flows
Brent was about $108 on 28 September on a geopolitical development, the rupee touched 95.60 on 17 August, and US yields were near 5.21%. These moved the whole market, not only these banks, and none can be forecast from this note’s evidence.
08
Secondary sourcing and four banks without a September update
Ten of the twelve Q1 FY27 asset-quality rows rest on secondary summaries, and the Q2 figures are summaries of filings the desk could not open. SBIN, MAHABANK, CENTRALBK and IOB have no September figures located. The ranking could change when the gaps are filled.

Summary

VERDICT: EVIDENCE RANKING AGAINST STATED CRITERIA — NOT A RECOMMENDATION

The September updates show lending growing faster than deposits at six of seven banks with full numbers, and the reported June asset quality is better than a year earlier almost everywhere it could be compared. Eight of twelve updates were located; SBIN, MAHABANK, CENTRALBK and IOB had none located by 4 October. Gross NPA ranges from 1.33% (IOB) to 2.78% (PNB) and fell year on year at the nine banks with a comparison; the weaker reported readings are margins, which slipped at four banks, and Bank of Baroda’s ₹5,680 cr one-off charge. This quarter pre-dates the September sell-off, which the sources attribute mainly to crude above $100, rising bond yields, rupee pressure and the prospect of an RBI hike.

On the stated criteria the three highest-ranked banks are MAHABANK, INDIANB and UCOBANK, with BANKINDIA fourth. The same three come out in six of ten variants, and the result changes when provision coverage is dropped, when only banks with a Q2 update are ranked, and when only asset-quality criteria are used. The RBI decides on 7 October. Polls lean to a 25 bp hike (35 or 38 of 61 economists in Reuters, depending on the report; eight of ten in Business Standard), the last four decisions were holds, and the channels into PSU banks are bond-book mark-to-market, repricing of repo-linked loans against deposit costs, and credit growth.
