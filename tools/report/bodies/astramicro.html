This is the only company in the entire library whose latest quarter shows both revenue and profit declining. Every other name reported growth on at least one line.

Management described the quarter as “modest”, attributing it to temporary delays in customer approvals and last-stage technical issues, and said these are expected to normalise in coming quarters.

That explanation is plausible for a defence supplier — approvals and acceptance testing genuinely do slip, and revenue recognition in this industry is lumpy. But it is also the standard explanation for a weak defence quarter, and it is unfalsifiable until the next print. Q2 is where it gets tested.

Context worth holding: FY26 operating margin was roughly 28%, with Q4 FY26 at 33.3%. The 18.72% recorded this quarter is a sharp step down, consistent with under-absorption on lower revenue.

The ₹2,205 Crore HAL Order

Announced17 August 2026
CustomerHindustan Aeronautics Limited (HAL)
Value~₹2,205 Cr
ScopeCritical subsystems for the Uttam radar
Scale check~1.9× FY26 revenue of ₹1,163 Cr, in a single order

This is the most important development at Astra since the June report, and it is a genuinely large win. Uttam is India’s indigenous AESA fire-control radar programme for combat aircraft, and supplying its critical subsystems places Astra at the centre of the country’s most strategically significant radar programme.

Three things make it more than a headline:

  • It is nearly two years of revenue in one order, from a single creditworthy domestic customer.
  • AESA T/R modules are Astra’s core capability — this is the company’s strongest technical franchise, not an adjacency.
  • Indigenous radar programmes are policy-protected, which makes the revenue durable once the programme is committed.

The caution is timing. A ₹2,205 crore order on a defence programme converts over many years, not quarters, and the Q1 miss was itself caused by approval and acceptance delays. The order improves the multi-year picture and does nothing for the next two prints.

06 —

The Managing Director Is Stepping Down

Announced alongside the Q1 results: the Managing Director is stepping down, with a leadership transition ahead.

A leadership change is not automatically negative, and this report will not speculate on the reason, which is not established in the sources reviewed. Two things make it worth flagging as more than routine:

  • The timing. It was announced with a quarter in which revenue fell 10.7% and profit fell 25%, and the stock dropped 9%. A weak print plus an MD departure is a harder combination for the market to read than either alone.
  • The ownership structure. Promoter holding is roughly 6.5% — unusually low. In a widely held company with minimal promoter stake, professional management is the continuity. There is no controlling family to anchor strategy through a transition.

Watch who is appointed and from where. An internal technical successor signals continuity in a company whose value rests on RF engineering depth; an external commercial appointment would signal a different intent.

07 —

The Business and Its Moat

Astra’s advantage is genuine and narrow. RF and microwave subsystem design for radar and electronic warfare requires deep specialist engineering, and the company has been building it since 1991. Qualification into Indian strategic programmes takes years and, once achieved, is durable.

The June report recorded FY26 ROCE of 19.4%, up from a ~14.6% historical average — evidence the franchise had begun earning properly on its capital. Q1’s revenue decline and margin step-down interrupt that trajectory rather than reverse it, provided management’s “temporary delays” framing proves correct.

The structural limitation is customer concentration by construction. With 97.6% of the book domestic defence, space and public sector, Astra has one effective customer — the Indian state, in several forms. That is a high-quality, policy-supported customer, and it is also one whose procurement timing no supplier controls. The 2% export share is the honest measure of how little diversification exists today.

Guidance

FY27 revenue guidanceLifted to ~₹1,350 Cr
Implied growth on FY26 (₹1,163 Cr)15 – 20%

Management lifted guidance in the same quarter that revenue fell 10.7%. That is an unusual combination and it is the clearest statement of the company’s own view: the Q1 shortfall is timing, and the year is intact.

The arithmetic makes the claim checkable. Q1 delivered ₹176 crore standalone. Reaching ~₹1,350 crore requires roughly ₹1,174 crore across the remaining three quarters — about ₹391 crore a quarter, more than double the Q1 run-rate.

That is a demanding back-loading, and it is the single number to test the guidance against. Defence revenue genuinely is H2-weighted, so it is not implausible. But Q2 needs to show a substantial step up for the full-year figure to remain credible, and if it does not, the guidance goes with it.

Summary

RATING: HOLD — RECORD BOOK, WEAK QUARTER, MD DEPARTING

Astra Microwave won a ₹2,205 crore order from HAL for Uttam radar subsystems on 17 August — nearly two years of revenue in a single award, on the company’s strongest technical franchise, in India’s most strategically significant radar programme. The total order book is a record ₹4,300 crore, 3.7× FY26 revenue. That is a genuinely strong multi-year position.

In the same window, Q1 revenue fell 10.7%, profit fell 25%, operating margin dropped from roughly 28% to 18.7%, the stock fell 9% on the print, and the Managing Director announced he is stepping down. It is the only company in this library with both revenue and profit declining. On your own framework it has failed the trend template and left the computed Stage 2 track, though the provider’s weekly list still carries it.

Management calls the shortfall temporary and lifted FY27 guidance to ~₹1,350 crore in the same breath. That requires roughly ₹391 crore a quarter for the rest of the year against ₹176 crore delivered — a specific, checkable claim that Q2 will settle. At 84.6× earnings and 12.2× book, the price leaves no room for it to be wrong.
