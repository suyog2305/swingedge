02 —

Read This First

This report exists because the framework and the price sheet are saying different things, and both of them are right.

The framework could hardly be more emphatic. Syrma passes all seven trend-template checks on relative strength in the high eighties, sits at an all-time high set within the last few sessions, and has been in Stage 2 continuously since June 2025 on the provider’s list. Nothing in the scan is flashing amber. Most of the library’s reports open by explaining why a name failed a check; this one cannot.

Four things sit against that, and they need to be read together rather than one at a time:

  • The multiple. 85.13× trailing earnings and 11.04× book. That is not a rounding error above the peer group — Section 10 shows it is at or near the top of the listed Indian EMS set on both a trailing-earnings and an enterprise-value basis.
  • The margin is going the wrong way sequentially. Operating EBITDA margin fell from 11.9% in Q4 FY26 to 10.2% in Q1 FY27, and profit after tax fell 11.4% quarter on quarter — inside a quarter that also delivered 67% revenue growth and 112% profit growth year on year. A decelerating margin inside an accelerating growth story is exactly the combination that gets missed when only the year-on-year line is read.
  • The run is four sessions old. The stock added roughly a seventh of its value across the four sessions from 2 to 8 September — about 14% (derived from the closing prices on those two dates). Whatever the merits of the business, a good part of the current price was put there last week, on a facility opening rather than on a number.
  • Stage 2 tenure is now late-stage by the framework’s own author. Weinstein’s point about Stage 2 is that it is finite. A run past a year is not a stronger signal than a run of six weeks; it is a later one. The framework reports tenure but does not penalise it, so the reader has to.

None of this makes the business bad. The business is genuinely good and genuinely accelerating — Sections 04 to 07 make that case with the company’s own numbers. The question this report tries to answer is narrower: whether a business growing this fast, at this price, this far into a trend, after a move this sharp, is something the framework is qualified to wave through on its own. It is not, and the rest of the report is the part the framework does not do.

Two honest caveats about that chain.

First, there is a three-session gap between the inauguration and the move. The facility opened on Wednesday 2 September; the stock’s decisive day was Monday 7 September. If the market were pricing the news efficiently it would have done so on the 2nd or 3rd. Second, the MOFSL note is dated the same day as the second leg of the move, so it cannot be established as a cause rather than a consequence. It is recorded here because it is dated and sourced, not because the sequence is proven.

Checked and not found: no index inclusion or rejig affecting SYRMA in this window; no fresh order announcement, corporate action or block deal in the sources reviewed; and no sector-wide EMS move on 7 September — the 5paisa wrap names Syrma as the top Nifty 500 gainer that day and does not report Kaynes, Dixon, Amber, Cyient DLM, PG Electroplast or Avalon moving with it. The last sector-wide EMS catalyst on record is 16 July 2026, when the Union Cabinet cleared a combined outlay of roughly ₹1.9 lakh crore for semiconductors and mobile manufacturing and the EMS complex rallied together. That is background, not the 2–8 September trigger.

What this means for the reader: the move is explained, and what explains it is a factory opening. A 20,000 sq ft JV plant is a real asset with real customers ahead of it, but it is not an earnings event, not an order win, and not a guidance change. The price rerated on capability, not on cash.

Management’s own explanation has three parts, and each of them is checkable.

  • Mix. Consumer — the lowest-margin vertical — rose from 26% of revenue in Q4 FY26 to 34% in Q1 FY27, while industrials fell from 31% to 24%. That single swap is the largest identifiable driver. Management guides consumer to 30–32% for the full year, so on their own numbers the mix should partly unwind.
  • Input costs and supply chain. Gross margin fell 140 bps sequentially to 24.5%, which management attributes to component cost inflation and West Asia’s supply-chain disruption. Gross margin is the cleanest read on this because it sits above all operating decisions.
  • Employee cost. ₹75.3 crore in the quarter, up roughly 50% year on year and 25% sequentially — ahead of revenue on both comparisons. Staffing for the new facilities lands in the P&L before the revenue from them does.

The part that deserves scepticism is the guidance bridge. Full-year guidance is 10.5%–11.0% and Q1 came in at 10.2%. That requires the remaining three quarters to average above the guided range simply to hit the middle of it. Management has said the second half is where the defence and industrial schedules land, which is a coherent reason — but it is a back-loaded plan, and back-loaded plans are the ones that get revised.

Margins in the table above are the company’s own rounded figures, so the sequential fall reads as 170 bps. The scan carries the same two quarters unrounded at 11.89% and 10.17%, a fall of 172 bps; ICICI Securities also states 172 bps. The three sources agree — only the rounding differs.

The company reports two margins. Operating EBITDA margin excludes other income and is the figure used throughout this report (10.2% in Q1 FY27). The company also reports a headline EBITDA margin including other income of 11.0%, which is the figure most press coverage quoted as “margin expanded 31 bps.” Both are the company’s own. The wider one flatters the quarter; the narrower one is the one that fell 170 bps sequentially.

FY27 guidanceRevenue growth >35%; EBITDA growth 30–35% (~₹700 Cr+); operating EBITDA margin 10.5–11.0%. Management says it is on track to exceed the revenue and EBITDA growth guidance.

This is the part of the story that justifies a premium multiple, and it should be given its due. Contract electronics manufacturing is structurally a thin-margin, high-turnover business. The route out of that is design ownership — ODM — where the manufacturer owns the product rather than assembling somebody else’s. Syrma’s ODM line has roughly doubled year on year for two consecutive years and now stands at 17% of revenue. Each point of ODM mix is worth more margin than a point of pure assembly.

Exports carry the same logic and the same direction of travel: a quarter of revenue, growing at the same rate as the whole, and described by management as the higher-margin channel.

The counterweight is the order book’s own arithmetic. ₹6,770 crore at a 10–10.5 month average execution period is roughly three quarters of forward revenue at the current run-rate (derived). That is decent visibility for an EMS business but it is not a multi-year backlog, and it grew only 2.6% sequentially against 8.4% sequential revenue growth. Execution is currently drawing the book down faster than order intake is filling it. One quarter does not make a trend, but it is the single number in this section that argues against extrapolation.

What 85× requires — derived arithmetic, not a forecast

A trailing P/E of 85.13 implies an earnings yield of 1.17% (derived). The useful question is not whether that is “too high” but what earnings growth would have to occur for a buyer at today’s multiple to end up holding a normal multiple later. That is pure arithmetic and is set out below.

If the multiple settles at…Earnings must reachRequired CAGR over 3 yrsRequired CAGR over 5 yrs
50×1.70× today’s19.4%11.2%
40×2.13× today’s28.6%16.3%
30×2.84× today’s41.6%23.2%

This is derived arithmetic and is explicitly not a forecast, a target or a valuation. It is the algebra of 85.13 divided by an assumed exit multiple, annualised. It assumes the share price is unchanged, which it will not be; it says nothing about what the multiple will be, only what earnings growth each assumed multiple would require to leave the buyer whole on the multiple alone. No estimate of Syrma’s future earnings is used or implied anywhere in this table.

Two cross-checks on that arithmetic, both derived. First, the trailing PEG of 2.16 against a P/E of 85.13 implies the PEG is built on a growth input of roughly 39.4% — close to the 41.6% that a 30× exit in three years would demand. Second, management guides EBITDA growth of 30–35% for FY27. Compounding the midpoint of that guidance for three years produces earnings roughly 2.3× today’s, which would leave the multiple around 37× at an unchanged price.

So the honest verdict on the multiple is neither “absurd” nor “fine”. If management delivers guidance and keeps delivering it for three years, 85× resolves into something in the high thirties — expensive but not indefensible for a business compounding at that rate with a 20% goodwill-adjusted ROCE. What it does not leave is any room for the guidance to be missed. The margin already came in below the full-year guided range in Q1, the highest-margin vertical fell sequentially, and a ₹1,000 crore QIP has been approved that would raise the denominator. The price requires the plan to work; it does not require it to work spectacularly, but it does require it to work.

For reference and not as a recommendation: ICICI Securities published a Buy on 31 July 2026 with a ₹1,680 target on 52× FY28E earnings, estimating FY27E EPS of ₹21.8 and FY28E EPS of ₹32.3. MOFSL published a Buy with a ₹2,000 target on 8 September 2026. ICICI’s implied multiples of 63.3× FY27E and 42.7× FY28E were struck against a share price of ₹1,378 on 31 July. Forward multiples scale directly with price, so at any higher price they are proportionately higher; the EPS estimates above let the reader recompute at whatever price is current. Scenarios

Bull
The Q1 margin dip proves to be exactly what management called it — a one-quarter mix and input-cost effect. Consumer normalises toward the guided 30–32%, the rear-end-loaded defence and industrial schedules land in H2 FY27, and the full-year operating margin finishes at or above 10.5%. ODM keeps roughly doubling and crosses 20% of revenue, dragging structural margin up with it. The Elemaster JV facility contributes from Q3 FY27 and the PCB plant from FY28 at the guided 15–17% EBITDA margin, giving the business a genuinely different margin profile by FY29. Revenue growth exceeds the 35% guidance. The QIP is priced high and funds the PCB phase two with minimal dilution. Earnings compound fast enough that 85× resolves into the high thirties without the price falling, and the year-long Stage 2 simply keeps going.
Base
Revenue delivers close to the 35% guidance and margin lands near the bottom of the 10.5–11.0% range or slightly below it, because Q1 started 30 bps under the range and the recovery is back-loaded. ODM and exports keep growing but the mix shift is gradual rather than transformative. The PCB plant commissions broadly on time for April 2027 and contributes from FY28 as guided. Earnings grow strongly; the multiple compresses toward the peer group as it does. Returns come from earnings outrunning multiple compression, which at this starting multiple is a narrower path than it looks — and the four-session September move is given back or consolidated through before the trend resumes.
Bear
The sequential margin decline turns out to be the trend rather than the exception: consumer stays the growth engine because that is where the volume is, industrials keeps slipping, and the full-year margin comes in below 10.5%. Working capital keeps absorbing cash — net cash has already fallen from ₹467 Cr to ₹122 Cr in a single quarter — and the ₹1,000 Cr QIP is executed into weakness, diluting rather than funding from strength. The PCB plant slips past April 2027 and carries depreciation before revenue. At 85× trailing and 50× EV/EBITDA there is no valuation floor whatsoever, and a Stage 2 already more than a year old ends the way Stage 2 ends. A rerating that took eight sessions to add 11% to the multiple can be given back faster than that.

Key Risks

01
Valuation — 85× trailing earnings with no floor
85.13× trailing earnings, 11.04× book, 50.4× EV/EBITDA — second-dearest of seven listed EMS names on enterprise value, on a 1.17% earnings yield (derived). The arithmetic in Section 10 shows that even a 40× exit multiple in three years requires earnings to compound at 28.6%. There is no asset backing, no dividend yield and no recovery discount to cushion a disappointment. This is the risk that determines the size of the loss if any of the others materialise.
02
Margin trajectory is pointing the wrong way
Operating EBITDA margin fell 170 bps sequentially to 10.2%, gross margin fell 140 bps to 24.5%, and PAT fell 11.4% quarter on quarter — on revenue up 8.4%. Q1 came in below the full-year guided range of 10.5–11.0%, so the guidance now depends on a back-loaded H2 recovery. Employee cost is growing at 50% year on year against 67% revenue growth and will keep running ahead as the new facilities staff up. In a business whose entire premium rests on operating leverage, a quarter of negative operating leverage is the most informative number in the release.
03
Working capital, the net-cash swing, and the approved ₹1,000 Cr QIP
Net working capital rose to 71 days from 63 in the prior quarter. Working capital loans rose from ₹285.5 Cr to ₹619.3 Cr, total debt from ₹353.1 Cr to ₹686.0 Cr, and net cash fell from ₹467.2 Cr to ₹122.4 Cr in a single quarter — a ₹344.8 Cr swing (derived). Management frames the inventory build as a deliberate strategic buffer against West Asia supply disruption, which is credible, but it is cash out the door either way. Separately, the board approved raising up to ₹1,000 crore via QIP on 29 July 2026; that is real dilution optionality hanging over an 11×-book stock, and its timing and price are not in the shareholder’s control. Receivables are heavy in absolute terms — see the note below this section.
04
Stage 2 tenure and a four-session run
Stage 2 has been running since June 2025 on the provider’s list — past a year, which places it late in the phase rather than early. On top of that the stock added roughly a seventh of its value between 2 and 8 September on a facility inauguration, with the biggest single day on volume around 2.8× the 30-day average (derived). Buying after a vertical move, late in a trend, at an all-time high, is the specific situation in which the trend template’s “PASSES” is least informative.
05
Customer concentration
Top 5 customers ~38% of revenue, top 10 ~51%, top 20 ~66%. That is not extreme for contract manufacturing and management describes it as stable, but it is structural: EMS revenue is programme revenue, and a single large customer re-sourcing a programme removes a visible block of it at short notice. The 18 new customers added in Q1 dilute the risk slowly, not quickly. The consumer vertical — now 34% of revenue and the quarter’s growth engine — is also where customer schedules are most volatile, and management specifically attributed part of Q1 consumer strength to front-loaded schedules, which by definition borrow from later quarters.
06
Management change at the top, on several seats at once
A new Chief Executive, Jaidit Singh Brar, joined on 29 June 2026 — the last day of the reported quarter. Shareholders at the 22nd AGM approved the re-appointment of Sandeep Tandon as Executive Chairman for five years from 1 October 2026, and the appointment of Jayesh Nagindas Doshi as Whole-time Director, reported with a term running from 25 August 2026 to 31 March 2031 (headline coverage describes it as a five-year term; the stated end date is a little short of five years, and the primary BSE filing of 25 August 2026 could not be retrieved from here to resolve it). Continuity at chairman level is genuinely reassuring. But a new CEO taking over a business mid-scale-up, mid-capex-cycle and with a QIP approved is a live execution variable, and none of the quarters discussed in this report reflect his tenure.
07
Execution risk on the capex programme
PCB Phase 1 is ~₹400 Cr of which only ₹100–130 Cr has been spent; equipment arrives from October 2026, power-on is guided for the January–March quarter and commercial production for April 2027 at 40–50% utilisation. Phase 2 is a further ~₹400 Cr, taking total capex to ₹800–900 Cr — roughly 1.2 to 1.4 times a full year of current run-rate operating EBITDA (derived by annualising Q1 FY27’s ₹161.6 Cr) — funded 50–60% by debt with government incentives assumed. This is a first-time backward-integration into bare PCB fabrication — a different manufacturing discipline from assembly, and the guided 15–17% EBITDA margin on it is a plan, not a track record.
08
Surveillance status not independently confirmed
The provider’s Stage 2 list of 28 August shows no ASM, ESM or GSM flag, and this report has found no evidence of one. But the exchanges’ own surveillance lists could not be read from this machine — bseindia.com returned HTTP 403 and no NSE list was retrievable. ASM raises margins and ESM is more restrictive still, and both routinely exclude a name from broker MTF lists. Given the size and speed of the recent move on well-above-average volume, this is worth confirming directly rather than assuming.

On receivables, stated as precisely as the sources allow. The scan carries receivables at 33.69% of sales. Converted to days that is 123 days of sales (derived: 0.3369 × 365). Screener.in, read 8 September 2026, separately reports debtor days of 139 for the year to March 2026. The two are close but not identical and use different sales denominators and dates, so they are shown both ways rather than reconciled. The company’s own Q1 FY27 release discloses net working capital days (71) and the debt and cash schedule but does not break out a trade receivables line, so the absolute balance could not be verified against the primary source and is not stated here. On either measure receivables run to roughly four months of sales, which is normal for contract electronics manufacturing and is the reason working capital consumes cash whenever revenue accelerates.

13 —

Summary

RATING: HOLD AT CMP — REAL GROWTH, LATE-STAGE TENURE, 85× PRICE

The business is genuinely good and the framework genuinely passes it. Neither fact settles the question. Q1 FY27 delivered revenue up 66.7%, operating EBITDA up 68.8% and profit after tax up 111.7%, with ODM revenue roughly doubling to 17% of sales, exports at a quarter of revenue, an order book of ₹6,770 crore and a goodwill-adjusted ROCE of 20.1%. The scan passes it 7/7 on high relative strength at an all-time high. Very little in this library scores that well on both sides at once.

The same quarter contains the argument against paying up for it. Sequentially, operating EBITDA margin fell 170 basis points to 10.2%, gross margin fell 140, and profit after tax fell 11.4% on revenue that rose 8.4%. Industrials — the highest-margin vertical, and the one carrying defence — fell 18% sequentially, while the growth came from consumer, the lowest-margin one. Q1 margin landed below the full-year guided range, so the guidance now leans on a back-loaded second half. Net cash fell from ₹467 crore to ₹122 crore in three months as working capital absorbed it, and a ₹1,000 crore QIP has been approved but not priced.

And the price is not waiting for any of it. 85.13× trailing earnings and 11.04× book, second-dearest of seven listed EMS peers on EV/EBITDA — after a rerating of roughly 11% on both multiples in eight sessions, with no results, no guidance change and no order announcement in between (derived). The trigger is established and is a 20,000 sq ft JV factory that opened on 2 September: a real asset, but not an earnings event. None of the reported numbers contain that facility, the PCB plant, or the H2 defence ramp — the first two cannot appear before Q3 FY27 and FY28 respectively.

Hold at CMP, and the reasoning is the arithmetic rather than a view on the company. Section 10 shows that even a 40× exit multiple in three years needs earnings to compound at 28.6%, and that management’s own guidance, compounded, lands the multiple near 37×. That is a coherent outcome — it is simply one with no margin for error, arriving at the end of a year-long Stage 2, four sessions after a 14% move, into a quarter whose margin already missed. The framework is not wrong about this name. It is answering a question about trend, and the question the price now poses is about price.

What to monitor
