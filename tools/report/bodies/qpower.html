02 —

The Business: Selling Into the Grid Bottleneck

Quality Power makes the equipment that lets high-voltage power actually move through a grid. Reactors manage reactive power. FACTS equipment stabilises transmission. Instrument transformers measure it. None of this is glamorous, and all of it is mandatory.

Why demand is structural rather than cyclical

  • Renewables break grids. Solar and wind are intermittent and geographically remote from demand. Integrating them requires exactly the reactive-power and stabilisation equipment Quality Power builds. Every gigawatt of renewable capacity added creates grid-equipment demand.
  • Data centres are the new large load. One of Q1’s order wins was ₹48.3 crore of high-voltage reactors for a US data-centre project — a demand source that barely existed five years ago.
  • HVDC is where long-distance transmission is going, and it needs specialised components with few qualified suppliers.

What limits it

This is project equipment supply — orders are discrete, lumpy and won competitively against global players. Revenue of ₹256 crore in a quarter makes this a small company selling into very large infrastructure projects, where a single deferral moves a quarter materially.

04 —

The Turkey Adjustment — What Ind AS 29 Actually Is

The ₹7.82 crore gap between adjusted and reported profit is a non-cash net monetary loss arising from applying Ind AS 29 to the company’s Turkish operations.

Ind AS 29 is the hyperinflationary-economies standard. When a subsidiary operates in an economy classified as hyperinflationary — Turkey is — its financial statements must be restated for the change in purchasing power. Holding net monetary assets in a rapidly depreciating currency produces a loss of purchasing power that must be recognised in the profit and loss account, even though no cash has moved.

This is one of the few adjusted-versus-reported gaps that genuinely deserves to be added back for analytical purposes. It reflects accounting for currency debasement, not operational performance. Three qualifications keep that from being a free pass:

  • It is recurring, not one-time. As long as Turkey remains hyperinflationary and Endoks holds net monetary assets, this charge repeats every quarter. It is not an exceptional item.
  • The underlying economic exposure is real. The accounting is non-cash, but the erosion of purchasing power on Turkish-lira assets is genuine.
  • It will recur at unpredictable magnitude, driven by Turkish inflation rather than by anything management controls.

The honest treatment: use the adjusted ₹54.5 crore to judge operating performance, and the reported ₹46.7 crore to judge what accrues to shareholders. Both are correct for their purpose.

07 —

HVDC CTC Magnet Wire — The Value-Chain Move

FacilityHVDC CTC magnet wire
StatusMachinery installation scheduled to commence August 2026

CTC — continuously transposed conductor — magnet wire is a specialised input used in the windings of large transformers and reactors, including HVDC converter transformers. It is a higher-specification, higher-barrier product than the equipment Quality Power currently assembles, and globally there are few qualified suppliers.

Two reasons this matters more than its likely near-term revenue:

  • It moves the company up the value chain from assembling equipment toward making a critical input — historically a route to structurally better margins.
  • It creates an addressable market beyond its own products. Magnet wire can be sold to other transformer and reactor makers, including competitors.

Machinery installation commencing in August 2026 means meaningful revenue is an FY28 event at the earliest. This belongs in the monitoring list, not in current-year numbers.

Summary

RATING: HOLD AT CMP — EXCELLENT BOOK, 20× BOOK VALUE

Quality Power is operationally one of the cleanest stories in this batch. The order book grew 39% in a single quarter to ₹1,945.5 crore — roughly 1.9× annual revenue — while revenue rose 32% and adjusted profit 47%. Q1 order wins spanned a US data centre, a Japanese FACTS project and 400kV domestic equipment, which demonstrates qualification breadth that takes years to build.

It is also the rare case where the gap between adjusted and reported profit has a clean, verifiable explanation: a ₹7.82 crore non-cash Ind AS 29 charge on Turkish operations. Reported PAT of ₹46.7 crore reconciles exactly with the +26% growth in your own screener data — a cross-check that passed.

What holds this at Hold is scale against price. ₹256 crore of quarterly revenue carries an ₹11,077 crore market capitalisation — 20.4× book and 5.69× the order book, against HFCL at 1.44× and Man Industries at 1.58×. The HVDC magnet-wire move is genuinely interesting and is an FY28 story at the earliest. The business deserves a premium; whether it deserves this one is the question the price is asking.
