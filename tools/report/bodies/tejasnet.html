A note on one screener field before it misleads. The scan reports “net profit +4.32% YoY” for Q1 FY27. That is not growth. The company lost ₹193.87 Cr in Q1 FY26 and ₹202.24 Cr in Q1 FY27; the loss grew 4.32%. Section 03 shows why even that is the wrong way round.

The +99.1% is real and it is off a collapsed base. ₹202 Cr in the year-ago quarter was itself down about 87% year-on-year, because FY25’s enormous BSNL delivery cycle had just ended. Doubling from the bottom of an air pocket is arithmetic, not a trend — the more useful comparison is that ₹402 Cr is 21% above the preceding quarter, which is the fourth consecutive sequential gain.

The tax line is the part worth stopping on. Pre-tax, the loss actually improved by ₹26.54 Cr year-on-year. After tax, it worsened by ₹8.37 Cr. Nothing operational caused that reversal: the deferred tax credit shrank from ₹103.48 Cr to ₹68.57 Cr (derived). The screener’s “+4.32%” is therefore a comparison of two losses whose direction has been flipped by a tax entry. The honest read is that Q1 FY27 was a better quarter than Q1 FY26 at every line above tax, and the headline says otherwise.

The counterweight is that a tax credit on accumulated losses is an asset that has to be recovered out of future taxable profit. On a company that has now lost money for five consecutive quarters, that recoverability is an assumption, not a fact, and it is carrying part of the reported bottom line.

04 —

The Margin Path — Improving, and Still Deeply Negative

Operating marginValue

Q4 FY26 (last quarter)−35.53%

The direction is unambiguous and it is the strongest single fact in the bull case. Four thousand two hundred basis points of margin repair in four quarters is a large move, and it comes almost entirely from operating leverage — the same fixed cost base spread over twice the revenue — rather than from pricing.

That mechanism has a clear implication and it is worth stating as arithmetic rather than as hope. At a −24.96% margin on ₹402 Cr, the operating loss is roughly ₹100 Cr per quarter. If the cost base is broadly fixed, revenue would need to rise toward ₹500 Cr a quarter — about a quarter more than Q1 FY27 delivered — for the operating line simply to reach zero (derived, and it assumes no gross-margin change and no cost growth, neither of which is safe). Management’s own guidance on the 28 July 2026 call was “positive EBITDA and EBIT” first, then PAT, with “12 to 18 months a reasonable time to expect”.

What that guidance concedes is the timing. Twelve to eighteen months from July 2026 places EBITDA breakeven somewhere between mid-FY28 and late FY28, and PAT profitability after that. Every quarter until then adds to the loss, and — as Section 06 shows — is funded from a balance sheet that is already carrying ₹4,277 Cr of net debt.

Read that ratio slowly. The company is owed two and a half times everything it has sold in the last twelve months. That expectation is testable, and the quarter in which it will be tested is Q2 FY27 — the quarter ending 30 September 2026, reporting around October. No reported number available today contains it. If net receivables fall in that quarter, the working-capital thesis is intact and this report’s central objection weakens considerably. If they rise again, then the pattern established in Q1 FY27 — revenue growth that converts into receivables rather than cash — is the operating model, and the equity is being asked to fund it.

Two consequences follow, and they are the reason this report cannot rate the stock better than Hold on the accounts alone.

  • The gap between the operating loss and the reported loss is financing. Q1 FY27 lost ₹100 Cr at EBITDA and ₹271 Cr before tax. The ₹171 Cr difference is depreciation and net finance cost (derived). Roughly two thirds of the pre-tax loss sits below the operating line — which means operating improvement alone does not fix the bottom line while the debt stays where it is.
  • Working capital is the whole balance sheet. ₹5,694 Cr of receivables and inventory against ₹2,931 Cr of book equity means the equity is a minority of the working capital, and the rest is borrowed. Inventory has been written down before — about ₹145 Cr in Q2 FY26 — and ₹2,358 Cr of telecom equipment inventory carries genuine obsolescence risk in a market moving from 4G to 5G.

The Q1 FY27 press release gives cash of ₹589 Cr, which reconciles exactly with gross ₹4,866 Cr less net ₹4,277 Cr. Nothing reported to date contains any of it. The ₹402 Cr revenue quarter, the ₹1,529 Cr order book, the ₹2,232 Cr net receivable and the −24.96% margin are all measured at 30 June 2026 — two months before the LOI. The price has moved on the announcement; the accounts have not yet been asked to.

Second, whether it is new business

In May 2025, Tejas disclosed an add-on Advance Purchase Order routed through TCS under the existing master contract, worth ₹1,526 Cr, for 18,685 BSNL 4G sites. Through FY26 the company attributed its revenue collapse to delayed BSNL purchase orders, and the Q3 FY26 shortfall was tied to that ₹1,526 Cr specifically. The order was never carried in the reported order book — the Q1 FY26 book of ₹1,241 Cr was explicitly stated as excluding it.

The August 2026 LOI covers the same 18,685 sites at ₹1,537 Cr. On the evidence available, the most reasonable reading is that this is the formal conversion of the May 2025 add-on after roughly fifteen months of delay, not a second, incremental award. The company has not stated the relationship either way in the disclosures reviewed here, and this report does not assert it as established.

That reading does not make the LOI unimportant — quite the opposite. An order that could not be executed for fifteen months, now formalised, is precisely the thing that unlocks the revenue ramp the margin path needs, and at ₹1,537 Cr it is larger than the entire order book reported at 30 June. But it is a different fact from “the order book has doubled on new demand”, and the difference matters to anyone extrapolating.

Also pending and not yet awarded: on the 28 July 2026 call management described a further BSNL expansion order covering roughly 26,000 sites as “in the final stages of conclusion”. Separately, BSNL’s proposed ₹77,000 Cr five-year capital programme covering about 2 lakh additional 4G sites was reported on 11 August 2026 — that is BSNL’s own proposed capex and explicitly not an order awarded to Tejas.

09 —

BSNL — The Customer, the Concentration and the Cycle

Financial yearRevenuePATWhat was happening
FY24₹2,471 Cr₹63 CrPre-ramp
FY25₹8,923 Cr₹447 CrThe ₹7,492 Cr BSNL 4G/5G delivery cycle
FY26₹1,103 Cr−₹909 CrThe air pocket after it

That is the shape of the business, and no discussion of Tejas is honest without it. Revenue fell 88% in a single year — not because the company lost the customer or the technology failed, but because it finished the job. The ₹7,492 Cr pan-India order for roughly 100,000 4G/5G sites was completed in May 2025, BSNL launched commercially on Tejas RAN with about 97,500 towers deployed, and then there was nothing of comparable size to deliver.

This is a genuine engineering achievement and a genuinely dangerous revenue profile at the same time. Building indigenous 4G RAN at national scale is something very few companies anywhere have done, and it created a qualified position that is hard to displace. But the order book is 93% domestic, and the domestic story is overwhelmingly one customer running a state capital programme on a political and budgetary timetable that Tejas does not control. FY26 is what that dependency costs when the timetable slips.

The diversification that is actually happening

  • Revenue mix in Q1 FY27 was roughly evenly split between India and international — the first quarter in which that has been true, driven by international 5G radio shipments.
  • First commercial win for an end-to-end 5G network deployment in South America, and a first significant 5G customer win in Europe.
  • A partnership with NEC for 5G massive-MIMO radios, described on the call as a preferred-partner relationship.
  • Domestically, beyond BSNL: GPON OLT supplies to Indian telcos, 17,000 IP/MPLS routers delivered for BharatNet Phase III (completed 30 March 2026), and a power-utility modernisation vendor selection.

The tension is that the revenue mix has diversified while the order book has not — 93% India at 30 June, and the largest single new commitment since then is another BSNL order. International is 7% of the book and roughly half of one quarter’s revenue, which means the shipments are being converted almost as fast as they are won. That is good for cash and bad for visibility.

Scenarios

Bull
The ₹1,537 Cr LOI converts to a firm purchase order within Q2 FY27 and the ~26,000-site BSNL expansion is awarded behind it, taking the order book past ₹3,000 Cr. Net receivables fall in the September quarter as the BSNL acceptance tests close, so revenue starts converting to cash rather than to debtors, and net debt stops rising. Revenue climbs toward ₹500 Cr a quarter, EBITDA crosses zero inside the guided 12–18 months, and international 5G — already half of Q1 FY27 revenue — grows into a second leg that reduces the 93% domestic concentration. In that world FY25-type throughput returns, EV/sales collapses through revenue rather than through price, and RS clears 70 as the leadership the framework is currently withholding arrives.
Base
The LOI converts and executes, but on BSNL’s timetable rather than the company’s — meaningful revenue lands from Q3 FY27 and runs into FY28. Margin keeps improving on operating leverage and EBITDA breakeven arrives near the far end of the guided window, but PAT stays negative because roughly two thirds of the pre-tax loss sits below the operating line in depreciation and interest on ₹4,866 Cr of gross borrowings. Receivables come down slowly and unevenly, net debt plateaus rather than falls. The stock has no earnings anchor to re-rate against, so it trades on order-flow headlines: sharp moves on announcements, long flat stretches between them. RS oscillates around the threshold and the framework keeps saying not yet.
Bear
The LOI takes months to become a purchase order — as the May 2025 add-on did — and execution slips again. Net receivables rise for a second consecutive quarter, net debt pushes past ₹4,500 Cr, and funding the working capital requires equity: at 3.58× book with reserves already a quarter lower than a year ago, a raise would be dilutive and would land while the stock is well off its high. Some part of the ₹2,358 Cr inventory is written down again, as ₹145 Cr was in Q2 FY26. The deferred tax asset supporting the reported loss becomes harder to justify and is trimmed. On 11.3× EV/sales with no earnings, no P/E floor and a failing trend template, there is nothing structural holding the price up if the order flow pauses.

If the August 2026 LOI is that order formalised rather than an additional one, the incremental demand is smaller than the headline implies. The relationship has not been stated by the company in the disclosures reviewed.
04
Single-customer, single-programme concentration
The order book is 93% domestic and the domestic business is dominated by BSNL. FY25 revenue of ₹8,923 Cr and FY26 revenue of ₹1,103 Cr are the same customer relationship in two different years. Nothing about that lumpiness has changed.
05
Inventory of ₹2,358 Cr and obsolescence
Held against orders that have repeatedly slipped, in a market transitioning from 4G to 5G. Roughly ₹145 Cr of inventory write-downs and ₹44 Cr of warranty provisions went through Q2 FY26 alone. Inventory days of 1,160 leave a great deal of time for equipment to become obsolete.
06
It fails your own trend template
RS 67 against a 70 threshold, six of seven checks passing. It failed intraday at 69 and closed lower, so the reading moved away from the line. The provider’s Stage 2 classification is “Reentry” — a returning trend, not an established one.
07
The reported loss depends on a deferred tax credit
A ₹68.57 Cr credit stands between the ₹270.81 Cr pre-tax loss and the ₹202.24 Cr reported loss (derived). That asset has to be recovered out of future taxable profit at a company that has lost money for five consecutive quarters. A change in that judgement reduces reported profit without any operating cause.
08
Surveillance — clear today, flagged twice this year
No ASM, ESM or GSM measure is active as of 8 September 2026, verified independently of the provider list. But TEJASNET went onto BSE Short-Term ASM Stage I twice in 2026 — 5–30 March and 8–18 May. Given the volatility that repeatedly triggers it, a further ASM spell would raise margin requirements and is a live constraint, not a historical footnote. ESM is structurally inapplicable at this market capitalisation.
09
No earnings anchor to the valuation
About 11.3× EV/sales and 3.58× book with ROE at −26.8% and no P/E. Nothing in the accounts sets a floor. The price is supported by expectations of order flow, and it reprices when order flow news arrives or fails to.
10
Dilution
Reserves fell from ₹3,667 Cr to ₹2,750 Cr over FY26. If working capital keeps absorbing cash and lenders reach their limit, equity is the remaining source. No raise has been announced; the only 2026 issuance found is routine employee stock options.
13 —

Summary

RATING: HOLD — STAGE 2 PRICE, STAGE 4 ACCOUNTS

Tejas Networks has an appealing turnaround narrative and it is largely true. BSNL’s pan-India indigenous 4G network was built and launched on Tejas equipment across roughly 97,500 towers. Revenue has risen for four consecutive quarters and nearly doubled year-on-year. The operating margin has repaired by more than 4,200 basis points in a year. International 5G has produced first commercial wins in Europe and South America and now accounts for about half of quarterly revenue. On 27 August a ₹1,537 crore Letter of Intent arrived that is larger than the entire order book reported at 30 June. The promoter is Tata, at 53.33% with nothing pledged.

And the accounts say something harder. The company lost ₹909 crore in FY26 and has lost money in each of the last five quarters. There is no P/E. The operating margin is still −24.96%. Net debt rose ₹746 crore in a single quarter to ₹4,277 crore, about 1.46× book equity. Most damning, trade receivables of ₹3,256 crore stand at roughly two and a half times trailing annual revenue, with a 2,010-day cash conversion cycle — and on management’s own net measure, receivables went up by ₹327 crore in the June quarter on ₹402 crore of revenue. The business is currently converting growth into debtors and funding the gap with borrowing.

The framework agrees, narrowly and specifically. Six of seven checks pass; the failure is relative strength at 67 against a threshold of 70, and it failed away from the line rather than toward it. The price structure is intact — this is not MTAR, where the chart was broken. What is missing is leadership, and the provider’s own classification of “Reentry” says the same thing in different words.

The timing rule settles the rating. The LOI is dated 27 August; the last reported quarter closed 30 June. No reported number contains the order, the revenue from it, or the BSNL collections management said would clear “during the quarter”. All three are Q2 FY27 or later events, reporting around October. The price has already moved on the announcement; the accounts have not yet been asked to confirm it. A loss-making company with receivables at 2.5× sales, ₹4,277 crore of net debt and a failing trend template does not earn better than Hold on an order that has not yet become a purchase order — particularly when the same 18,685 sites were announced fifteen months earlier and did not execute.

Equally, it does not earn worse. The order is real, the margin repair is real, the promoter is solid, no surveillance measure is active, and one clean collection quarter would remove this report’s central objection. Hold is not indecision here. It is a statement that the case rests entirely on a quarter that has not been reported yet, and that the framework — for one specific, checkable reason — is still saying wait.
