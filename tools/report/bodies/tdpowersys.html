02 —

The Business

TD Power makes the generator that sits at the end of a turbine. Wherever something spins — a steam turbine in a biomass plant, a gas turbine, a hydro unit, a large engine — a generator converts that rotation into electricity. The company sells these to turbine OEMs and EPC contractors rather than to end users, which is an important structural fact: it is a component supplier inside someone else's project, not a project company itself.

That positioning has two consequences worth holding onto. It keeps the balance sheet lighter than a project business, because TD Power does not carry execution risk on the whole plant. And it caps pricing power, because the customer is a sophisticated industrial buyer who can qualify alternatives.

The demand backdrop is genuinely strong and genuinely global: power generation capacity is being added worldwide, data-centre load is driving new generation, and the replacement cycle in developed markets is active. TD Power is a direct beneficiary of that without needing any India-specific policy to work.

That is a different signature from Happy Forgings (margin expanding) or IOL Chemicals (margin expanding sharply). Future earnings growth must come from more revenue, not better revenue.

This is the weakest order book in the batch, and the number deserves plain treatment. ₹2,208 crore against a raised FY27 revenue guidance of ₹2,600 crore is 0.85× a single year of sales — less than twelve months of coverage. HFCL carries five years. Jyoti CNC carries 2.3 years. Happy Forgings carries 0.6 years but operates on schedules rather than orders.

TD Power does book discrete orders, so the comparison is fair, and the conclusion is that the company must keep winning orders continuously to hit its own guidance. Management expects full-year order inflow above ₹2,800 crore, which is more than the entire current book. The business is running hand-to-mouth relative to its ambitions — profitable and growing, but with limited buffer if inflow slows for even a quarter or two.

05 —

Exports — The Concentration Nobody Highlights

Exports accounted for 93% of Q1 FY27 order inflow, at ₹683.60 crore, up 165.6% year on year. Management specifically credits the United States, where products ship directly from the factory to customers.

Take that number seriously. A company whose new order inflow is 93% export-dependent is not an Indian capital-goods play that happens to export; it is an export manufacturer that happens to be listed in India. Every element of the thesis — growth rate, margin, order inflow — now depends on demand conditions outside India, and specifically on the US.

Three exposures follow directly, and none of them is hedged by anything in the Indian order book:

  • Trade policy. A tariff, a duty change, or a procurement-preference rule in the destination market hits 93% of new orders, not 9%.
  • Currency. Rupee strength compresses realisations on the bulk of the book.
  • Foreign demand cycles. A US capex slowdown reaches TD Power directly and without an Indian buffer.

This is a real and material concentration, and it is the risk least visible in the headline numbers.

06 —

The Railway Business

Railways contribute ₹211 crore of the order book — 9.6% of the total. It is a genuine second leg and it is domestic, which partially offsets the export concentration described above.

At under a tenth of the book, however, it is not yet large enough to change the risk profile of the company. Treat railways as a diversification in progress rather than a diversification achieved. If it scales toward 20–25% of the book over the next two years, the export-concentration argument weakens materially and the business becomes structurally better balanced.

The >100 MW Question

Management has stated that large generator opportunities above 100 MW are close to being signed, with an announcement expected in August 2026.

This matters more than its likely order value. TD Power has historically operated in the mid-size generator range; moving above 100 MW puts it into a segment with fewer qualified global competitors, larger unit economics and higher barriers. A confirmed >100 MW win would be a genuine capability signal, not just an order.

The discipline to apply: this is currently an expectation of an announcement, not an announcement. It belongs in the monitoring list, not in the valuation.

Summary

RATING: HOLD — STRONG EXECUTION, EXTREME BOOK MULTIPLE

TD Power delivered one of the cleanest quarters in your portfolio: revenue, EBITDA and PAT all up ~72%, with margins held flat and no accounting complexity. Management raised guidance. The end-market — global power generation — is in a genuine multi-year expansion that requires no Indian policy support to continue.

Three facts sit against that. The order book is the thinnest here at under twelve months of coverage, so the company must keep out-winning its own backlog. 93% of new order inflow is export-driven, concentrating the entire growth thesis outside India and largely in the US. And at 21.8× book it carries double the asset multiple of anything else you own.

It is also your largest position at roughly ₹3.01 lakh, which makes the combination of those three facts worth conscious attention rather than passive acceptance. The business is performing; the question the price asks is whether order inflow can keep replacing a book that only covers ten months.
