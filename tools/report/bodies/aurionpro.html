05 —

The Deceleration — From 20% to 6%

Management attributed the quarter to four causes: MEA disruptions, project timing shifts, supply chain pressures, and capacity allocated to the AI-native stack build-out.

Those are four separate explanations for one weak quarter, and they warrant different treatment:

  • MEA disruptions — genuinely external, and Middle East exposure is a real feature of the business. Plausible and largely outside management’s control.
  • Project timing shifts — normal in enterprise software with lumpy implementation revenue. Recoverable in later quarters.
  • Supply chain pressures — relevant to the TIG hardware side (fare collection, data centres), less so to software.
  • Capacity allocated to the AI-native stack — this one is a choice, not a headwind, and it is the most interesting of the four. See Section 08.

When a company offers four reasons for a miss, the honest reading is that no single one is decisive. What is decisive is the pattern: FY26 grew 20.3%, this quarter grew 6.3%, and margins fell 300 basis points at the same time. Growth decelerating and margin compressing together is a harder combination to explain away than either alone.

Both engines slowed together, which removes the most comfortable explanation. If one segment had stalled while the other grew, the story would be a fixable problem in one division. Banking & Fintech at +5% and TIG at +8.4% means the deceleration is company-wide.

Banking software should be the more defensive of the two — core banking and transaction banking systems are deeply embedded and hard to displace. Growing at 5% suggests a new-license problem rather than a retention one.

This is the part of the quarter that argues the franchise is intact. A $33 million-plus US order — roughly ₹290 crore, or 80% of a full quarter’s revenue — from a digital payments platform is a substantial win in the company’s hardest market. Twenty-three new logos in a quarter is not the profile of a business losing competitiveness.

The tension this creates is the whole investment question: the order book is growing and new customers are being added, while revenue growth has collapsed to 6.3% and margins are falling. Bookings and revenue have decoupled, which in enterprise software usually means implementation capacity — not demand — is the constraint. That points back at Section 08.

08 —

The AI-Native Stack — Investment or Distraction

Management explicitly cited capacity allocated to the AI-native stack build-out as a cause of the weak quarter. Aurionpro owns Arya.ai, its enterprise AI platform.

Diverting engineering capacity from revenue-generating implementation to internal platform development is a deliberate trade: near-term revenue for a future product position. It can be exactly the right decision — and it can also be how a services-inflected business quietly stops growing.

The discipline is to insist on a timeline and a measurable outcome. Two questions decide whether this reads as investment or drift:

  • How many quarters is the capacity reallocation expected to last?
  • What AI-native revenue does it produce, disclosed separately?

Neither is established in the sources reviewed. Until they are, a shareholder is funding an unquantified internal project out of foregone growth, and that is the honest description of what the ₹1,950 crore order book is not currently converting into.

The balance sheet is the strongest part of this story and the returns trend is the weakest. Essentially no net debt and a current ratio near 3.0 mean there is no financial distress here — the company can fund the AI build-out and wait out a slow patch.

ROE falling from ~23% in FY23 to ~13% is the number that matters more. That is a halving of the return on shareholders’ capital over three years, and with Q1 margins down a further 300 basis points, the trend has not turned. A business earning 13% on equity is worth a materially lower multiple than one earning 23%, and that re-rating is precisely what the −48% share price reflects.

On every conventional measure Aurionpro is cheap. 18.5× earnings and 2.26× book for a debt-free enterprise software company with a ₹1,950 crore order book, ~20% historical EBITDA margins and 30+ country reach is not a demanding price.

The discipline is to ask why. A stock is rarely 48% below its high and at 2.26× book by accident. The market is pricing a specific view: that growth has structurally slowed from 20% to single digits, that margins are compressing, and that ROE halving from 23% to 13% is a trend rather than a dip.

If that view is wrong — if the AI build-out completes, implementation capacity frees up, and the ₹1,950 crore book converts — then 18.5× is a genuine opportunity. If it is right, 18.5× on declining earnings is not cheap at all, because the denominator keeps shrinking. Q1 gave no evidence either way except that the deceleration is continuing.

Summary

RATING: HOLD — CHEAP FOR A REASON THAT IS STILL UNFOLDING

Aurionpro trades at 18.5× earnings and 2.26× book — the lowest book multiple in this import batch — for a debt-free enterprise technology company with a ₹1,950 crore order book, a record 23 new customer logos, and the largest US order in its history at $33 million-plus. On a screen it looks like value.

Q1 explains the price. Revenue grew 6.3% against 20.3% for FY26, EBITDA fell 9.8%, profit fell 12.6%, and margin dropped 300 basis points. Both engines slowed together. ROE has halved from ~23% in FY23 to ~13%. The stock is 48% below its 52-week high — the deepest drawdown in this library — carries RS 10, and is still falling.

The most interesting disclosure is that management cites capacity diverted to an AI-native stack build-out as one cause. That is a choice rather than a headwind, and it could be the right one — but with no stated timeline and no separately disclosed AI revenue, shareholders are funding it out of foregone growth. Cheapness has not been a floor here for over a year, and nothing in Q1 marks the turn. The AI build-out completing, with capacity returning to converting that ₹1,950 crore book, is the specific event that would change this.
